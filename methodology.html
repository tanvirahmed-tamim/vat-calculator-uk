Method

Methodology

Last updated 15 November 2026

Every calculator on this site uses a stated method. This page sets out the arithmetic, the rounding, where each figure comes from and what the tools deliberately leave out.

The arithmetic

Three formulas cover almost everything:

  • Add VAT: gross = net × (1 + rate ÷ 100)
  • Remove VAT: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100), then VAT = gross − net
  • VAT fraction: fraction = rate ÷ (100 + rate), so VAT = gross × fraction

At 20% the fraction is 1/6, at 5% it is 1/21 and at 17.5% it is 7/47. Removing VAT is a division, never a subtraction of the rate: taking 20% off £120 gives £96, not the correct net of £100.

Rounding

Amounts are held in pence as integers and rounded to the nearest penny, with half a penny rounding up. The main calculator rounds once, at the final step, so the net, VAT and gross always add up exactly.

The bulk calculator rounds each line before adding the totals, because that is what an invoice shows when every line carries its own VAT amount. A spreadsheet that sums first and rounds once can land a penny or two away. Neither approach is a mistake, but a VAT return should agree with the invoices actually issued.

HMRC accepts normal rounding to the nearest penny and does not require a particular per-line method. The important thing is consistency across a return period.

Historical rates are handled explicitly. The rate history table records each date a standard rate applied, so an old invoice can be checked at the rate in force at its tax point rather than at today's rate.

Scheme calculations

The Flat Rate Scheme tool multiplies the selected percentage by VAT-inclusive turnover, applies the 1 percentage point first-year reduction, and switches to 16.5% when the limited cost business box is ticked. The sector percentages are the current HMRC table, including the catering, hotel and pub rates that returned on 1 April 2022.

The margin scheme tool applies VAT to the margin, not the selling price, and uses the margin VAT fraction. The reverse charge tool shows the deemed VAT on a domestic reverse charge supply and the net amount payable. Neither tool decides whether a supply is eligible; that is a question about the goods, the customer and the invoice.

What the tools do not do

  • They do not decide the VAT liability of a product or service. Liability depends on the supply, not on arithmetic.
  • They do not apply partial exemption, capital goods scheme adjustments or business/non-business apportionment.
  • They do not prepare a return, post to a ledger or file anything with HMRC.
  • They do not store your figures. Calculations run in the browser and nothing is uploaded.

Source verification

Every source link is recorded in a central registry with the date it was last checked. The build resolves each link and the test run fails if a page's visible sources and its structured citations disagree. Rates, thresholds and the rate history are covered by automated browser tests on the calculator itself.

Dates and versions

Each page shows the date its facts were last reviewed. Changes to a method, a formula or a rounding rule are recorded in the corrections log, along with any factual correction that changes what a reader should do.
