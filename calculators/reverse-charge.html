What the domestic reverse charge is

The domestic reverse charge moves the duty to account for VAT from the supplier to the customer. It applies mainly to certain construction and building services supplied between VAT-registered businesses that are registered for the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS).

The supplier issues an invoice without VAT and states that the domestic reverse charge applies. The customer calculates the deemed VAT, includes it as output tax on its VAT return, and usually reclaims the same amount as input tax.

When it applies

  • The supply is a specified construction or building service.
  • The customer is VAT-registered and registered for CIS.
  • The customer is not the final consumer or an intermediary connected to the end user.
  • The supplier and customer are both in the UK for the purposes of the supply.

The rules are specific about which services and which customers are covered. If the conditions are not met, normal VAT accounting applies and the supplier charges VAT.

How the calculation works

The deemed VAT is calculated on the net invoice value at the rate that would otherwise apply:

Deemed VAT = net amount × rate ÷ 100

On a £1,000 net invoice at 20%, the deemed VAT is £200. The supplier is paid £1,000, not £1,200. The customer records £200 of output tax and, where full recovery applies, £200 of input tax.

The net cash effect is usually nil, but both entries are still required. If the customer cannot recover the VAT in full, the difference becomes a real cost.

Invoice wording

The supplier's invoice should make the position clear. A common form of words is:

Reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies. Customer to account for VAT.

The invoice shows the net amount and no VAT. Some software adds a note automatically. Check that the note is present before you send the invoice, because the customer needs it to account for the VAT correctly.

Overseas services are different

The domestic reverse charge is not the same as the reverse charge for services bought from overseas suppliers. Cross-border services follow the place of supply rules, and a UK business may need to account for VAT on services received from abroad. This calculator is aimed at the domestic construction charge.

Questions

Does the supplier charge VAT under the domestic reverse charge?
No. The supplier invoices the net amount without VAT and states that the domestic reverse charge applies. The customer accounts for the deemed VAT on its own VAT return.
Is the reverse charge cash neutral?
For a fully taxable business that can recover all the input tax, the output tax and input tax entries offset each other, so the net cash effect is usually nil. Businesses with partial exemption may not recover it all.
Which rate do I use?
Use the rate that would have applied if the supplier had charged VAT, normally 20% for standard-rated construction services. Reduced or zero rates apply only if the underlying supply qualifies.
Does the customer pay the VAT to the supplier?
No. The customer pays only the net invoice amount to the supplier. The VAT is accounted for through the customer's VAT return, not paid across the invoice.
