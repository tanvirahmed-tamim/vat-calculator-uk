What a VAT fraction is

A VAT fraction converts a VAT-inclusive amount into the VAT it contains. It is not the same as the VAT rate. At 20%, the VAT fraction is 1/6: one sixth of the gross price is VAT. At 5%, it is 1/21.

VAT fraction = rate ÷ (100 + rate)

At 20%: 20 ÷ 120 = 1/6. At 5%: 5 ÷ 105 = 1/21. At 17.5%: 17.5 ÷ 117.5 = 7/47.

Why you cannot just subtract 20%

Subtracting 20% from a gross price removes a fifth of the gross, but VAT is only a sixth of the gross at the standard rate. On £120, subtracting 20% gives £96, while the correct net is £100 and the VAT is £20.

The fraction method avoids that error because it starts from the relationship between net, VAT and gross: gross = net × 1.20, so VAT = gross × (0.20 ÷ 1.20).

When to use it

  • You have a receipt showing only a VAT-inclusive total.
  • You need the input tax on a purchase but the invoice does not split it out.
  • You are checking a quoted inclusive price against a net price.
  • You are entering gross takings into software that expects a net figure.

Rounding

The calculator rounds the VAT to the nearest penny and takes the net as the remainder, so the two always add back to the gross. On a gross of £10.00 at 20%, VAT is £1.67 and net is £8.33.

Questions

Is the VAT fraction the same as the VAT rate?
No. The rate is a percentage of the net amount. The fraction is the VAT share of the gross amount. At 20% the rate is 20% but the fraction of the gross is 1/6.
What is the VAT fraction at 5%?
At 5% the fraction is 5 ÷ 105 = 1/21 of the gross amount. On £105 gross, the VAT is £5 and the net is £100.
Does this work for margin scheme invoices?
No. A margin scheme invoice normally does not show VAT separately, and VAT is calculated on the dealer's margin rather than the full selling price. Use the margin scheme calculator instead.
Which rate should I enter?
Enter the rate that applied at the tax point. Most goods and services are 20%, domestic energy and some other supplies are 5%, and zero-rated supplies contain no VAT.
