What the £90,000 threshold measures

The registration threshold is a test of taxable turnover, not profit and not cash in the bank. It is the VAT-exclusive value of the supplies you make that are not exempt and not outside the scope of VAT. Zero-rated sales count in full even though no VAT is charged on them.

Because the test uses VAT-exclusive values, a business that has already registered should divide its VAT-inclusive takings by 1.2 at the standard rate before comparing the figure with the threshold.

The two tests HMRC applies

There are two separate triggers, and either one creates an obligation to register.

Backward look: the last 12 months

If your taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, you must register within 30 days of the end of the month in which you crossed the line. Your effective date of registration is the first day of the second month after that month.

Crossed in July → register by 30 August → registered from 1 September

Forward look: the next 30 days

If you expect your taxable turnover to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days, you must register by the end of that 30-day period. The effective date is the date you realised the threshold would be exceeded, not the date the money arrives.

The forward look catches businesses that sign a large contract. A single £100,000 order in May can make you liable from the day you accept it, even before you invoice.

When you go over the threshold

  • Register online through your HMRC business tax account.
  • Charge VAT from your effective date of registration, not from the date HMRC replies.
  • Account for VAT on sales made after the effective date, even if you have not yet received the VAT number.
  • Late registration means paying VAT on sales since the date you should have registered, and possibly a penalty.

If the breach is temporary, you can ask HMRC for a registration exception. HMRC decides case by case and writes to confirm the outcome. Separately, businesses that make mostly zero-rated supplies can apply for an exemption from registration.

Deregistration uses £88,000

The threshold for leaving the register is lower than the threshold for joining. You can ask to deregister if your taxable turnover for the next 12 months is expected to fall below £88,000. Deregistration is not automatic, and you must account for VAT on stock and assets you keep when you leave, subject to the normal rules.

Four common mistakes

  • Including exempt income. Rental income from a VAT-exempt letting does not count towards the threshold, even though it counts as turnover in your accounts.
  • Using the tax year. The test is a rolling 12 months, not 6 April to 5 April.
  • Ignoring the forward look. A signed contract can trigger registration before any invoice is raised.
  • Forgetting reverse-charged services. Services bought from overseas suppliers count in your taxable turnover.

Voluntary registration

You can register below £90,000. That is useful if you buy a lot from VAT-registered suppliers and want to reclaim input tax, or if your customers are VAT-registered businesses that expect a VAT invoice. It also means charging VAT to customers who may not be able to reclaim it, so the commercial effect needs checking first.

Questions

Is the VAT threshold based on profit or turnover?
Turnover. The test is the VAT-exclusive value of your taxable sales over a rolling 12 months. Profit, cash received and the value of your assets are all irrelevant to the registration test.
Do zero-rated sales count towards the £90,000 threshold?
Yes. Zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies at 0%, so they count in full. Only exempt supplies and supplies outside the scope of VAT are left out.
Do exempt sales count towards the VAT threshold?
No. Exempt supplies are outside the scope of the turnover test, which is why a business can have a large exempt turnover and no obligation to register.
What happens if I go over the threshold temporarily?
You can apply to HMRC for a registration exception. HMRC considers the evidence and either grants the exception or registers you. The application does not suspend the duty to register while it is being decided.
Does the threshold change every April?
It can change at a Budget, but it is not indexed automatically. The registration threshold has been £90,000 and the deregistration threshold £88,000 since 1 April 2024. Check GOV.UK before relying on either figure.
