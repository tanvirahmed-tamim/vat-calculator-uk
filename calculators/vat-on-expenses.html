Input tax in one paragraph

Input tax is the VAT a VAT-registered business pays on purchases for the business. It is offset against output tax on the VAT return. To claim it, the cost normally needs to be for business use, supported by a valid VAT invoice, and linked to taxable supplies.

Extracting the VAT

Receipts usually show a VAT-inclusive total. To find the VAT, use the VAT fraction rather than subtracting the rate:

VAT = gross × rate ÷ (100 + rate)

At 20%, a £120 gross expense contains £20 of VAT and £100 of net cost. Subtracting 20% from £120 would give £96, which is wrong.

Mixed business and private use

If an expense is partly private, only the business proportion of the VAT is normally recoverable. A mobile phone used 60% for work allows 60% of the VAT to be claimed, if the split is reasonable and supported by records.

For cars, separate rules apply. VAT on most cars available for private use is blocked, and reclaiming fuel VAT usually requires a fuel scale charge.

Blocked expenses

  • Business entertainment of UK clients.
  • Most cars available for private use.
  • Goods and services used only for exempt supplies.
  • Private spending that is not a business cost at all.
  • Business gifts above the £50 threshold, which can create an output tax liability.

This calculator does not test eligibility. It only does the arithmetic once you have decided the expense is claimable.

Records

Keep the VAT invoice or receipt, note the business purpose, and record how any apportionment was calculated. If you use Making Tax Digital software, check that it records the reclaimable proportion rather than the full VAT.

For the rules behind the claim, read reclaiming VAT on business expenses. For the reverse calculation on a sale, use the VAT fraction calculator.

Questions

Can I reclaim all the VAT on a business expense?
Usually yes if the expense is entirely for business use, you hold a valid VAT invoice and it relates to taxable supplies. Mixed-use costs and blocked categories reduce or remove the claim.
How do I work out the VAT on a gross receipt?
Multiply the gross amount by the VAT fraction. At 20% the fraction is 1/6, so a £120 gross expense contains £20 of VAT. At 5% the fraction is 1/21.
Can I reclaim VAT on client entertaining?
Generally no for UK clients. Business entertainment is a blocked category. Some overseas entertaining can be treated differently, so check the specific rules before claiming.
What if I use something partly privately?
Reclaim only the business proportion of the VAT, and keep a reasonable record of how you worked out the split. For cars and fuel, separate rules and scale charges apply.
