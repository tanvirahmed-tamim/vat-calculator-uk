The maths

How the flat rate is charged

Under the Flat Rate Scheme you do not work out VAT invoice by invoice. You multiply a single percentage by your VAT-inclusive turnover for the period and pay that amount to HMRC. The percentage depends on your business type, with adjustments for your first year and for limited cost businesses.

VAT payable = VAT-inclusive turnover × flat rate

A photographer billing £1,000 plus £200 VAT has VAT-inclusive turnover of £1,200. At the 11% photography rate the payment is £132, not £220. Whether that is better or worse than normal VAT accounting depends on how much input VAT the business would otherwise reclaim.

Who can join

  • You are VAT-registered, or registering.
  • You expect VAT taxable turnover of £150,000 or less, excluding VAT, in the next 12 months.
  • You have not left the scheme in the last 12 months.
  • You have not committed a VAT offence in the last 12 months.
  • You have not joined, or been eligible to join, a VAT group in the last 24 months.
  • You are not closely associated with another business, and you have not joined a margin or capital goods scheme.

The scheme cannot be combined with the Cash Accounting Scheme, and a business that goes over £150,000 must leave. That limit is on VAT-exclusive turnover, unlike the calculation itself, which uses VAT-inclusive turnover.

The limited cost business test

If you spend very little on goods, you pay 16.5% instead of your sector rate. You are a limited cost business if your goods cost less than either 2% of your turnover or £1,000 a year. Services do not count as goods for this test, and neither do most food or travel costs.

The test is applied to each accounting period, so a business can move in and out of the 16.5% rate. That is worth checking before treating a single estimate as the whole year.

The first-year discount

In your first year as a VAT-registered business you take 1 percentage point off the flat rate, including the 16.5% limited cost rate. The reduction applies to the first year of registration, not the first year of trading.

What you give up

You normally cannot reclaim VAT on purchases under the scheme. The main exception is single capital assets of £2,000 or more, including VAT. For a business with heavy costs, the flat rate often costs more than normal VAT accounting, even though the paperwork is simpler.

Why older figures do not match

Catering, hotel and pub rates were cut temporarily during the pandemic. The reduced percentages applied from 15 July 2020 and stepped back up until 1 April 2022, when the current rates returned. If you are checking an old return, use the rate that applied in that period rather than today's percentage.

Leaving the scheme

You can leave voluntarily, and you must leave if you stop being eligible, for example by going over £150,000. On leaving you account for VAT on stock and assets on hand under the normal rules, so the exit cost is worth planning before you join.

Questions

Is the flat rate applied to net or gross sales?
Gross. The flat rate is multiplied by VAT-inclusive turnover, which is your sales plus the VAT you charged on them.
What is the limited cost business rate?
16.5%. You are a limited cost business if you spend less than 2% of turnover, or less than £1,000 a year, on goods. The first-year 1% reduction still applies on top.
Can I reclaim VAT on purchases under the Flat Rate Scheme?
Usually not. The exception is single capital assets of £2,000 or more including VAT. Ordinary purchases and services are covered by the flat rate itself.
What is the turnover limit for the scheme?
£150,000 or less of VAT-exclusive taxable turnover in the next 12 months. You must leave the scheme if you go over it.
Does the calculator include the COVID-19 temporary rates?
No. It uses the rates that apply from 1 April 2022. The temporary catering, hotel and pub rates are only relevant when checking an old return.
