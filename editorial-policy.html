Editorial policy

Last reviewed 19 November 2026

This page explains who is accountable for the content, which sources are allowed, how figures are checked and what happens when something is wrong.

Scope of the site

VAT in UK covers UK value added tax only. That includes the rates, registration, returns, schemes, invoicing, place of supply and the way VAT interacts with the four nations. It does not cover income tax, corporation tax, payroll, customs duty or business rates except where a reader needs to know that those taxes are separate from VAT.

Every page has to answer a question a reader could act on. Pages that only repeat another page are merged or removed rather than kept for volume.

Who is accountable

Content is published under the name VAT in UK team. The site does not use individual bylines because the work is maintained by a small editorial team and no single person is the author of every page.

We do not invent authors, reviewers or credentials. A person is named on this site only where that person has agreed in writing to be named. No page claims review by an accountant, tax adviser or solicitor unless that review actually happened.

Accountability sits with the team that publishes the page: whoever changes a figure is responsible for the source behind it, and for updating the review date only when the fact has been rechecked.

Sources we allow

Primary sources only. In practice that means:

  • GOV.UK guidance for rates, thresholds, deadlines and scheme rules.
  • HMRC VAT notices and manuals, such as VAT Notice 700, 700/1, 708, 733 and 741A, where the detail is not on the guidance page.
  • legislation.gov.uk for the Value Added Tax Act 1994 and related instruments.

We do not cite blogs, forums, accountancy marketing pages or other calculators as authority. A secondary source can suggest a question to check, but it is never the reason a figure is published.

Each content page lists the specific sources behind its own figures at the bottom, and the same links are recorded in the page's structured data so the citation trail is machine-readable.

How facts are checked

  1. The claim is identified: a rate, threshold, deadline, definition or procedure.
  2. The current GOV.UK page or HMRC notice is opened and read at the point where the claim is made.
  3. Where a rule has a legal basis, the section of the Act or statutory instrument is checked as well.
  4. The source link is recorded in our source registry. A citation that does not match the visible source list on the page fails the build.
  5. A second pass checks that the visible sources on the page and the structured citation data match.

Automated checks catch broken internal links, mismatched citations, invalid structured data, duplicate IDs and encoding faults. They do not decide whether a sentence is true; that remains an editorial judgement.

A fact is only treated as checked when the relevant passage has been read in this pass. Reusing wording from another page is not verification, because the same sentence can be right in one context and wrong in another.

Review dates

Each page carries a review date. The date moves only when the underlying facts have been rechecked, not when a page is reformatted or a sentence is rewritten for style. Pages are reviewed on a scheduled cycle and immediately after any Budget, rate change, threshold change or new HMRC notice that affects them.

The visible date and the structured data share one value at build time, so they cannot drift apart. If a page's facts were not reopened, its date stays where it was, even if other pages in the same collection were rechecked.

A stale date is treated as a defect, not as a neutral detail. If a page has not been checked in a year and its subject can change, it is prioritised for review.

Use of automation

Build scripts help with repeated work: page templates, source lists, structured data, link checks and arithmetic tests. Some drafting is assisted by software. Every published figure and every statement about a rule is checked by a person against a primary source before it goes live.

Worked examples are arithmetic, not case studies. When a page shows a bill, a margin or a threshold timeline, the numbers are chosen to show the rule and are calculated from it. We do not present them as real clients, real disputes or anonymised cases.

We do not publish unattended machine-written explanations of tax rules, invented case studies or synthetic quotes from advisers.

Corrections

If a figure, date or rule is wrong, we want to know. Report it through the contact page or the issue tracker, with the page and the source you are relying on.

  • Confirmed factual errors are corrected on the page and, where the change matters to a reader's decision, recorded in the corrections log.
  • The review date is updated when the correction involves a fact, and left alone when the change is purely editorial.
  • We do not delete a page to hide an error. If a page is retired, its replacement is linked.

Independence and funding

The site is independent of HMRC and of any accountancy firm. It is not a government service and it does not give tax advice. It is free to use, sets no cookies and carries no third-party analytics at the time of writing.

If advertising or affiliate links are added later, they will be labelled, separated from editorial content, and disclosed on this page and in the privacy and cookies policies before they go live. Payment will never decide what a page says about a VAT rule.

Questions about this policy can be raised on the contact page or in the public issue tracker.
