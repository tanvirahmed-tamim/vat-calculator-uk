VAT for E-commerce: UK Sellers, Platforms and Imports

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Online selling adds three questions to the usual VAT calculation: who is responsible for the VAT, where the goods are, and what happens when a customer returns them.

In short

VAT-registered online sellers charge 20% on taxable goods, but who accounts for it depends on the platform and where the goods are. A marketplace can be the deemed supplier, imports in consignments of £135 or less are taxed at the point of sale, and refunds need a credit note.

Who this is for: Amazon, eBay, Etsy and Shopify sellers, dropshippers, and retailers selling physical or digital goods into and within the UK.

Registration

Register when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. Include sales through marketplaces, your own website and any other channel: the threshold looks at all taxable sales, not just one shopfront.

Selling through a marketplace does not remove your own registration duty on direct sales. A business established outside the UK has no UK registration threshold, so it must register as soon as it makes taxable sales in the UK or holds stock here for sale.

Who accounts for the VAT

An online marketplace can be treated as the supplier for VAT purposes. That is called deemed supply, and it moves the obligation to charge and declare VAT from the seller to the platform. The result depends on where the goods are and who owns them.

Marketplace VAT liability for sales to UK customers
Situation Who accounts for VAT
Goods outside the UK at the point of sale, consignment of £135 or less The marketplace, where it facilitates the sale. Without a marketplace, the overseas seller must register and account for UK VAT.
Goods already in the UK, owned by an overseas seller, sold through a marketplace to a consumer The marketplace, at any value. The overseas seller makes a zero-rated deemed supply to the marketplace and shows the value in box 6.
Goods sold directly by a UK business, with no marketplace involved The UK business, under the normal VAT rules.

Do not declare output tax twice. If the platform has accounted for VAT on a sale, keep its VAT report with your records and leave that sale out of your own output tax. The platform's commission is a separate supply to you, and any VAT it charges can be reclaimed as input tax when you hold a valid invoice.

HMRC treats an interface as a marketplace when it sets the terms of supply, is involved in authorising or taking payment, and is involved in ordering or delivery. A business that only processes payments, or only lists or advertises goods, is not a marketplace for these rules.

The £135 threshold

For goods outside the UK at the point of sale, a consignment valued at £135 or less is taxed at the point of sale rather than at the border. The threshold is not an exemption: VAT is still due, but the seller or marketplace charges it on the sale instead of the importer paying import VAT.

The test is the intrinsic value of the whole consignment, not the price of each item. It is the sale price excluding transport and insurance, unless those costs are included in the price and not shown separately, and excluding other taxes and charges. If a consignment contains several goods and its total value is over £135, normal import VAT applies instead.

Sales to a UK VAT-registered business also fall under the £135 rules, but the customer accounts for the VAT by reverse charge when it gives a valid UK VAT number. The invoice must say so, for example reverse charge: customer to account for VAT to HMRC. If no valid number is given, treat the sale as business to consumer.

The rules do not apply to consignments containing excise goods, to non-commercial gifts between private individuals, or to consignments from Jersey and Guernsey covered by the Import VAT Accounting Scheme. Gift relief for consignments valued up to £39 remains.

Import VAT

Goods imported into the UK above the £135 threshold are subject to import VAT at the border. A VAT-registered importer can use postponed VAT accounting to declare and recover the same amount on one VAT return instead of paying it upfront.

With postponed VAT accounting, include the import VAT in box 1 and the recoverable amount in box 4. Include the value of the imports, excluding VAT, in box 7. The monthly postponed import VAT statement supports the figures, and the normal input tax rules still apply.

Keep the customs declaration and either the import VAT certificate (C79) or the postponed import VAT statement. HMRC can ask for them long after the return has been submitted.

Delivery and refunds

Delivery charged as part of a sale follows the VAT treatment of the goods. If the goods are standard-rated, the delivery is standard-rated. A separate delivery service that a customer can buy on its own is normally standard-rated.

Discounts applied before the VAT point reduce the taxable amount. A discount given after the invoice has been issued usually needs a credit note. A refund also needs a credit note, and the output tax is adjusted in the period the credit note is issued. Where a marketplace was the deemed supplier, the refund and the VAT adjustment belong to the marketplace, not to the seller.

Gift cards and vouchers have their own rules. Do not assume VAT is due when the card is sold; check the position when it is redeemed.

Records

Keep electronic records of sales, marketplace VAT reports, import documents and credit notes for six years. Marketplaces and direct sellers covered by the overseas goods rules must be able to provide those records to HMRC electronically on request.

Reconcile the platform payout to your VAT return rather than to gross sales. Fees, refunds, shipping and currency movements mean the payout is rarely the same as the taxable turnover reported on the return.

Common mistakes

  • Counting only website sales when checking the registration threshold.
  • Declaring output tax on a sale the marketplace has already accounted for.
  • Applying the £135 threshold to each item instead of the whole consignment.
  • Treating the £135 threshold as an exemption rather than a change in where VAT is collected.
  • Adjusting refunds in the wrong VAT period.

Related terms

Distance selling, Place of supply, Credit note.

Questions

Do I need to register for VAT to sell on Amazon?
Only if your taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months. Amazon may account for VAT on some sales, but registration depends on your own turnover and circumstances.
Does Amazon collect VAT for me?
Sometimes. A marketplace can be the deemed supplier for goods in the UK owned by an overseas seller, and for consignments of £135 or less sold to UK customers. Check your seller agreement and the platform's VAT reports.
What is the £135 threshold?
The £135 threshold is the value of a consignment of goods located outside the UK at the point of sale. At £135 or less, VAT is collected at the point of sale instead of at import. It is not an exemption.
Is the £135 threshold per item or per consignment?
The threshold applies per consignment. If several goods are sent together, add their intrinsic values. If the consignment is over £135, normal import VAT applies.
Do overseas sellers have a VAT registration threshold?
No. A business established outside the UK has no UK registration threshold and must register as soon as it makes taxable sales in the UK or holds stock here for sale.
Is delivery charged VAT?
Yes, at the same rate as the goods when it is part of the sale. A separate delivery service is normally standard-rated.
How do I handle refunds?
Issue a credit note, refund the customer, and reduce your output tax in the period the credit note is issued. If a marketplace was the deemed supplier, the marketplace handles the VAT adjustment.
