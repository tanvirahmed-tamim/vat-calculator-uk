VAT on EU Sales After Brexit: Rules for UK Businesses

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of International and cross-border VAT

Brexit moved the UK outside the EU VAT area. Sales of goods from Great Britain to the EU are now exports, while sales into the UK from the EU follow UK import rules.

In short

Goods exported from Great Britain to the EU can be zero-rated with evidence. B2B services are usually outside the scope of UK VAT, B2C services depend on the type of service, and digital services to EU consumers are taxed in the customer's country.

Who this is for: UK businesses selling to the EU, EU businesses selling to the UK, and marketplace sellers with cross-border sales.

What changed

From 1 January 2021 the UK is outside the EU VAT area. A sale of goods from Great Britain to the EU is an export, and a sale from the EU to Great Britain is an import. The old acquisition VAT, EC Sales List and EU distance-selling threshold no longer apply to GB businesses.

Northern Ireland is different. Goods moving between Northern Ireland and the EU follow the Windsor Framework, which is covered on a separate page.

Goods from GB to the EU

Goods sold from Great Britain to the EU are zero-rated exports when you meet the conditions and keep evidence. The goods must leave the UK and you must obtain the evidence within 3 months of the time of supply.

The EU customer normally pays import VAT and any customs duty on arrival. The Incoterms in the contract decide who arranges transport and who acts as importer. Under DDP terms the seller handles the EU import; under ex-works terms the buyer does.

You still need a commercial invoice, a customs declaration and, where the customer is a business, its EU VAT number. Do not charge UK VAT on the export.

Services

For most B2B services, the place of supply is where the customer belongs. A UK business invoicing an EU business therefore does not charge UK VAT, and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge. Keep evidence of the customer's business status and location, such as a VAT number, contract and business website.

UK businesses no longer submit an EC Sales List for services supplied to EU businesses. The reverse charge wording on the invoice should still make the position clear, for example reverse charge: customer to account for VAT.

B2C services

The general rule for B2C services is that the place of supply is where the supplier belongs, so a UK supplier usually charges UK VAT at 20% to a consumer abroad.

There is an important exception for professional, technical and intangible services. Where those are supplied to a consumer outside the UK, the place of supply is the customer's country and the supply is outside the scope of UK VAT. The same applies where the use and enjoyment rules move the supply outside the UK.

Land, events, passenger transport, restaurant and catering services and digital services have their own place-of-supply rules. Do not assume the general B2C rule applies to every service.

Digital services

Digital services to consumers are taxed where the consumer is located. A UK business selling downloads, streaming, SaaS or automated online courses to EU consumers does not charge UK VAT; EU VAT is due instead.

UK businesses can no longer use the UK VAT MOSS system. The EU-wide threshold for digital services also ceased to apply to UK businesses, so EU VAT is due on all supplies of digital services to EU consumers regardless of the value of the sales.

The options are to register for the Non-Union VAT MOSS scheme in one EU member state, or to register for VAT in each member state where you have customers. A platform that facilitates the sale can be liable for the VAT instead of the seller.

OSS and IOSS

The One Stop Shop lets a business report VAT on EU distance sales through one registration. The UK's OSS Union scheme is for distance sales of goods from Northern Ireland to consumers in the EU. It applies once sales exceed the pan-EU threshold of £8,818 (10,000 euros).

The UK scheme does not cover goods sent from Great Britain to the EU. A GB business must use an EU OSS registration or register in each member state. The same applies to services, where the Non-Union scheme is the usual route for a non-EU business.

IOSS is the Import One Stop Shop for consignments into the EU valued at 150 euros or less. It is an EU scheme, not a UK one. A GB business can register for IOSS in an EU member state and then account for import VAT on low-value sales through one return. If it does not register, the EU customer pays import VAT at the border.

Selling into the UK

An EU business selling goods into Great Britain follows UK import rules. Consignments valued at £135 or less are taxed at the point of sale, and a UK marketplace can be the deemed supplier for sales by overseas sellers.

A business established outside the UK has no UK VAT registration threshold. It must register as soon as it makes taxable sales in the UK or holds stock here for sale. For digital services to UK consumers, UK VAT is due and the non-UK seller must register with HMRC.

Evidence

Keep the export declaration, the commercial invoice, the shipping documents and the customer's location evidence. For services, keep the contract, the customer's VAT number and correspondence showing where the customer belongs.

Cross-border records are the first thing a compliance check will ask for. Without evidence, a zero-rated export can be reassessed as standard-rated and a B2B treatment can fail.

Common mistakes

  • Zero-rating an EU sale without export evidence and a customs declaration.
  • Charging UK VAT on B2B services to an EU business.
  • Assuming every B2C service follows the supplier's location.
  • Trying to use the UK OSS scheme for goods sent from Great Britain to the EU.
  • Treating IOSS as a UK scheme rather than an EU one.

Related terms

Export, OSS, Place of supply.

Questions

Do I charge VAT on sales to the EU?
Not on goods exported from Great Britain to the EU, provided you keep export evidence. B2B services to EU businesses are usually outside the scope of UK VAT.
Can I use UK VAT MOSS for EU sales?
No. UK businesses can no longer use the UK VAT MOSS system. You must use the Non-Union scheme in an EU member state or register for VAT in each member state where you have customers.
What is OSS?
The One Stop Shop, which lets a business report VAT on EU distance sales through one registration. The UK OSS Union scheme covers goods sent from Northern Ireland to EU consumers, not goods sent from Great Britain.
What is IOSS?
The Import One Stop Shop, an EU scheme for consignments valued at 150 euros or less. A GB business registers in an EU member state; if it does not, the EU customer pays import VAT at the border.
Do EU sellers need a UK VAT number?
Often yes, when selling goods into the UK. The £135 threshold and marketplace rules decide who accounts for the VAT, and a non-UK business has no UK registration threshold.
Do I charge UK VAT to an EU consumer?
For most B2C services, yes, because the place of supply is where the supplier belongs. Professional, technical and intangible services to consumers outside the UK are outside the scope, and digital services are taxed where the consumer is located.
What evidence do I need for a zero-rated EU sale?
Keep the export declaration, commercial invoice, shipping documents and customer location evidence. The goods must leave the UK and the evidence must be obtained within 3 months.
