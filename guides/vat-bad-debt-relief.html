What Is VAT Bad Debt Relief and How Do You Claim It in the UK?

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

VAT bad debt relief lets a VAT-registered supplier recover output tax on an invoice that a customer has not paid. Under invoice accounting, the supplier pays VAT when it issues the invoice, even if the money never arrives. Relief corrects that mismatch once the debt is old enough and written off, and it follows a strict set of conditions in HMRC VAT Notice 700/18.

In short

Wait 6 months from the later of the due date and the supply date, write the debt off to a separate bad debt account, then claim the VAT in Box 4. The final deadline is 4 years and 6 months from that same later date. If the customer pays later, repay the VAT element in Box 1.

Who this is for: UK limited companies, sole traders, partnerships and finance teams that invoice customers on credit and need to deal with unpaid sales invoices, insolvency and input tax clawback.

General information: This guide is based on HMRC VAT Notice 700/18, the Value Added Tax Act 1994, VAT Notice 700 and VAT Notice 700/12. It is not personal tax, legal or accounting advice. Check the current GOV.UK guidance or confirm your treatment with a qualified UK accountant before you claim or file.

Why Does VAT Bad Debt Relief Exist Under Invoice Accounting?

Under invoice accounting, VAT becomes due at the tax point, which is usually the invoice date or the date the goods or services are supplied. The supplier must account for output tax on that sale in the return covering the tax point, even when the customer has 30, 60 or 90 days to pay. If the customer never pays, the supplier has paid VAT on income it never received.

Bad debt relief removes that unfair result. It lets the supplier recover the output tax that relates to the unpaid amount, so the VAT position reflects the commercial reality. The relief is not a credit note and it is not automatic. The supplier must meet every condition, keep a separate record and make the claim within the time limit.

The relief matters most to businesses that carry large debtor balances: wholesalers, manufacturers, recruitment agencies, professional services firms and construction subcontractors. It also matters when a major customer becomes insolvent, because the VAT already paid to HMRC can be a significant cash loss on top of the unpaid invoice.

Invoice accounting versus cash accounting

A business on the Cash Accounting Scheme accounts for output tax when the customer pays, not when it invoices. If the customer never pays, no output tax arises, so there is nothing to reclaim. That is why bad debt relief is unnecessary under cash accounting and why the scheme can reduce bad debt VAT risk for smaller businesses.

Invoice accounting still suits many businesses because it returns input tax sooner and matches the way larger customers expect to trade. The trade-off is that the supplier carries the VAT risk until the debt is paid or relief is claimed.

What Conditions Must You Meet to Claim VAT Bad Debt Relief?

HMRC sets six conditions for a valid claim. The rules apply to supplies made on or after 1 April 1989, and the current time limits apply to supplies made after 30 April 1997. Check each condition before you put an amount in Box 4.

The six conditions

  1. You have already accounted for the VAT on the supply and paid it to HMRC.
  2. You have written the debt off in your day to day VAT accounts and transferred it to a separate bad debt account.
  3. The value of the supply is not more than the customary selling price.
  4. The debt has not been paid, sold or factored under a valid legal assignment.
  5. The debt has remained unpaid for at least 6 months after the later of the due date for payment and the date of supply.
  6. You make the claim within 4 years and 6 months of that same later date.

The customary selling price test stops a supplier from inflating a claim by charging a connected customer more than the open market price. If you sold at a normal commercial price to an unconnected customer, the test is usually straightforward. If the customer is connected, keep evidence that the price matched what you charge others.

The 6 month waiting rule

The clock starts on the later of the due date for payment and the date of supply. A supply made on 15 January 2025 with payment due on 15 February 2025 reaches the 6 month point on 15 August 2025. You cannot claim on a return for a period that ends before that date, because entitlement has not yet arisen.

If you agree a longer credit period with the customer, the later due date moves the start of the 6 months. In the absence of a separate agreement, HMRC accepts the invoice date as the due date. Keep the credit terms and the invoice in your records so the date is easy to prove.

The 4 year and 6 month time limit

The final deadline is 4 years and 6 months from the later of the due date and the supply date. Using the same example, a debt that reaches its 6 month point on 15 August 2025 must be claimed by 15 February 2030. The deadline is not extended because you were late in writing the debt off. If you miss it, the output tax stays with HMRC.

Claim in the return for the period in which the 6 month wait ends, or in a later return before the deadline. A late claim inside the deadline is still valid, but it delays the cash recovery and makes the bad debt account harder to reconcile.

Partial payments, set-off and security

You can claim only on the amount that remains unpaid. If the customer has paid part of the invoice, the relief is limited to the VAT inside the outstanding balance. Allocate payments to the earliest supply unless the customer specifies a particular supply and pays for that supply in full. That rule matters when one customer has several invoices at different VAT rates.

Reduce the claim by any amount you owe the customer and can set off against the debt. Reduce it by the value of any enforceable security you hold. If you receive payment from a guarantor or another person, reduce the claim by that amount. Insurance proceeds for bad debts do not reduce your entitlement.

Assignment and factoring

You cannot claim relief while a debt is absolutely assigned to a factor or debt buyer, because you no longer own the debt. If the contract provides for reassignment, relief becomes available once the debt is reassigned to you. No relief is available during the period in which the factor holds the debt.

Invoice discounting and factoring with recourse often leave the debt with you, but the contract terms decide the VAT treatment. Read the assignment clause before you claim, and keep a copy with the bad debt records.

How Much Bad Debt Relief Can You Claim? (Worked GBP Examples)

Start with the VAT-inclusive amount that remains unpaid, then separate the VAT using the VAT fraction for the rate on the invoice. At 20% the fraction is 20/120, which is the same as 1/6. At 5% it is 5/105, which is 1/21. The VAT fraction calculator handles the arithmetic if you need to check a figure.

Example: £1,200.00 invoice with a part payment

A standard-rated invoice totals £1,200.00, made up of £1,000.00 net and £200.00 VAT. The customer pays £480.00 and then stops paying. The VAT inside the payment is £480.00 × 20/120 = £80.00. The unpaid balance is £720.00, and the VAT inside it is £720.00 × 20/120 = £120.00.

The supplier originally paid £200.00 of output tax. It keeps £80.00 as the VAT on the amount the customer actually paid and recovers £120.00 as relief. The net VAT cost then matches the £80.00 the customer paid.

Example: later recovery of £300.00

Suppose the customer later pays £300.00 towards the £720.00 balance. The VAT element is £300.00 × 20/120 = £50.00. The supplier adds £50.00 to Box 1 in the return for the period in which the payment arrives. Relief already claimed falls from £120.00 to £70.00, which is the VAT still tied to the £420.00 that remains unpaid.

HMRC expresses the same adjustment as the claim amount multiplied by the payment received, divided by the amount outstanding when the claim was made. Here that is £120.00 × £300.00 ÷ £720.00 = £50.00. Use the formula when the original claim was restricted, for example by a set-off or a margin scheme.

Invoices with more than one VAT rate

Calculate the relief separately for each rate. A £600.00 standard-rated balance carries £100.00 of VAT at 20/120, while a £420.00 reduced-rated balance carries £20.00 at 5/105. Do not apply a single fraction to a mixed invoice, because the VAT content differs by line.

Where Does Bad Debt Relief Go on a VAT Return?

Include the relief in Box 4 of the VAT return for the period in which the conditions are met. Box 4 is the input tax box, and HMRC treats the relief as an input tax adjustment. Do not reduce Box 1, because the original output tax was correctly accounted for when the invoice was issued.

Buyer input tax clawback under section 26A

The rule works in both directions. A VAT-registered buyer that has not paid a supplier within 6 months of the later of the due date and the supply date must reduce its Box 4 input tax on the unpaid amount. Section 26A of the Value Added Tax Act 1994 imposes the adjustment, and the buyer must monitor unpaid creditors without waiting for a notice from the supplier.

If the buyer pays later, it can reclaim the input tax in Box 4 of the return for the period in which the payment is made. The amount is based on the VAT originally deducted, so a partially exempt buyer repays only the recoverable proportion. Section 26AA disapplies the clawback where an insolvency procedure starts after the supply but before the repayment date, so check the position before adjusting an insolvent supplier.

Reverse charge supplies work differently. The supplier cannot claim bad debt relief because the customer accounts for the VAT. If the consideration stays unpaid after 6 months, the customer may reduce both the input tax and the corresponding output tax, following the rules in VAT Notice 735.

Insolvency and administration

Insolvency does not remove the 6 month wait. If a customer enters administration, liquidation or a company voluntary arrangement, write the debt off to the bad debt account and claim once the normal conditions are met. Keep the insolvency notice, the creditor correspondence and the proof of debt with the claim records.

If the insolvency practitioner later pays a dividend, treat that payment as a recovery. Repay the VAT element in Box 1 for the period in which you receive it. Do not wait until the insolvency closes, because each payment creates its own adjustment.

What Records Should You Keep for a Bad Debt Relief Claim?

HMRC expects a separate bad debt account, not a note in the sales ledger. The account must show the amount written off, the VAT claimed, the period of the claim, the total VAT charged on each supply, the period in which you originally accounted for that VAT, the payments received, the customer name and the invoice date and number.

  • A copy of the VAT invoice, or an equivalent document if you did not issue one.
  • The payment history and any credit terms that set the due date.
  • Evidence that you chased the debt, such as statements, emails or letters.
  • The bad debt account showing the write-off and the relief calculation.
  • The VAT return period in which you claimed and the amount claimed.
  • Insolvency notices, guarantor payments or security documents where relevant.
  • Records of any later payment and the Box 1 repayment.

Keep the bad debt records for 4 years from the date of the claim. That sits alongside the standard 6 year VAT record period, so the practical retention date is usually the later of the two. The VAT record keeping guide covers digital records and the wider retention rules, and the VAT invoices and MTD guide explains what a valid invoice must show.

How Do Special VAT Schemes Affect Bad Debt Relief?

The scheme you use changes whether relief is available and how you claim it.

  • Cash accounting: no relief, because output tax follows payment and an unpaid invoice never creates a VAT liability.
  • Annual accounting: claim on the same annual return in which you accounted for the supply, provided the conditions are met by the end of the annual accounting period.
  • Flat Rate Scheme: relief is available, but the calculation differs. On the basic or retailer's turnover method, claim at the standard rate on eligible supplies. On the cash turnover method, claim the difference between the VAT charged and the flat rate VAT you would have paid.
  • Margin scheme: relief is capped at the VAT on the margin. If the debt is larger than the margin, claim the VAT fraction of the margin, not the whole debt.
  • Retail schemes: schemes that adjust daily gross takings for opening and closing debtors already account for VAT on amounts received, so relief is unnecessary.

Group registrations need their own discipline. Each group member keeps a separate bad debt record, and the representative member makes the claim on behalf of the group. If a member leaves the group before the claim, the former member usually makes the claim.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Claiming before the 6 month point because the invoice is old, without checking the due date.
  • Claiming the full invoice VAT when the customer has paid part of the balance.
  • Issuing a credit note for non-payment instead of using bad debt relief.
  • Forgetting to repay the VAT element when the customer pays after the claim.
  • Claiming on a debt that has been absolutely assigned to a factor.
  • Ignoring the buyer clawback when your own supplier invoices remain unpaid.
  • Keeping the claim in the sales ledger without a separate bad debt account.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to claim VAT bad debt relief?
You have 6 months from the later of the due date for payment and the date of supply before you can claim. You must then claim within 4 years and 6 months of that same later date. Claim on the VAT return for the period in which the 6 month wait ends.
Can I claim bad debt relief if the customer pays part of the invoice?
Yes. You can claim relief on the VAT included in the unpaid balance only. If the customer pays more later, you repay the VAT element of that payment in Box 1 of the return for the period in which you receive it.
Do I need to issue a credit note for a bad debt?
No. A credit note is not the correct route for a customer who simply has not paid. HMRC allows a credit note only for a genuine mistake, an overcharge or an agreed reduction in the value of the supply. Use bad debt relief instead.
What records must I keep for a bad debt relief claim?
Keep a separate bad debt account, the VAT invoice or equivalent document, the payment history, the date and period of the original VAT accounting, the claim calculation and the VAT period of the claim. Keep these records for 4 years from the date of the claim.
Can I claim VAT bad debt relief under cash accounting?
No. Cash accounting delays output tax until the customer pays, so there is no VAT on an unpaid invoice to reclaim. The same logic applies to retail schemes that adjust daily gross takings for debtors.
What happens if the customer pays after I claim?
You must repay the VAT element of the later payment in Box 1 of the VAT return for the period in which you receive it. Use the claim amount multiplied by the payment received, divided by the amount outstanding when you made the claim.
Does the buyer have to repay input tax on an unpaid invoice?
Yes. A VAT-registered buyer must normally reduce Box 4 input tax if any part of the invoice remains unpaid 6 months after the later of the due date and the supply date. The reduction covers the VAT on the unpaid amount, and the buyer can reclaim it in Box 4 when the invoice is paid, subject to the insolvency exception in section 26AA.
