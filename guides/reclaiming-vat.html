Reclaiming VAT and input tax

Maintained by the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 8 min read

Reclaiming VAT, formally called input tax, is what balances the VAT a business pays against the VAT it charges. The rules are mostly about eligibility and evidence rather than arithmetic: is the cost for business use, is there a valid invoice, and does anything block the claim?

In short

A VAT-registered business can usually reclaim the VAT it pays on business purchases, called input tax. The cost must be for business use, supported by a VAT invoice, and linked to taxable supplies. Business entertainment of UK clients, most cars and private spending are blocked.

What input tax is

Input tax is the VAT a VAT-registered business pays on goods and services it buys for the business. On each VAT return it is set against the output tax charged on sales, and the difference is what you pay HMRC or reclaim.

For a business that makes only standard-rated sales, the arithmetic is mechanical. The judgement is about eligibility, and most disputes with HMRC start there rather than with the sums.

The three tests for a claim

  1. The cost is for business use, not private spending.
  2. You hold a valid VAT invoice or receipt that shows the VAT.
  3. The cost relates to taxable supplies, so it is not blocked by an exempt activity.

If all three are met, the input tax is normally recoverable in full. If only part of the cost is for business use, only that proportion of the VAT is recoverable.

Extracting the VAT from a cost

Receipts usually show a VAT-inclusive total, so use the VAT fraction rather than subtracting the rate:

VAT = gross × rate ÷ (100 + rate)

At 20%, a £120 gross expense contains £20 of VAT and £100 of net cost. The VAT on expenses calculator applies the fraction and any business-use percentage in one step.

Worked example: a phone bill

A monthly phone bill is £120 including VAT, so the VAT is £20. The contract is 60% for business use, based on itemised usage kept with the bill. The reclaimable input tax is 60% of £20, which is £12. The remaining £8 is private and cannot be claimed.

Mixed business and private use

When a cost is used partly for business and partly privately, reclaim only the business proportion. A phone used 60% for work allows 60% of the VAT to be claimed, provided the split is reasonable and supported by records. Over time, a series of small private claims can add up, so keep evidence for the apportionment you use.

There is an alternative for some assets: claim all the VAT and account for output tax on the private use through the capital goods scheme or an annual private-use charge. That is more administration, not less tax, and only suits specific assets.

Partial exemption

A business that makes both taxable and exempt sales cannot usually recover all of its input tax. It has to attribute costs and apply the partial exemption method agreed with HMRC. Many smaller businesses stay inside the de minimis limit and recover everything, but the limit is tested over the year and is easy to exceed in a single quarter.

If any of your sales are exempt, read partial exemption before assuming a full claim.

Evidence and deadlines

Keep the VAT invoice or receipt, note the business purpose, and record how any apportionment was worked out. Input tax can normally be claimed up to four years after the return period it relates to, and records must be kept for six years.

A claim needs evidence. If the invoice is missing, ask the supplier for a copy rather than reconstructing the VAT from a bank statement. A bank statement alone does not show the VAT charged.

Common mistakes

  • Claiming gross as net. The VAT is the fraction of the gross, not the whole amount.
  • Claiming client entertaining. It is blocked for UK clients even when the meeting was clearly for business.
  • Ignoring private use. A mobile, broadband or home office bill needs an honest business percentage.
  • Claiming before the invoice arrives. Wait until you hold valid evidence.
  • Overlooking the claim window. Older input tax is not lost immediately, but the four-year limit does apply.

Questions

Can I reclaim VAT on everything I buy for my business?
No. Most business costs qualify, but business entertainment of UK clients, most cars available for private use, private spending and costs linked to exempt supplies are blocked or restricted.
How much VAT can I reclaim on a partly private expense?
Only the business proportion. If a cost is 80% for business, 80% of the VAT is normally recoverable, as long as the split is reasonable and you keep a record of how it was worked out.
How long do I have to claim input tax?
You can normally claim input tax up to four years after the return period it relates to, provided you hold the evidence. Keep VAT records for six years.
What proof do I need to reclaim VAT?
A valid VAT invoice or receipt showing the VAT, plus a note of the business purpose and any private-use apportionment. Without evidence, HMRC can refuse the claim.
