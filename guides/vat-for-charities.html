VAT for Charities: Reliefs, Trading and Zero-Rated Sales

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Charities are VAT-registered in the same way as businesses, but much of their income is not business income. Separating the two is the key to knowing what VAT you charge, what you can reclaim and which reliefs apply.

In short

Charities can reclaim VAT only on business activities. Donations and most grants are outside the scope, fundraising events can be exempt, donated goods sold in charity shops are zero-rated, and some purchases qualify for charity reliefs.

Who this is for: Registered charities, community interest companies, sports clubs, social enterprises and not-for-profit organisations.

Business versus non-business

A charity can recover VAT only on costs that relate to its business activities. The test is whether there is a supply of goods or services for consideration, made for the purpose of obtaining income. A not-for-profit organisation can still be carrying on a business activity for VAT.

Grants, donations and voluntary income with nothing supplied in return are usually outside the scope of VAT. VAT on costs that relate only to that income is generally not recoverable.

Where a cost supports both business and non-business activity, apportion it on a fair and reasonable basis. A VAT-registered charity then applies the partial exemption rules to any exempt business income. Keep the method and the supporting records.

Registration

Only taxable business income counts towards the £90,000 registration threshold. Donations and exempt fundraising income do not count. A charity below the threshold can register voluntarily, but a charity with no taxable supplies at all cannot register.

Registration brings the right to recover input tax on taxable activities, but it also restricts recovery on exempt activities and non-business costs. Check the position before registering voluntarily, because the extra records and returns may cost more than the VAT recovered.

Fundraising events

A qualifying fundraising event is exempt from VAT under Group 12 of Schedule 9 to the VAT Act 1994. The event must be organised to raise money and promoted so that people attending know it is a fundraiser. The exemption covers admission charges and other income generated at the event, such as food and commemorative items.

The exemption is limited to 15 events of the same kind at the same location in the charity's financial year. If a charity holds 16 or more, none of them qualify. Small events with gross takings of no more than £1,000 a week, such as coffee mornings, do not count towards the 15-event limit.

The exemption does not cover normal trading, sales of surplus goods after the event, or recordings sold later. Fundraising holidays and day trips that fall within the Tour Operators Margin Scheme are also excluded. VAT on purchases for an exempt event is generally not recoverable, although VAT on taxable supplies made at the event may be.

If an event does not qualify, the charity can set a standard-rated minimum charge and invite a voluntary donation on top. The donation is outside the scope only if the publicity makes clear that the minimum charge admits the person without further payment, the extra payment buys no additional benefit, and the amount is left to the ticket holder.

Charity shops

The sale, hire or export of goods donated to a charity, or to its trading subsidiary, is zero-rated. The goods must have been donated, be offered to the public or to at least two disabled or means-tested benefit recipients, and not have been used by the charity first. An arrangement with the donor or buyer before the goods go on sale can remove the zero rate.

Bought-in goods are different. A charity shop selling bought-in goods accounts for VAT at the standard rate unless the goods are zero-rated by statute, such as books or children's clothes. Keep donated and bought-in stock separate so the two treatments can be evidenced.

Grants and sponsorship

A grant with no goods or services supplied in return is outside the scope of VAT. Conditions that make sure the money is spent as intended are good housekeeping, not consideration for a supply. Funding becomes taxable when the funder, or a third party, receives a direct benefit in return.

Sponsorship is standard-rated when the sponsor receives a significant benefit, such as advertising or naming rights. A flag, a sticker or a name in a list of supporters is normally insignificant. Where a payment mixes sponsorship and donation, separate the two in the agreement so the donation can stay outside the scope.

Charity reliefs

Some supplies to charities are zero-rated. They include medical and veterinary equipment bought with charity funds, aids for disabled people, advertising and goods used to collect donations, talking books, sea rescue equipment, and the construction of buildings used solely for a relevant charitable purpose.

Fuel and power for a charity's non-business use can be reduced-rated at 5%. The reliefs depend on the goods, the status of the charity and the use, and some require a certificate or declaration. Check the conditions before treating a purchase as zero-rated.

The zero-rated versus exempt guide explains why the distinction matters for recovery.

Trading subsidiaries

Many charities carry out non-primary purpose trading through a separate subsidiary. The subsidiary is not itself a charity, so most charity reliefs do not apply to it. It is VAT-registered in its own right and can gift-aid its profits to the charity.

Keep the two entities' VAT records separate. A charity and its subsidiary may be able to form a VAT group if the conditions are met, but grouping is not automatic and changes how supplies between them are treated.

Common mistakes

  • Reclaiming VAT on costs linked only to grants or donations.
  • Charging VAT on donated goods sold in a charity shop.
  • Treating the 16th event of the same kind as exempt.
  • Applying a charity relief without checking the goods and use.
  • Assuming a trading subsidiary can use the charity's reliefs.

Questions

Can charities reclaim VAT?
Only on business activities. VAT linked to grants and donations is generally not recoverable, because that income is outside the scope of VAT.
Are donations subject to VAT?
No. A genuine donation with nothing supplied in return is outside the scope of VAT.
Is a charity fundraising event exempt from VAT?
Often, if it is organised and promoted as a fundraiser and is one of no more than 15 events of the same kind at that location in the charity's financial year. Events with gross takings of £1,000 a week or less do not count towards the limit.
Are donated goods sold in a charity shop VAT-free?
Yes, normally zero-rated, provided the goods were donated, are offered to the public, have not been used by the charity first and are not sold under an arrangement made before donation.
Do bought-in goods in a charity shop carry VAT?
Yes, at the standard rate unless the goods are zero-rated by statute, such as books or children's clothes.
Can a charity buy equipment without VAT?
Some purchases qualify for zero-rating, including medical and veterinary equipment bought with charity funds, aids for disabled people and advertising. Check the specific relief before assuming a purchase is VAT-free.
Does a trading subsidiary share the charity's VAT registration?
No, usually not. A trading subsidiary is a separate entity with its own VAT registration and records, and most charity reliefs do not apply to it.
