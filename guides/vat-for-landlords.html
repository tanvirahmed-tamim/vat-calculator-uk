VAT for Landlords: Rent, Property and Option to Tax

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Landlords meet VAT in three places: the rent, the cost of the property, and the building work. The first two depend on whether the property is residential or commercial, and whether you have opted to tax.

In short

Residential rent is exempt, so you cannot charge VAT or reclaim VAT on related costs. Commercial rent is also exempt unless you opt to tax the property, which adds 20% VAT but allows input tax recovery. New builds can be zero-rated, some conversions and renovations 5%, and holiday lets are standard-rated.

Who this is for: Residential landlords, commercial property owners, serviced accommodation hosts and anyone letting property through a company.

Residential rent

Rent from residential property is exempt from VAT. You do not charge VAT, and you generally cannot reclaim VAT on costs linked to that letting, including repairs, letting agent fees and furnishings. For most residential landlords, VAT is a cost rather than a reclaimable tax.

Service charges and management fees follow the same treatment when they are part of the exempt residential letting. A separate supply, such as cleaning or furniture hire, may be taxable, so keep the invoices separate.

Exempt does not mean outside the VAT system. If you are VAT-registered for another activity, the residential rent still belongs in box 6 of your return, even though no VAT is charged on it.

Commercial rent

Commercial rent is exempt by default. The landlord does not charge VAT and cannot recover input tax on the property unless the property is opted to tax.

If you are VAT-registered and let commercial property, check whether an option to tax is already in place. An option can be inherited with the property, and it changes the treatment of the rent.

Option to tax

Opting to tax makes the property taxable at 20%. You charge VAT on rent and can reclaim VAT on qualifying costs. The option is usually irrevocable and lasts 20 years, so take advice before using it.

Opting is most useful when the tenant is VAT-registered and can reclaim the VAT, and when there are significant VAT costs to recover, such as a refurbishment. It is much less attractive to a tenant who cannot reclaim, such as a small charity or a residential occupier.

Some supplies are exempt from the option, including certain residential and charity uses. The option to tax guide covers the exclusions and the notification to HMRC.

Building work

The first sale of a new build is zero-rated when the conditions are met. Converting a non-dwelling into a dwelling, or changing the number of dwellings, can use the 5% reduced rate. So can renovating a dwelling that has stood empty for at least two years. General repairs, maintenance and extensions are standard-rated at 20%.

Energy-saving materials are a moving target. Qualifying installations are at 0% until 31 March 2027, after which the rate returns to 5%. Check the date on the invoice and the current guidance before quoting.

VAT on property is separate from stamp duty. England and Northern Ireland use SDLT, Scotland uses LBTT and Wales uses LTT; those taxes do not change the VAT treatment. The VAT on property guide sets out the 0%, 5% and 20% boundaries.

Holiday lets

Holiday accommodation is standard-rated at 20% if you are VAT-registered. That covers short-term lets, serviced accommodation and platforms such as Airbnb. The income counts towards the £90,000 registration threshold because it is taxable turnover.

The furnished holiday lettings income tax and corporation tax regime was abolished from April 2025. That change affected income tax reliefs and capital gains treatment. It did not change the VAT treatment of holiday accommodation, which was already standard-rated.

On a platform booking, two VAT questions arise. Your letting is your supply, and the platform's commission is a separate supply to you. The platform charges VAT on its fee, and you can usually reclaim that VAT if your letting is taxable.

Do I need to register?

Only taxable turnover counts towards the £90,000 threshold. Residential rent is exempt, so it does not count. Holiday lets, opted commercial rent and most other property services do count.

A landlord with £120,000 of residential rent and no other business activity is not required to register. A landlord with £70,000 of residential rent and £30,000 of holiday lets has £30,000 of taxable turnover, which is below the threshold. The residential rent still goes in box 6 once registered for the holiday lets.

For a mixed portfolio, input tax usually has to be apportioned between taxable and exempt use. Read partial exemption before claiming all the VAT on a shared cost.

Common mistakes

  • Charging VAT on residential rent, which is exempt.
  • Reclaiming VAT on residential repairs without a taxable business activity.
  • Adding exempt residential rent to taxable turnover when testing the £90,000 threshold.
  • Opting to tax without checking the 20-year effect and the exclusions.
  • Assuming the April 2025 furnished holiday lettings change altered the VAT rate.
  • Treating SDLT, LBTT or LTT as if they were VAT.

Related terms

Exempt supply, Option to tax, Zero-rated.

Questions

Do landlords pay VAT on rental income?
No on residential rent, which is exempt. Commercial rent is exempt unless the landlord opts to tax the property.
Can I reclaim VAT on repairs to a rented house?
Usually no. Residential letting is exempt, so VAT on repairs and other costs linked to it is generally blocked.
What does opting to tax mean?
Opting to tax makes a commercial property taxable at 20%, so you charge VAT on rent and can reclaim VAT on qualifying costs. The option usually lasts 20 years.
Is VAT charged on a new build?
No. The first sale of a new build is zero-rated when the conditions are met.
Do I charge VAT on Airbnb?
If you are VAT-registered, yes: holiday accommodation is standard-rated at 20% and the rental income counts towards the £90,000 threshold. Below the threshold you do not charge VAT on the letting, but the platform still charges VAT on its own fees.
