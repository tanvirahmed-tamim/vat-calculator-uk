VAT on Imports and Exports: Duty, PVA and Evidence

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of International and cross-border VAT

Imports and exports split the VAT question in two: goods leaving the UK are usually zero-rated, while goods entering the UK attract import VAT that you may be able to recover.

In short

Exports of goods are zero-rated when you keep evidence and meet the time limits. Imports attract import VAT, which a VAT-registered business can declare and recover on the same return through postponed VAT accounting.

Who this is for: Importers, exporters, online sellers and businesses that buy or sell goods across the UK border.

Exports

Goods exported from the UK are zero-rated, provided you meet the conditions and keep evidence that they left the UK. Zero-rated is a taxable supply: the sale still goes on your VAT return, but the VAT charged is £0.

A direct export is one you arrange yourself, using your own transport or a carrier you appoint. An indirect export is one where an overseas customer collects the goods or arranges their export. Both can be zero-rated, but HMRC expects stronger evidence for an indirect export because the transport is controlled by the buyer.

If your customer is established in the UK, the supply cannot be zero-rated as an export, even if the customer sends the goods abroad afterwards. The exception is where you arrange a direct export yourself.

Services are different. Most B2B services to overseas customers are outside the scope of UK VAT, not zero-rated, and the customer accounts for tax under the reverse charge.

Time limits and evidence

For most direct and indirect exports, the goods must leave the UK and you must obtain valid evidence within 3 months of the time of supply. Processing or incorporation before export allows 6 months. If you miss the limit, the supply cannot be zero-rated and VAT is due at the appropriate UK rate.

You need evidence of the supply and evidence of the export. Official evidence is the customs export declaration with its Movement Reference Number. Commercial evidence includes a bill of lading, an air waybill, a CMR consignment note or a certificate of shipment.

The evidence must identify the supplier, the customer, the goods and quantities, the value, the destination, and the route and mode of transport. Vague or contradictory descriptions are not accepted. Keep the records for 6 years.

Imports

Goods imported into the UK attract import VAT. Customs duty may also apply, and it is calculated separately from VAT. You need an EORI number and a customs declaration, and the goods must be classified under the correct commodity code.

A VAT-registered business can usually recover the import VAT if the goods are for business use and the evidence is kept. Since 1 January 2021, goods from the EU follow the same import rules as goods from the rest of the world.

Postponed VAT accounting

Postponed VAT accounting lets a VAT-registered importer declare and recover import VAT on the same VAT return instead of paying it at the border. Include the VAT due in box 1, the recoverable amount in box 4, and the value of the imports, excluding VAT, in box 7.

The monthly postponed import VAT statement supports the figures. The normal input tax rules still apply, so you cannot recover more than the VAT that is properly due on business imports. Postponed accounting helps cash flow; it is not a relief.

Postponed VAT accounting does not apply to consignments valued at £135 or less, which follow the point-of-sale rules below, or to some special procedures such as merchandise in baggage.

Low value imports

A consignment of goods located outside the UK at the point of sale, valued at £135 or less, is taxed at the point of sale rather than at the border. The test is the intrinsic value of the whole consignment, not the price of each item, and it excludes separately shown transport and insurance.

Where an online marketplace facilitates the sale, the marketplace is the deemed supplier and accounts for the VAT. A direct overseas seller with no marketplace must register and account for UK VAT itself. A UK VAT-registered business customer can account for the VAT by reverse charge if it gives a valid VAT number.

The threshold is not an exemption. Consignments containing excise goods, gifts between private individuals and consignments from Jersey and Guernsey covered by the Import VAT Accounting Scheme are outside these rules.

Customs duty

Customs duty is separate from VAT. It depends on the commodity code, the origin of the goods and their customs value, and it is collected under the UK Global Tariff. Import VAT is charged on the customs value plus any duty and other charges due.

The Incoterms in the sales contract decide who arranges freight and insurance and who acts as importer. Under DDP terms the seller handles the import; under ex-works terms the buyer does. The importer is the person who must account for import VAT, so agree the terms before the goods move.

Records

Keep the export declaration, commercial invoice, shipping documents and customs entries for exports. For imports, keep the customs declaration and either the import VAT certificate (C79) or the postponed import VAT statement.

HMRC can ask for these records years later. Without export evidence, a zero-rated sale can be reassessed as standard-rated; without import evidence, the input tax claim can be refused.

Common mistakes

  • Zero-rating an export without proof that the goods left the UK within 3 months.
  • Treating the £135 threshold as a general VAT exemption.
  • Recovering import VAT without the customs declaration or PVA statement.
  • Confusing customs duty with import VAT.
  • Assuming an indirect export needs no more evidence than a direct export.

Related terms

Export, Import VAT, Postponed VAT accounting.

Questions

Do I charge VAT on exports?
No. Exports of goods are zero-rated when you keep evidence that the goods left the UK within the time limit. The sale still goes on your VAT return.
How long do I have to export the goods?
Normally 3 months from the time of supply, and you must obtain the evidence within the same period. Goods that are processed or incorporated before export allow 6 months.
Can I reclaim import VAT?
Yes, if you are VAT-registered, the goods are for business use, and you hold the import evidence. Postponed VAT accounting puts the import VAT and the recovery on the same return.
What is postponed VAT accounting?
A way for a VAT-registered importer to declare import VAT in box 1 and recover it in box 4 of the same VAT return, instead of paying it at the border. The value goes in box 7.
What is the £135 threshold?
The £135 threshold applies to the intrinsic value of a consignment of goods located outside the UK at the point of sale. It moves the VAT to the point of sale but is not an exemption.
Is customs duty the same as VAT?
No. Customs duty is a separate charge based on the commodity code, origin and customs value. Import VAT is calculated separately and can be recovered by a VAT-registered business.
What evidence do I need for a zero-rated export?
Keep evidence of the supply and official or commercial evidence of export, such as a customs declaration with an MRN, a bill of lading, an air waybill or a CMR note. It must identify the goods, value, destination and route.
