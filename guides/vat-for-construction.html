VAT for Construction: Domestic Reverse Charge Explained

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Construction has more rate boundaries than most sectors. The same site can involve zero-rated new build work, 5% conversions and 20% repairs, and the domestic reverse charge can move the VAT to the customer.

In short

Most construction work is standard-rated at 20%, but the domestic reverse charge moves the VAT to the customer for specified CIS supplies. New builds can be zero-rated, some renovations use 5%, and repairs stay at 20%.

Who this is for: Builders, contractors, subcontractors, property developers and anyone supplying construction services to VAT-registered CIS customers.

Domestic reverse charge

The domestic reverse charge applies to specified construction and building services supplied between VAT-registered businesses where the customer is registered for CIS. The supplier invoices without VAT and notes the reverse charge; the customer accounts for the deemed VAT on its return.

It applies from 1 March 2021 to standard and reduced rate services reported within CIS. It does not apply to zero-rated work, to consumers, or to customers who are not registered for CIS. It also does not apply where the customer has given you written confirmation that they are an end user or an intermediary supplier.

The reverse charge changes who accounts for the VAT, not the rate. The underlying supply is still standard-rated or reduced-rated, and the customer normally reclaims the deemed VAT as input tax on the same return.

The reverse charge calculator works out the deemed VAT and the invoice wording.

What counts as construction

The services covered are the same as CIS construction operations, with one exception: workers supplied by an employment business are outside the charge.

0%, 5% or 20%

Rate and reverse charge are separate questions. A zero-rated new build is outside the reverse charge because the charge applies only to standard and reduced rate supplies.

Materials you supply and incorporate follow the rate of the work. Materials supplied on their own, without installation, are usually standard-rated. Mixed contracts may need a fair and reasonable apportionment between qualifying and non-qualifying work.

Invoicing

A reverse charge invoice must show the usual invoice details plus the net amount, the deemed VAT, and a reference to the reverse charge. Do not add the deemed VAT to the total VAT charged.

HMRC accepts wording such as:

  • reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies
  • reverse charge: S55A VATA 94 applies
  • reverse charge: Customer to pay the VAT to HMRC

On the return, the supplier includes the net value in box 6 and no output tax in box 1. The customer includes the deemed VAT in box 1, reclaims the same amount in box 4, and puts the net purchase in box 7. The cash never changes hands, but both sides must record it.

For normal supplies, show the net amount, the rate and the VAT separately. Keep the invoice wording consistent across your accounting software so the boxes populate correctly.

CIS and subcontractors

CIS deductions are separate from VAT. The reverse charge affects who accounts for VAT; CIS affects tax deducted from subcontractor payments. A subcontractor can be caught by both, so check CIS registration before deciding whether the reverse charge applies.

For example, a subcontractor invoices a CIS-registered contractor £10,000 for standard-rated work. The invoice shows £10,000 plus £2,000 deemed VAT, but the contractor pays £10,000. The contractor accounts for £2,000 in box 1 and usually reclaims £2,000 in box 4. CIS is then deducted separately from the labour element under the CIS rules, not from the VAT.

A customer who is the final user of the building can give you written confirmation that they are an end user. A business that buys the work and supplies it on to its own customer can confirm that it is an intermediary supplier. In either case the reverse charge does not apply and you charge VAT normally. Keep the written confirmation with your records.

Common mistakes

  • Charging 20% when the domestic reverse charge applies.
  • Applying 5% to ordinary repairs or improvements.
  • Assuming all work on a new build is zero-rated.
  • Confusing CIS deductions with VAT.

Questions

Does the domestic reverse charge apply to all construction work?
No. It applies to specified construction and building services supplied between VAT-registered businesses where the customer is registered for CIS.
Do I charge VAT on a new build?
No. The first sale of a new dwelling can be zero-rated when the conditions are met.
Is a kitchen fit-out 5% or 20%?
Usually 20%. The 5% rate applies to specific renovations and conversions, not to general improvements or repairs.
What should a reverse charge invoice say?
Show the net amount, the deemed VAT and a reference such as "reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies". Do not add the deemed VAT to the total VAT charged.
What is an end user declaration?
A written confirmation from a CIS-registered customer that they are the final user of the building, so the reverse charge should not be applied. Keep it with your records and charge VAT normally.
How is the reverse charge shown on a VAT return?
The supplier puts the net value in box 6 and no output tax in box 1. The customer puts the deemed VAT in box 1, reclaims it in box 4 and records the net purchase in box 7.
Does CIS replace VAT?
No. CIS is a separate scheme for tax on subcontractor payments; VAT still applies under the normal rules or the reverse charge.
