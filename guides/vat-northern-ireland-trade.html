VAT and the Windsor Framework: NI Trade Rules

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of International and cross-border VAT

Northern Ireland sits inside the UK VAT system but shares a land border with the EU. The Windsor Framework manages the movement of goods, and XI VAT numbers identify Northern Ireland businesses when they trade with the EU.

In short

VAT rates in Northern Ireland are the same as the rest of the UK, and businesses use the same UK VAT return. Goods sold between Great Britain and Northern Ireland normally carry UK VAT on the invoice, while goods moving between Northern Ireland and the EU follow dispatch and acquisition rules.

Who this is for: Northern Ireland businesses, GB businesses selling into Northern Ireland, and traders moving goods across the Irish border.

What the Framework does

Northern Ireland is part of the UK VAT system. The rate, the registration threshold and the VAT return are the same as in England, Scotland and Wales, and a Northern Ireland business does not need a second registration.

The Windsor Framework adds one layer. Northern Ireland aligns with EU VAT rules for goods, so movements between Northern Ireland and the EU are dispatches and acquisitions rather than exports and imports. UK rules for services apply across the whole of the UK. Sales between Great Britain and Northern Ireland are normally UK supplies with VAT on the invoice.

XI VAT numbers

An XI VAT number is the same VAT registration number with XI in place of GB. It identifies a Northern Ireland business as trading under the Windsor Framework. It is not a separate registration and it does not change the rate of VAT charged.

A business can tell HMRC it is eligible if any of these apply: the goods are in Northern Ireland at the time of sale, it receives goods in Northern Ireland from VAT-registered EU businesses for business purposes, or it sells or moves goods from Northern Ireland to an EU country.

Once HMRC confirms the position, the business uses XI before its usual number on invoices and other documents sent to EU customers and suppliers. Dispatches of goods to VAT-registered EU customers must also be reported on an EC Sales List. XI is not used for trade between Great Britain and Northern Ireland, or for services.

Great Britain to Northern Ireland

For goods sold from Great Britain to Northern Ireland, the seller charges VAT and shows it on the invoice. The sale is accounted for as output tax on the UK return, and a VAT-registered Northern Ireland customer recovers it as input tax under the normal rules. VAT is not normally collected at the border on these sales.

A business that moves its own goods, rather than selling them, accounts for VAT on the movement as output tax. It can reclaim the same amount as input tax when the goods are used for taxable supplies, with an adjustment where the business is partly exempt.

There are exceptions. VAT is accounted for differently where goods enter a special customs procedure, where the domestic reverse charge applies to gold or to gas and electricity, where the onward supply procedure is used, or where an overseas seller sells to a consumer through an online marketplace.

Customs duty is a separate question from VAT. The UK Internal Market Scheme (UKIMS) lets an authorised business declare goods "not at risk" of moving on to the EU, which affects the duty charged, not the VAT rate. A UKIMS movement uses an EORI number and a simplified Internal Market Movement Information declaration instead of a full customs declaration, and the supporting evidence must be kept for 5 years.

Northern Ireland to Great Britain

Goods sold from Northern Ireland to Great Britain are a normal UK supply. The seller charges UK VAT on the invoice and does not zero-rate the sale as an export, because the goods have not left the UK for VAT purposes.

A business moving its own goods from Northern Ireland to Great Britain does not account for VAT unless the goods have been sold or supplied. No import VAT arises on the movement.

The VAT Retail Export Scheme continues to operate in Northern Ireland, so a retailer can refund VAT to a visitor who takes goods away in personal luggage. Where those goods are brought into Great Britain under a claim, import VAT is due and the retailer accounts for it on the same return as the refund. The two amounts are equal, so no refund is actually paid out.

Northern Ireland to the EU

A supply of goods from Northern Ireland to a VAT-registered EU business is zero-rated when the customer has given a valid EU VAT number, the goods are removed to an EU member state, and the supply is included on an EC Sales List. The value goes in boxes 6 and 8 of the VAT return, and the customer accounts for acquisition VAT in the destination country.

Evidence of removal must be obtained within 3 months of the time of supply, or 6 months where the goods are processed or incorporated before removal. If the conditions are not met, UK VAT is due at the appropriate rate.

Distance sales to EU consumers are different. A Northern Ireland business charges UK VAT until its EU-wide distance sales exceed £8,818 (€10,000) in a calendar year. Above that threshold it registers for VAT in each member state where it sells, or uses the UK's OSS Union scheme to report the VAT through one return. Excise goods require a registration in the destination country from the first sale.

Removal to the Republic of Ireland needs particular care because there is no ferry crossing to prove the goods left. Keep the customer's written order, the invoice with the EU VAT number, the carrier's consignment note or delivery note signed on receipt, and the vehicle details where the customer collects.

The EU to Northern Ireland

Goods received in Northern Ireland from a VAT-registered EU supplier are an acquisition. The Northern Ireland business accounts for acquisition VAT at the UK rate in box 2, recovers it in box 4 under the normal rules, and shows the value in boxes 7 and 9.

The time of acquisition is normally the earlier of the 15th day of the month after the goods arrive or the date the supplier issued the invoice. Convert a foreign currency price using the HMRC period rate or the UK market selling rate at the time of supply.

A business that is not VAT-registered pays VAT at the supplier's local EU rate. Buying £90,000 or more of goods from EU countries in a year can create a UK registration obligation. Acquisitions above the Intrastat threshold also need a supplementary declaration.

Services

Services are outside the goods rules in the Framework. A Northern Ireland business applies the same place-of-supply rules as the rest of the UK and does not use an XI number for a service.

Most B2B services to an EU business are outside the scope of UK VAT, and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge. Most B2C services follow the supplier's location, with exceptions for professional, technical and intangible services and for digital services, which are taxed where the consumer is located.

Records

Keep VAT records for 6 years. For EU dispatches that means the customer's EU VAT number, the invoice, the EC Sales List entry and the evidence of removal. For acquisitions, keep the supplier's invoice and the figures behind boxes 2, 4, 7 and 9.

For GB to NI movements, keep the customs reference, the Internal Market Movement Information and the evidence behind a "not at risk" declaration for 5 years.

Common mistakes

  • Zero-rating a sale from Northern Ireland to Great Britain as an export.
  • Charging VAT at the border on goods sold to a VAT-registered Northern Ireland customer.
  • Using an XI number on domestic GB–NI invoices or on service invoices.
  • Treating UKIMS authorisation as a change to the VAT rate rather than to the duty position.
  • Missing the 3-month evidence window for a zero-rated dispatch to the EU.
  • Forgetting box 2 when goods arrive in Northern Ireland from an EU supplier.

Related terms

Windsor Framework, XI VAT number, Acquisition VAT, Postponed VAT accounting.

Questions

Does the Windsor Framework change VAT rates?
No. VAT rates in Northern Ireland are the same as the rest of the UK. The Framework changes how goods movements are treated, not the rate charged on a sale.
What is an XI VAT number?
An XI VAT number is a UK VAT number with XI in place of GB. It identifies a Northern Ireland business trading under the Windsor Framework and is used on documents sent to EU customers and suppliers, not for trade with Great Britain.
Do I charge VAT on goods sold from Great Britain to Northern Ireland?
Yes. The seller charges UK VAT and shows it on the invoice, and a VAT-registered Northern Ireland customer recovers it as input tax. VAT is not normally collected at the border on these sales.
Do I charge VAT on goods sold from Northern Ireland to Great Britain?
Yes, at the normal UK rate. The sale is a domestic UK supply, so it is not zero-rated as an export even though the goods cross the Irish Sea.
What does 'not at risk' mean?
The phrase describes goods declared under UKIMS as staying in the UK, for sale to or final use by end consumers. It affects the duty charged and does not change the VAT rate.
When can I zero-rate a sale from Northern Ireland to the EU?
When the customer is VAT-registered in an EU member state and has given a valid VAT number, the goods are removed to the EU, and the supply is reported on an EC Sales List. Evidence of removal must be obtained within 3 months.
How do I report VAT on goods bought from the EU into Northern Ireland?
Account for acquisition VAT at the UK rate in box 2 of the VAT return, recover it in box 4 under the normal rules, and show the value in boxes 7 and 9.
Do services follow the Windsor Framework?
No. Services follow UK place-of-supply rules across the whole of the UK, and an XI number is not used for a service.
