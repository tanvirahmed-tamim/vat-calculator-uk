VAT on Discounts, Deposits and Advance Payments

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of VAT calculation

Discounts and deposits look similar on an invoice but behave differently for VAT. A discount reduces the consideration; a deposit can create a tax point before the goods or services are supplied.

In short

VAT is charged on the price the customer actually pays, after a discount. A deposit for goods is a payment on account and usually creates a tax point when paid. A refundable deposit is not consideration for a supply, so it carries no VAT.

Who this is for: Retailers, service businesses, builders, landlords and anyone who takes deposits or offers discounts.

Discounts

A trade discount, bulk discount or promotional reduction lowers the consideration for the supply. VAT is charged on the discounted net price, not on the list price.

A 10% discount on a £100 net sale leaves £90 net and £108 gross at 20%. The VAT is £18, not £20. If the discount is applied after VAT has been charged, the invoice needs a credit note and the output tax must be adjusted.

A discount given for early payment is treated differently. See the next section.

Prompt payment discounts

If you offer a discount for payment within a set period, you can calculate VAT on the discounted price, provided the invoice shows the terms. If the customer does not take the discount, you must account for VAT on the full amount.

In practice, many businesses issue the invoice at the full price and raise a credit note if the discount is taken. Either method can work, but the VAT return must reflect the price actually paid.

Deposits

A deposit for goods is a payment on account. It is consideration for the supply, so it usually creates a tax point when it is paid, even if the goods are delivered later. The VAT on the deposit is due in the period of payment.

A deposit for services follows the service tax point rules, which generally use the earlier of payment, invoice or performance. A retainer or advance payment can therefore create a tax point before the work is done.

A refundable deposit is different. A damage deposit or key deposit that is returned to the customer is not consideration for a supply, so it is outside the scope of VAT. If part of the deposit is kept for damage, that retained amount may become consideration for a repair or service, and VAT may apply at that point.

Vouchers

A gift card or face-value voucher is not usually a supply when it is sold, because it is a means of payment. VAT is accounted for when the voucher is redeemed for goods or services, at the rate that applies to those goods or services.

A voucher sold at a discount, or a voucher that can be redeemed for a range of items, needs a careful analysis. Keep the redemption records so the VAT can be linked to the supply.

Credit notes

When goods are returned or a discount is allowed after the invoice, issue a credit note. The credit note reduces the output tax in the period it is issued, not retrospectively in the original period.

If the customer has already recovered the VAT, the credit note should show the VAT adjustment so both sides can correct their records. The rounding and mixed invoices guide covers the invoice presentation.

Common mistakes

  • Charging VAT on the list price after a discount has been applied.
  • Treating a refundable damage deposit as taxable consideration.
  • Forgetting the tax point on a deposit paid before delivery.
  • Adjusting the original return instead of the period the credit note is issued.

Questions

Do I charge VAT before or after a discount?
After the discount. Apply the discount to the net price first, then add VAT to the discounted amount, because VAT is charged on the price the customer actually pays.
Is VAT due on a deposit?
Usually yes for goods, because a deposit is a payment on account and creates a tax point when paid. A refundable damage deposit is not consideration and carries no VAT.
How do I treat a prompt payment discount?
You can calculate VAT on the discounted price if the invoice shows the terms. If the customer does not take the discount, account for VAT on the full amount.
Is VAT charged when a gift card is sold?
Usually not. VAT is accounted for when the card is redeemed for goods or services, at the rate that applies to those goods or services.
What is bad debt relief?
A claim to recover VAT on an unpaid invoice after six months, subject to conditions. It is not the same as a credit note.
Can I adjust VAT in the original period?
No, once the return has been submitted. A credit note adjusts output tax in the period it is issued.
