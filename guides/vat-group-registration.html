VAT Group Registration: How VAT Groups Work in the UK

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

A VAT group treats several companies as one taxable person. That simplifies VAT on intra-group supplies, but it also means the group shares one registration, one return and one partial exemption calculation.

In short

Companies under common control can apply for VAT group registration. The group has a representative member that files the return, supplies between members are disregarded, and the group is treated as a single taxable person.

Who this is for: Groups of companies, holding companies and subsidiaries that want to simplify VAT or avoid VAT on intra-group charges.

What a VAT group is

A VAT group is two or more eligible bodies corporate that HMRC treats as a single taxable person for VAT. The group has one VAT registration number and files one VAT return covering all members.

Group registration is optional. It is used most often by groups that make significant intra-group supplies, because those supplies can be disregarded for VAT, or by groups that want to share a single registration and reduce administration.

Common control

The members must be under common control. In practice this means one company controls the others, or the same person or group controls them, usually through more than 50% of the voting rights or shares.

Each member must also be eligible: generally a body corporate carrying on a business and either already VAT-registered or liable to be registered. A partnership or a sole trader cannot join a VAT group in the same way as a company.

Representative member

One member is appointed as the representative member. It files the group return, pays or reclaims the VAT, and corresponds with HMRC on behalf of the group. The other members are still bound by the group's obligations.

The representative member can change, but HMRC must be told. The group registration application should identify the members, the representative and the effective date.

Intra-group supplies

Supplies between members of the same VAT group are generally disregarded for VAT. That removes the need to charge VAT on management fees, recharges and internal services within the group.

The exception is supplies that would be outside the scope if made by a stand-alone business, and certain supplies involving persons outside the group. Do not assume every intercompany charge disappears; check the nature of the supply first.

Practical effects

Group registration affects partial exemption. The group is treated as one business, so the de minimis test and the annual adjustment are applied to the group as a whole, not member by member.

It also affects the registration threshold and the option to tax. A group can hold one option covering property held by its members, and the group's turnover is aggregated for registration purposes. Take advice before joining or leaving a group, because the changes can trigger adjustments.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming every intercompany charge is automatically outside the scope.
  • Applying partial exemption member by member instead of to the group.
  • Changing the representative member without telling HMRC.
  • Joining a group without checking the effect on the option to tax.

Questions

What is VAT group registration?
VAT group registration treats two or more eligible companies under common control as a single taxable person, with one VAT number and one return.
What is the common control test for a VAT group?
The members must be under common control, usually through more than 50% of the voting rights or shares held by the same person or group.
Do intra-group supplies carry VAT?
Generally no. Supplies between members of the same VAT group are disregarded, although there are exceptions for certain supplies.
Who files the VAT group return?
The representative member files the return and pays or reclaims the VAT on behalf of the whole group.
Does group registration affect partial exemption?
Yes. The group is treated as one business, so the de minimis test and annual adjustment apply to the group as a whole.
Can a sole trader join a VAT group?
No. VAT group members are normally bodies corporate. A sole trader or partnership cannot join in the same way as a company.
