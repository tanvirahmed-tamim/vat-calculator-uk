VAT on Property: New Builds, Conversions and Option to Tax

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

Property has three VAT rates and a lot of conditions. New builds can be zero-rated, some conversions and renovations use 5%, and repairs and most commercial work are standard-rated at 20%.

In short

The first sale of a qualifying new build is zero-rated. Converting a non-dwelling into a dwelling, or changing the number of dwellings, can use 5%, as can renovating a long-term empty home. Repairs and general work are 20%.

Who this is for: Property developers, builders, landlords, investors and anyone buying, selling or refurbishing property.

New builds

The first sale of a new qualifying dwelling is zero-rated, along with the construction services and materials that go into it. The relief is subject to conditions: the building must be a dwelling, it must be new, and the sale must be the first sale of the freehold or a long lease.

A new build that is not a dwelling, such as a commercial building, does not get the same zero rate. The zero rate is specific, and HMRC expects evidence that the conditions are met.

The 5% rate

The reduced rate applies to specific property works:

  • Converting a non-dwelling into a dwelling.
  • Changing the number of dwellings in a building.
  • Renovating a dwelling that has been empty for the required period.
  • Installing certain energy-saving materials, where the temporary zero rate does not apply.

The 5% rate is not a general rate for improvements. A kitchen refit, a loft conversion that does not change the number of dwellings, and ordinary repairs are standard-rated. The conditions are in the VAT notices, and the burden of proof is on the business charging the reduced rate.

Repairs and maintenance

Repairs, maintenance, extensions and general improvements are standard-rated at 20%. That includes roofing, plumbing, painting, electrical work and the materials used in those jobs.

A builder working on a new build can zero-rate the qualifying work, but a later repair to the same building is standard-rated. Keep the contract and the invoices separate so the two treatments do not get mixed.

Option to tax

Commercial rent is exempt by default. A landlord can opt to tax the property, which makes the rent and most sales standard-rated at 20% and allows VAT recovery on qualifying costs.

The option usually lasts 20 years and must be notified to HMRC within 30 days. It can transfer with the property on a sale. Residential property and certain charity uses are excluded. The option to tax guide covers the detail.

Land and mixed use

Sales of bare land are generally exempt, unless the land is opted to tax or the sale is part of a taxable business. A mixed-use building needs apportionment between the residential and commercial parts, and the option may apply to only part of the property.

Stamp duty is separate. SDLT, LBTT and LTT apply to the property transaction, while VAT applies to the supply. The two are calculated on different bases and should be shown separately on the completion statement.

Common mistakes

  • Zero-rating a new build without checking the qualifying conditions.
  • Applying 5% to ordinary repairs or improvements.
  • Ignoring the option to tax when buying a commercial property.
  • Treating SDLT, LBTT or LTT as if it were VAT.

Questions

Is VAT charged on a new build?
No. The first sale of a qualifying new dwelling is zero-rated, provided the conditions are met. Later repairs and improvements are standard-rated.
When does the 5% rate apply to property?
The 5% rate applies to specific works: converting a non-dwelling into a dwelling, changing the number of dwellings, renovating a long-term empty home, and certain energy-saving installations.
Are property repairs standard-rated?
Yes, repairs, maintenance and general improvements are standard-rated at 20%. The 5% rate is not a general rate for building work.
Do I charge VAT on commercial rent?
Commercial rent is exempt unless the landlord opts to tax the property. After opting, VAT is charged at 20% and input tax may be recoverable.
Does VAT apply to the sale of land?
Usually not, because land is exempt. It can become taxable if the land is opted to tax or the sale is part of a taxable business.
Is stamp duty the same as VAT?
No. SDLT, LBTT and LTT are property transaction taxes. VAT is a separate tax on the supply and is calculated on a different basis.
