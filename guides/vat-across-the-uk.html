VAT Across the UK: England, Scotland, Wales and NI

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 4 min read

One VAT system covers all four nations. What differs is the property transaction tax, the body that sets business rates, and the goods rules for Northern Ireland.

In short

VAT rates, thresholds and scheme rules are identical in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales set their own property and income taxes, and Northern Ireland has special goods rules under the Windsor Framework. None of those is VAT.

What is the same

VAT is reserved to the UK Parliament and administered by HMRC. The standard rate (20%), reduced rate (5%), zero rate (0%), registration threshold (£90,000), deregistration threshold (£88,000) and scheme rules apply in all four nations.

A business registered in one nation can trade in the others under the same VAT number. Crossing a UK internal border does not create a second VAT registration.

Business rates are set and collected differently in each nation, and the valuation body differs: the Valuation Office Agency in England and Wales, the Scottish Assessors in Scotland, and Land & Property Services in Northern Ireland. None of that changes the VAT on the rates bill, which is not a VATable supply.

A property purchase can involve VAT on professional fees, SDLT or LBTT or LTT on the purchase, and business rates on the property. Only one of those is VAT.

Northern Ireland

VAT rates are the same, but Northern Ireland has special arrangements for goods under the Windsor Framework. Some movements of goods between Great Britain and Northern Ireland are treated differently from wholly domestic supplies.

Businesses may hold an XI VAT number for certain movements of goods. The XI prefix identifies a Northern Ireland registration for VAT purposes involving goods. It does not mean a different VAT rate.

One current difference is domestic electricity. Qualifying supplies in Great Britain are temporarily zero-rated from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027; in Northern Ireland they remain at 5%. Read VAT on energy.

Cross-border trade

Within the UK, moving goods between England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland does not create an import or export for VAT in the ordinary sense. The Northern Ireland goods rules are the main exception.

For sales to or from other countries, the place of supply rules decide whether UK VAT applies. Exports are generally zero-rated, imports may attract import VAT, and cross-border services follow their own rules.

For goods movements between Great Britain and Northern Ireland, read VAT and Northern Ireland trade.

Related terms

Windsor framework, Devolved tax.

Questions

Are VAT rates the same in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?
Yes. VAT is a UK-wide tax set by the UK Parliament, so the rates and registration threshold are the same in all four nations. Devolved taxes such as Scottish income tax and Welsh land transaction tax are separate.
Does Northern Ireland follow different VAT rules?
For most supplies, Northern Ireland follows the same VAT rules as the rest of the UK. Goods movements under the Windsor Framework have specific rules, particularly for goods at risk of entering the EU.
Do Scottish VAT rules differ for land and property?
VAT on land and property follows UK-wide rules. Scotland's Land and Buildings Transaction Tax replaces stamp duty but has no effect on VAT treatment.
