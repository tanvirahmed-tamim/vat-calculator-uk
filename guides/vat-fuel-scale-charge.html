VAT Fuel Scale Charge 2026: Rates and How It Works

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Reclaiming VAT

If you reclaim VAT on fuel for a car that can be used privately, you must pay back a fixed output tax charge. The fuel scale charge is simpler than tracking every private mile, but it is not optional once you claim the fuel VAT.

In short

The fuel scale charge is a CO2-based output tax charge for businesses that reclaim VAT on fuel used in a car available for private use. The table is updated each year. The alternative is to reclaim no VAT on fuel.

Who this is for: Businesses that reclaim VAT on fuel for cars, including sole traders, companies and partnerships.

When it applies

The fuel scale charge applies when a VAT-registered business reclaims VAT on fuel used in a car that is available for private use. "Available for private use" is the test, not whether the private use actually happened.

If the car is used exclusively for business, with no private use at all, the charge does not apply and the full VAT on fuel can be recovered. If the car is used only privately, there is no business fuel VAT to reclaim.

The charge is for cars. Vans and commercial vehicles are treated differently, and the car scale charge does not apply to them.

How it is calculated

The charge is a fixed amount of output tax based on the car's CO2 emissions. It is not based on the actual fuel used, so you do not need to record private mileage for fuel.

HMRC publishes a table for each year, usually running from 1 May to 30 April. Find the CO2 band for the car, then use the corresponding figure for your VAT period. The table includes VAT, so the amount is the output tax to declare.

If the car has no CO2 figure, use the default band that HMRC specifies for cars without a CO2 emissions figure. Do not guess the band from engine size.

The VAT return

Include the scale charge in box 1 (VAT due on sales and other outputs). You cannot reclaim it as input tax. The fuel VAT you originally reclaimed stays in box 4, subject to the normal rules.

The scale charge is due for each VAT period in which the car is available for private use and fuel VAT is reclaimed. If the car is sold or no longer used, the charge stops from the relevant period.

The alternative

You can choose not to reclaim VAT on fuel and keep private and business fuel separate. That avoids the scale charge but requires records of business fuel and a clear method for the private element.

Many businesses use the scale charge because it is simpler and gives certainty. Model both options if fuel costs are high or the car has low private use.

Records

Keep the fuel invoices, the car's CO2 figure, the scale charge table used and the calculation for each period. If HMRC checks the return, the CO2 band and the table are the first things they will ask for.

Common mistakes

  • Reclaiming fuel VAT without accounting for the scale charge.
  • Using last year's table after the annual update.
  • Applying the car scale charge to a van.
  • Guessing the CO2 band for a car without a figure.

Questions

When do I have to use the fuel scale charge?
When you reclaim VAT on fuel for a car that is available for private use. The test is availability, not whether private use actually happened.
How is the fuel scale charge calculated?
The fuel scale charge is a fixed output tax amount based on the car's CO2 emissions, taken from HMRC's table for the period. It is not based on actual fuel used.
Can I avoid the fuel scale charge?
Yes, by not reclaiming VAT on fuel and keeping private and business fuel separate. That avoids the charge but requires records of business fuel.
Does the fuel scale charge apply to vans?
No. The car fuel scale charge does not apply to vans or commercial vehicles. Private use of a van is handled differently.
Where does the scale charge go on the VAT return?
In box 1, as output tax. It cannot be reclaimed as input tax.
What if my car has no CO2 figure?
Use the default band that HMRC specifies for cars without a CO2 emissions figure. Do not estimate from engine size.
