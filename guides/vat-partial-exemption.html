VAT Partial Exemption: Recovering VAT on Exempt Supplies

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

If you make both taxable and exempt supplies, you cannot simply reclaim all the VAT on your costs. Partial exemption is the set of rules that works out how much input tax relates to taxable activity.

In short

Partial exemption applies when a business makes both taxable and exempt supplies. VAT directly linked to taxable supplies is recoverable, VAT linked to exempt supplies is not, and residual VAT is apportioned. Small amounts of exempt input tax are de minimis.

Who this is for: Businesses with a mix of taxable and exempt income, including financial services, landlords, healthcare providers, education bodies and charities.

Who is partly exempt

A business is partly exempt if it makes, or intends to make, both taxable and exempt supplies and incurs VAT on costs that relate to both. Typical examples are a property business letting exempt residential property alongside taxable commercial property, or a financial adviser with exempt and standard-rated income.

Non-business activity is a separate issue. VAT on costs used only for non-business purposes is not input tax at all, so it must be excluded before the partial exemption calculation begins.

The three steps

The calculation has three stages. First, attribute input tax directly to taxable supplies or to exempt supplies. Second, identify residual input tax, which is VAT on costs used for both or for the business as a whole. Third, apportion the residual input tax using a fair method.

VAT directly attributable to taxable supplies is recoverable in full. VAT directly attributable to exempt supplies is not recoverable, unless the de minimis rule applies. Residual VAT is recovered in the same proportion as taxable activity.

The standard method

The standard method uses the value of supplies:

taxable supplies ÷ total supplies × 100 = recoverable percentage

Apply that percentage to the residual input tax. The result is rounded up to the next whole number for most businesses, unless residual input tax is very large, in which case it is rounded to two decimal places.

Some values are excluded from the calculation because they distort it, including capital goods, incidental financial transactions and self-supplies. A business whose circumstances do not fit the standard method can apply to HMRC for a special method.

De minimis

If the exempt input tax is small, you can treat it as taxable and recover it in full. The test is that exempt input tax is no more than £625 a month on average and no more than half of your total input tax.

Both conditions must be met. A business with £800 a month of exempt input tax cannot use de minimis even if that is only 10% of its total input tax. The test is applied to each VAT period and again over the year.

Annual adjustment

The provisional recoveries made during the year are finalised by an annual adjustment at the end of the partial exemption year. If you recovered too much, you repay the difference. If you recovered too little, you claim it.

Keep the calculation, the method and the underlying figures. HMRC can ask for them during an inspection, and a special method cannot be changed without approval. The record keeping guide covers the evidence.

Common mistakes

  • Reclaiming all VAT without checking for exempt income.
  • Applying the standard method without excluding capital goods and incidental supplies.
  • Treating non-business VAT as part of the partial exemption calculation.
  • Forgetting the annual adjustment at the end of the year.

Related terms

Partial exemption, De minimis, Residual input tax, Exempt supply.

Questions

What is partial exemption?
Partial exemption is the set of rules for recovering VAT when a business makes both taxable and exempt supplies. Only the VAT linked to taxable activity is recoverable in principle.
What is the de minimis limit for VAT?
Exempt input tax is de minimis if it is no more than £625 a month on average and no more than 50% of your total input tax.
Can I reclaim all my VAT if I make exempt supplies?
Only if the exempt input tax is within the de minimis limits. Otherwise you must apportion the VAT and recover only the taxable share.
What is residual input tax?
VAT on costs used for both taxable and exempt supplies, or for the business as a whole. It is apportioned using the standard method or an approved special method.
Do I need HMRC approval for the standard method?
No. The standard method can be used without approval, provided it gives a fair and reasonable result. A special method needs written approval.
When is the annual adjustment made?
At the end of your partial exemption year, usually the tax year. It corrects the provisional recoveries made during the year.
