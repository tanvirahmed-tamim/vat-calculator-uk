How VAT is calculated in the UK

Maintained by the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 26 November 2026 · 10 min read

Every UK VAT calculation runs in one of two directions: forward from a net price, or backward from a gross price. This collection covers both, the fractions that avoid the classic 20% error, and the specialist methods for margin scheme goods, the domestic reverse charge and business expenses.

In short

Add VAT by multiplying the net amount by 1 plus the rate as a decimal: at 20%, net × 1.20. Remove VAT by dividing the gross by the same factor, or by multiplying it by the VAT fraction: 1/6 at 20% and 1/21 at 5%. Round the VAT to the nearest penny, then add it to the net.

General information: This guide provides general UK VAT information based on GOV.UK and HMRC guidance, including VAT Notice 700, VAT Notice 709/1 and VAT Notice 700/24. It is not personal tax, legal or accounting advice. Check GOV.UK or ask a qualified UK accountant about your specific circumstances.

The two directions

Adding VAT is a single multiplication from the net price. Removing VAT is a single division from the gross price. Both use the same factor, 1 plus the rate as a decimal, so the two calculations are exact opposites.

Gross = Net × (1 + rate ÷ 100)

Net = Gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)

At the 20% standard rate the factor is 1.20. At the 5% reduced rate it is 1.05. At a historical rate, such as the 17.5% that applied from 1991 to 2011 apart from the 2008-09 cut, it is 1.175.

How do you work out VAT on a price?

For calculating UK VAT, start from the figure you have. If you are quoting a net price, work forward: multiply by 1.20 at the standard rate. If you are checking a price that already includes VAT, work backward: divide by 1.20. This page shows how to calculate VAT UK-wide, from either direction, and working out VAT on a receipt is the same reverse step: the result is the net price plus the VAT that was already inside it.

The step-by-step guides show both directions with worked examples: how to add VAT and how to remove VAT. Use the same factor for both, so a figure does not drift when it is converted back and forth.

Why the VAT fraction exists

When you only need the VAT inside a gross amount, the VAT fraction does it in one step: rate ÷ (100 + rate). At 20% this is 20 ÷ 120 = 1/6, and at 5% it is 5 ÷ 105 = 1/21.

The fraction is the reason subtracting 20% from a gross price gives the wrong answer. 20% of the gross is a fifth, but VAT is only a sixth of the gross at the standard rate. On £120, the VAT is £20 and the net is £100, not the £96 you get by subtracting 20%.

The VAT fraction calculator applies the fraction to any gross amount and rate.

Rounding and line-level accuracy

HMRC expects normal rounding to the nearest penny, not truncation, but the rule differs by trader. Retailers must not round the VAT figure down, though they may round each calculation up or down. Businesses issuing VAT invoices to other businesses may use the Notice 700 paragraph 17.5 concession to round the total VAT down to a whole penny.

Calculate the VAT on the net amount, round it, then add it to the net so the two always reconcile. On a gross of £10.00 at 20%, the VAT is £1.67 and the net is £8.33.

On a mixed invoice you can calculate VAT per line or on the total for each rate. Both are used in practice, but stay consistent and keep a record of the method so a checker can follow it. The rounding guide works through a five-line comparison.

How Do You Calculate VAT on a Takeaway Order?

A takeaway order can mix rates in one basket. Hot food and hot drinks are standard-rated at 20%, while cold food that is zero-rated in its own right stays at 0%. Do not apply one rate to the whole order. Split the items, calculate the VAT on the standard-rated part, and add the two totals back together.

Take an order of £28.50: a hot takeaway meal at £24.00 and a cold sandwich at £4.50. The hot meal is standard-rated, so the £24.00 gross contains £4.00 of VAT and £20.00 of net sales. The cold sandwich is zero-rated, so it contains no VAT. The order totals £28.50 gross, £24.50 net and £4.00 VAT.

To check the VAT on the hot part, multiply the gross by the VAT fraction: £24.00 × 20/120 = £4.00. The food and drink guide explains which items are hot and which stay zero-rated, and the VAT fraction calculator does the arithmetic.

When the simple formula is not enough

Three situations need a different method. Under the margin scheme, VAT is charged on the dealer's margin rather than the full selling price. Under the domestic reverse charge, the customer accounts for the VAT instead of the supplier. And when reclaiming VAT on an expense, the input tax is the VAT already included in a gross amount.

Which rate applies

The arithmetic is only as good as the rate you feed it. Most supplies are standard rated at 20%, a defined list is reduced rated at 5%, and some supplies are zero rated at 0%. The rate is a legal question, not an arithmetic one, so check the VAT rates guide or the GOV.UK guidance before you rely on the number.

Questions

What is the VAT formula for adding VAT?
Multiply the net amount by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 20% multiply by 1.20, at 5% multiply by 1.05, and at 0% multiply by 1.00. The result is the gross amount.
What is the VAT formula for removing VAT?
Divide the gross amount by 1 plus the rate. At 20% divide by 1.20 to get the net, and take the VAT as the difference. Alternatively multiply the gross by the VAT fraction, which is 1/6 at 20%.
Why is the VAT fraction 1/6 at 20%?
The fraction is rate ÷ (100 + rate). At 20% that is 20 ÷ 120, which simplifies to 1/6. It tells you the VAT share of a VAT-inclusive amount.
Does the same method work for every rate?
Yes for ordinary supplies. Margin scheme goods, the domestic reverse charge and cross-border services use different rules, so use the specialist calculator for those.
How do I calculate VAT on a takeaway order?
Split the order by rate. Hot food and hot drinks are standard-rated at 20%, while cold zero-rated food carries no VAT, so calculate each part and add the VAT figures together.
