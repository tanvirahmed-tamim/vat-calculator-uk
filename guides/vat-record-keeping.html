VAT Record Keeping: What to Keep and for How Long

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

Good VAT records do two jobs: they support the return you filed and they answer the questions an inspector will ask years later. The legal minimum is six years, but the practical test is whether you can reconstruct any figure on any return.

In short

VAT-registered businesses must keep sales and purchase records, VAT invoices, the VAT account and import or export documents for six years. Making Tax Digital requires digital records and compatible software.

Who this is for: Every VAT-registered business, including sole traders, companies, charities and landlords.

What to keep

The core records are sales invoices, purchase invoices, credit notes, debit notes and the VAT account that summarises the figures on each return. You also need bank statements, contracts and any documents that explain an unusual entry.

Keep the records that support the rate you charged, the input tax you reclaimed and the place of supply for cross-border sales. For imports and exports, that means customs declarations, shipping documents and import VAT statements.

Digital records

Making Tax Digital requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file through compatible software. The digital record must include the data that goes into the return, not necessarily every underlying document.

You can keep paper invoices and scan them, provided the digital record is complete and the link between the record and the source document is clear. Spreadsheets can be used if the software supports the digital link to HMRC.

How long

Keep VAT records for six years from the end of the accounting period they relate to. HMRC can normally assess VAT for four years, and up to 20 years for deliberate errors, so six years is a minimum rather than a safe ceiling for disputed items.

If you use the margin scheme, the capital goods scheme or partial exemption, keep the calculations and the supporting records for the same period. These are the records most likely to be checked.

Evidence

A valid VAT invoice must show the supplier's name, address and VAT number, the customer's details, the date, the description of the supply, the net amount, the rate and the VAT. For a simplified invoice under £250, fewer details are required.

If you cannot produce the invoice, the input tax claim is at risk. A bank statement on its own is not proof of VAT. Keep the original invoice or a clear digital copy, and record why a missing document has been replaced.

Inspections

HMRC can ask to see your records and, in some cases, visit your premises. A well organised set of records by period, with a clear VAT account and a file for unusual items, makes an inspection shorter and less stressful.

If records are lost, tell HMRC and reconstruct them from bank statements, supplier statements and copies. The penalty risk is lower when you disclose the problem and rebuild the records than when you wait to be asked.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping only bank statements instead of VAT invoices.
  • Deleting digital records after the software changes.
  • Forgetting to keep import and export documents.
  • Discarding partial exemption calculations after the year end.

Questions

How long must I keep VAT records?
Normally six years from the end of the accounting period they relate to. Some records, such as those for deliberate errors, may need to be kept longer.
Do I need to keep paper invoices?
No, provided you keep a clear digital copy and the digital record is complete. The link between the record and the source document should be traceable.
What records does Making Tax Digital require?
Digital records of sales, purchases and the VAT account, kept in compatible software with a digital link to HMRC.
Can HMRC visit my premises?
Yes. HMRC can check records and, in some cases, visit premises as part of a VAT inspection. You can ask for the scope and for time to produce documents.
What if I lose my VAT records?
Tell HMRC and reconstruct them from bank statements, supplier statements and copies. Disclosing the problem early usually reduces the penalty risk.
Do I need to keep records for a nil return?
Yes. A nil return is still a return, and the records that support it should be kept for the normal six-year period.
