VAT for Motor Traders: Margin Scheme and Second-Hand Cars

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Motor traders deal with two VAT regimes at once: the margin scheme for eligible second-hand vehicles, and the normal rules for new or commercial vehicles, parts, servicing and fuel.

In short

Second-hand vehicles can use the margin scheme, so VAT is charged on the margin rather than the full price. Most cars are blocked for input tax unless used exclusively for business, and fuel has a scale charge.

Who this is for: Second-hand car dealers, vehicle traders, leasing companies, garages and businesses that buy or sell cars.

Margin scheme

The margin scheme lets a dealer account for VAT on the margin between the buying and selling price of eligible second-hand goods, including used vehicles. The margin is the selling price minus the purchase price, and VAT is calculated on that margin.

A margin scheme invoice normally does not show VAT separately. It must state that the margin scheme applies. The margin scheme calculator works out the VAT from the margin.

Cars and input tax

VAT on buying most cars is blocked, even for a VAT-registered business, because the car is assumed to be available for private use. There are exceptions, including cars used exclusively for business, taxis, driving school cars and some pool cars.

Commercial vehicles, vans and lorries are usually treated differently, and VAT is normally recoverable when the vehicle is for business use. Keep the evidence for the business use.

Fuel scale charge

If a business reclaims VAT on fuel used in a car that is available for private use, it must account for a fuel scale charge. The charge is a fixed amount of output tax based on the car's CO2 emissions, not on the actual fuel used.

The alternative is to reclaim no VAT on fuel and keep private and business fuel separate. Most businesses use the scale charge because it is simpler.

Leasing and Motability

When a car is leased and there is private use, 50% of the VAT on the lease can normally be recovered. For sales to eligible disabled customers, Motability and certain adapted vehicles can qualify for zero-rating.

Check the eligibility conditions before zero-rating a vehicle. The relief is specific and the evidence requirements are strict.

Records

Keep purchase invoices, sales invoices, margin scheme calculations and stock records for six years. For margin scheme vehicles, record the purchase price, the sale price and the margin for each vehicle.

Common mistakes

  • Charging VAT on the full selling price of a margin scheme vehicle.
  • Reclaiming VAT on a car that is available for private use.
  • Forgetting the fuel scale charge after reclaiming fuel VAT.
  • Zero-rating a Motability sale without the eligibility evidence.

Questions

Do I charge VAT on the full price of a used car?
Not under the margin scheme. VAT is charged on the margin between the purchase price and the selling price, and the invoice normally does not show VAT separately.
Can I reclaim VAT on a car I buy for my business?
Usually no, because most cars are assumed to be available for private use. There are exceptions for cars used exclusively for business, taxis and driving school cars.
What is the fuel scale charge?
A fixed amount of output tax a business accounts for when it reclaims VAT on fuel used in a car available for private use. It is based on CO2 emissions.
Can I reclaim VAT on a van?
Yes, normally, when the van is for business use and you hold a valid VAT invoice. Keep evidence of the business use.
How much VAT can I reclaim on a leased car?
Usually 50% of the VAT when the car has private use. The other 50% is blocked.
