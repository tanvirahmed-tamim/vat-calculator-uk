VAT Registration Threshold 2026: The £90,000 Rule Explained

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

The registration threshold is not a simple calendar-year test. It rolls, it looks forward, and crossing it starts a deadline.

In short

Register when taxable turnover for the last 12 months exceeds £90,000, or when you expect it to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days. You can ask to deregister when you expect turnover in the next 12 months to be below £88,000.

The current threshold

The VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover over a rolling 12-month period. Taxable turnover means standard-rated, reduced-rate and zero-rated sales. Exempt sales and supplies outside the scope of VAT do not count.

Turnover is the value of sales, not profit. A business with thin margins can cross the threshold quickly even if it keeps very little of the money.

The rolling 12-month test

At the end of every month, look back over the previous 12 months. If the total taxable turnover for that window is more than £90,000, you have crossed the threshold. It does not matter whether the sales were spread evenly or concentrated in one busy quarter.

Once you exceed the threshold, you must notify HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you crossed it. Registration is then effective from the first day of the second month after the month you exceeded it.

The 30-day forward look

There is a second test. If you expect your taxable turnover to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone, you must register before the end of that 30-day period. This catches businesses that land a large contract or start trading at scale quickly.

The forward test is about expectation, not certainty. If a signed contract makes it clear that you will cross the threshold, do not wait for the invoices to be issued.

A worked timeline

Suppose your rolling 12-month taxable turnover passes £90,000 during August.

  1. You have until 30 September to notify HMRC.
  2. Your registration is effective from 1 October.
  3. From 1 October, you must charge VAT on taxable sales and can reclaim input VAT on eligible purchases.
  4. You must keep digital records and file returns under Making Tax Digital.

In practice, allow time to update your prices, invoices and accounting software before the effective date. Backdating rules can apply if notification is late.

Deregistration

The deregistration threshold is £88,000, and the test looks forward, not back. You can ask HMRC to cancel your registration if you expect your taxable turnover in the next 12 months to be below £88,000. The relevant wording is in VAT Notice 700/1 paragraph 3.7, which refers to supplies that will not go over the deregistration threshold in the next 12 months.

You cannot apply for retrospective cancellation because turnover has already fallen. The decision is about what you expect to happen next, so keep the evidence behind the forecast.

The £2,000 gap between the two thresholds gives businesses headroom so they are not forced to register and deregister every time turnover wobbles around a single figure.

Deregistering means you stop charging VAT, but you also stop reclaiming it. You may need to account for VAT on stock and assets you hold at the deregistration date.

Voluntary registration

You can register before you reach the threshold. Voluntary registration can be attractive if you sell to other VAT-registered businesses, because they can reclaim the VAT you charge, and you can reclaim VAT on your own purchases.

It is less attractive if you sell to consumers who cannot reclaim the VAT. Adding 20% to your prices may lose sales, and absorbing the VAT cuts your margin. Registration also brings returns, record keeping and Making Tax Digital obligations.

What changes after registration

  • You charge VAT on taxable sales and show it separately on invoices.
  • You reclaim VAT on eligible business purchases, subject to the blocked items.
  • You file VAT returns, usually quarterly, through compatible software.
  • You keep digital records for at least six years.
  • You may choose a scheme such as the Flat Rate Scheme or annual accounting if eligible.

The threshold rose from £85,000 to £90,000, and the deregistration level from £83,000 to £88,000, on 1 April 2024. Both figures remain at those levels. If you are reading older guidance that quotes £85,000, it predates that change.

Questions

What is the VAT registration threshold?
The threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period. It is not a calendar-year test, so check the total at the end of every month.
When must I register for VAT?
You must tell HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you exceeded the threshold. Registration is normally effective from the first day of the second month after you crossed it.
What is the deregistration threshold?
The deregistration threshold is £88,000. You can ask HMRC to cancel your registration if you expect your taxable turnover in the next 12 months to be below that figure. You cannot cancel retrospectively because turnover has already fallen.
