VAT on Energy: Domestic Fuel, Business Power and Materials

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

Energy has a split rate: domestic fuel is reduced-rated, business energy is standard-rated, and a temporary zero rate applies to domestic electricity in Great Britain from October 2026.

In short

Domestic gas and most domestic fuel are 5%. Domestic electricity in Great Britain is temporarily 0% from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027, while Northern Ireland stays at 5%. Business energy is 20%. Energy-saving materials are 0% until 31 March 2027.

Who this is for: Householders, landlords, charities, energy suppliers, installers and businesses that pay for fuel or power.

Domestic fuel

Fuel and power supplied for genuine domestic use is reduced-rated at 5%. That covers gas, heating oil, solid fuel and electricity supplied to a dwelling, and non-business use by a charity. The temporary zero rate for electricity in Great Britain is the exception described below.

The reduced rate is not automatic for every supply. The supplier must be satisfied that the use qualifies, and some mixed-use premises need an apportionment between the domestic and business parts.

The electricity zero rate

From 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027, qualifying supplies of domestic electricity in Great Britain are zero-rated. The temporary rate applies in England, Scotland and Wales. In Northern Ireland, domestic electricity remains at the reduced rate of 5%.

The zero rate applies to the same qualifying supplies that previously received the reduced rate. Domestic gas and other domestic fuels remain at 5% across the UK. The end date was extended from 31 March to 30 June 2027 at Budget 2026, so older guidance that quotes March is out of date.

From 1 November 2026, electricity supplied through a publicly accessible or workplace electric vehicle charge point is not treated as domestic use, whatever the quantity. Charging an EV at home from the ordinary household supply is different: that remains domestic use, even if the charging is separately metered or on a special tariff. Read VAT Notice 701/19 paragraph 3.1.1 before repricing a charging service.

If you are a supplier, update the rate codes and the invoices for the period, and check the customer's qualifying use. If you are a business customer, do not assume the zero rate applies to business electricity: it does not.

Business energy

Electricity and gas supplied for business use are standard-rated at 20%. A VAT-registered business can normally recover the VAT as input tax, subject to the normal rules on private use and exempt activity.

A charity can receive the reduced rate on non-business use, but business use by a charity is standard-rated. Mixed use needs an apportionment, and the supplier may ask for a declaration.

Climate Change Levy is separate from VAT. It appears on business energy bills and is not a VAT charge, although VAT may apply to the levy as part of the bill.

Energy-saving materials

The installation of certain energy-saving materials in eligible buildings is zero-rated until 31 March 2027. The relief covers items such as insulation, solar panels, heat pumps and certain heating controls, when the conditions are met.

The relief is not a general zero rate for all energy work. It depends on the material, the building and who is having the work done. After 31 March 2027, the rate is expected to return to 5%, so a contract that spans the change needs careful handling.

Mixed use

A property used partly as a home and partly as a business needs a fair apportionment of the fuel bill. The domestic share can use the reduced rate, and the business share is standard-rated. Keep the calculation and the evidence of the split.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the electricity zero rate to business premises.
  • Charging 0% on domestic gas as well as electricity.
  • Assuming the energy-saving materials relief covers all building work.
  • Ignoring the rate change at the end of the temporary period.

Questions

Is VAT charged on domestic electricity?
In Great Britain, qualifying domestic electricity is temporarily zero-rated from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027. In Northern Ireland it remains at 5%.
Is VAT on domestic gas 0% or 5%?
Domestic gas remains at the reduced rate of 5%. The temporary zero rate applies to domestic electricity in Great Britain, not to gas.
Is business energy standard-rated?
Yes, business electricity and gas are standard-rated at 20%. A VAT-registered business can normally recover the VAT as input tax.
Are solar panels zero-rated?
The installation of qualifying energy-saving materials, including solar panels, is zero-rated until 31 March 2027 when the conditions are met.
What happens after 30 June 2027?
Domestic electricity in Great Britain is expected to return to 5% after the temporary zero rate ends. Energy-saving materials return to 5% after 31 March 2027. Check the current rate before invoicing.
Can a charity get 5% VAT on energy?
Yes, for non-business use that qualifies. Business use by a charity is standard-rated, and mixed use needs an apportionment.
