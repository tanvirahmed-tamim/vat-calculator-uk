History

UK VAT Rate History 2026: Every Change Since 1973

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 6 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

VAT replaced Purchase Tax in 1973. The standard rate history since then covers six changes, including the 15% temporary rate of 2008–09 and the 20% standard rate that has applied since 2011.

In short

VAT began at 10% in April 1973. The standard rate fell to 8% in 1974, rose to 15% in 1979, to 17.5% in 1991, briefly to 15% in 2008–09, back to 17.5% in 2010 and to 20% on 4 January 2011.

The timeline

VAT was introduced in the United Kingdom on 1 April 1973, the day the UK joined the European Economic Community. It replaced Purchase Tax and Selective Employment Tax. The initial standard rate was 10%.

In July 1974 the standard rate was cut to 8%, and a higher rate of 25% was introduced for luxury goods. The higher rate was abolished in 1979.

In June 1979 the standard rate rose to 15%, where it stayed for more than a decade. The 1991 Budget raised it to 17.5%.

In response to the financial crisis, the standard rate was cut to 15% from 1 December 2008 to 31 December 2009, then returned to 17.5% on 1 January 2010.

On 4 January 2011 the standard rate rose to 20%, where it remains. The change was announced in the June 2010 Budget as part of deficit reduction measures.

The reduced rate

A reduced rate of 5% was introduced in 1994 for domestic fuel and power. Since then it has been extended to a list that includes children's car seats, some energy-saving materials, certain residential renovations and some health-related installations.

The reduced rate has not changed since 1994, but the list of qualifying supplies has been amended several times. A supply can therefore move into or out of the reduced rate without the percentage itself changing.

Temporary rates

Temporary reduced rates have been used for specific sectors. During the coronavirus pandemic, hospitality, holiday accommodation and attractions had a temporary rate of 5% from 15 July 2020, then 12.5% from 1 October 2021, returning to 20% on 1 April 2022.

These temporary rates are a reminder that the headline standard rate is not always the rate that applied to a given transaction. Always check the date and the sector.

Why rates changed

Rate changes have usually followed fiscal pressure. The 1979 rise accompanied a shift from direct to indirect taxation. The 1991 rise helped fund local government finance changes. The 2008 cut was a stimulus measure, and the 2011 rise was part of deficit reduction.

Rate changes are announced in a Budget or a statement and normally take effect on a stated date. Businesses have to update prices, software and invoices from that date, and the tax point decides which rate applies to a supply that straddles the change.

Using old rates today

If you are checking a historical invoice, use the rate in force at the tax point, not today's rate. A 2009 invoice at 15% is correct for its period, and recalculating it at 20% would give a false answer.

The VAT calculator accepts a custom rate, so you can reproduce an old calculation. For current rates, see VAT rates explained.

Questions

When did VAT start in the UK?
VAT started on 1 April 1973 at a standard rate of 10%, when the UK joined the European Economic Community. It replaced purchase tax.
What is the highest standard rate the UK has had?
The highest standard rate is 20%, first introduced on 4 January 2011 and still in force. There was also a 25% higher rate for a small category of goods between 1974 and 1979.
Has the UK ever cut VAT temporarily?
Yes. The standard rate was cut from 17.5% to 15% between December 2008 and December 2009, and a temporary 5% rate applied to hospitality, holiday accommodation and attractions from July 2020 to September 2021.
