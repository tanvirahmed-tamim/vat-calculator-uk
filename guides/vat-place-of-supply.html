Place of Supply Rules for VAT: Where a Sale Is Taxed

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of International and cross-border VAT

Place of supply is the first question in any cross-border VAT calculation. It decides whether you charge UK VAT, no VAT, or tax in another country.

In short

Goods usually follow where they are when sold or delivered. B2B services usually follow the customer's country, and B2C services usually follow the supplier's country, with special rules for digital services and land.

Who this is for: Any business buying or selling across borders, and anyone invoicing a customer outside the UK.

Goods

For goods sold without transport, the place of supply is where the goods are when the sale takes place. If you transport them, the starting point is where the goods are when the movement begins. An export leaves the UK and is zero-rated with evidence; an import enters the UK and attracts import VAT.

Goods installed or assembled by the supplier are usually supplied where they are installed. That can move the sale into another country even though the goods were manufactured in the UK.

B2B services

For most B2B services, the place of supply is where the customer belongs. The supplier does not charge UK VAT on a cross-border supply, and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge.

Belonging is more than a postal address. It is the business establishment, the fixed establishment most closely concerned with the supply, or, for an individual with no establishment, their usual place of residence. A registered office with no staff or resources is not enough.

Get evidence that the customer is in business and where they belong. A VAT number is the best evidence; commercial documents can support a customer who is below a registration threshold. If you cannot evidence business status, treat the supply as B2C and apply the consumer rules.

A UK business receiving services from an overseas supplier usually accounts for the VAT under the reverse charge: output tax in box 1, the same amount as input tax in box 4, and the value in boxes 6 and 7.

B2C services

For most B2C services, the place of supply is where the supplier belongs. A UK business charges UK VAT to a consumer abroad, unless a special rule applies.

The exceptions matter more than the default. Land, events, transport, short-term hire and digital services all have their own tests, and several of them move the supply to the customer's country rather than the supplier's.

A use-and-enjoyment override can move certain digital, telecoms, broadcasting and hired goods supplies when the customer's country does not reflect where the service is actually used.

Evidence

Keep evidence of what was supplied, who received it and where they belong. For business customers, record the VAT number and the establishment that received the service. For consumers, keep the address, IP address, bank details or other commercial information that supports the location.

The place of supply decides the rate, the registration and the invoice wording, so the evidence should still make sense years later. If the place of supply is outside the UK, do not charge UK VAT, but check whether the customer's country requires you to register.

A non-UK business supplying services that are taxable in the UK may have to register here. There is no registration threshold for services supplied by businesses not established in the UK.

The digital services guide applies these tests to downloads and subscriptions, and the imports and exports guide covers goods.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the supplier's location always decides the place of supply.
  • Treating a consumer as a business without evidence.
  • Ignoring the special rules for land and digital services.
  • Failing to keep location evidence.

Questions

What is place of supply?
The rule that decides which country has the right to tax a sale. It determines whether UK VAT applies or the sale is taxed elsewhere.
Where is the place of supply for B2B services?
Usually the customer's country. The supplier does not charge UK VAT on a cross-border B2B supply, and the customer accounts for tax under the reverse charge.
Where is the place of supply for B2C services?
Usually the supplier's country. A UK business charges UK VAT to a consumer abroad, unless a special rule applies.
Do digital services follow the same rule?
No. Digital services are generally taxed where the consumer belongs, so the customer's location decides the VAT.
What evidence should I keep?
Evidence of the customer's business status and where they belong, plus the goods movement documents for exports. For consumers, keep the address, IP address or other commercial information that supports their location.
What if I cannot prove my customer is a business?
Treat the supply as B2C. Without a VAT number or commercial evidence of business status, the consumer rules apply and you may have to charge UK VAT or register in the customer's country.
Does the place of supply decide the VAT rate?
No. Place of supply decides which country taxes the sale. Once you know the supply is in the UK, a separate question decides whether it is standard-rated, reduced, zero-rated or exempt.
