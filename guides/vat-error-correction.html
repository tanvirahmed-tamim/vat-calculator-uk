How to Correct a VAT Error: Rules, Limits and Examples

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

A VAT error is not always a crisis, but it always has to be corrected. Small errors can go on your next return; larger or deliberate ones must be notified to HMRC separately. The test is arithmetic, then behaviour.

In short

You can correct an error on your next return if its net value is £10,000 or less, or between £10,000 and £50,000 and no more than 1% of box 6 on the return where you discover it. Above those limits, or for any deliberate error, notify HMRC online. The correction window is normally four years.

Who this is for: Any VAT-registered business that has found an error on a past return, whether it underpaid or overpaid VAT.

The limits

The rule turns on the net value of the error: the tax due to HMRC minus the tax due back to you. You can correct an error on your next return if the net value is £10,000 or less, or if it is between £10,000 and £50,000 and does not exceed 1% of box 6 on the return for the period in which you discover the error.

That last detail matters. The 1% is measured against the box 6 figure of the current return, not the return that contained the mistake. A growing business can therefore self-correct a larger error than its earlier turnover would suggest.

If the net error is over £50,000, or over £10,000 and more than 1% of box 6, you must use method 2 and tell HMRC separately. The same applies to any deliberate error, whatever its size. Deliberate errors cannot be netted off against other errors.

The correction window is normally four years from the end of the accounting period in which the error was made, or from the due date of the return for under-claimed input tax. Deliberate errors are not subject to that four-year limit.

Method 1: next return

To correct on your next return, adjust your VAT account and include the net amount in box 1 if you owe tax, or box 4 if HMRC owes you. Keep a note of the date you found the error, how it happened and the amount involved.

Do not simply change the original return. Once a return has been submitted, the correction is made in a later period, and the audit trail should show both the original entry and the correction.

Method 1 is not a disclosure for penalty purposes. If the error was careless, you can still correct it this way, but you will not get the maximum penalty reduction unless you also tell HMRC about the error and your grounds for a reduction in writing or through the online error notification.

Method 2: notify HMRC

For errors above the limits, report the error to HMRC online. The notification needs to explain how each error arose, the period it occurred in, whether it was input or output tax, the amounts under- or over-declared, how you worked them out, and the total adjustment.

If you cannot use the online service, you can write to the VAT Error Correction Team with the same information. Use the online service or a letter containing the notification details rather than an old paper form.

The same route applies if you are not sure which period the error belongs to, or if the error spans several returns. Describe the calculation clearly and attach the relevant invoices or schedules.

Deliberate errors

A deliberate error is one that was made knowingly, or because you were reckless about whether it was correct. It must always be reported separately, even if the amount is small. The penalty can be up to 70% of the tax, or up to 100% if the error was concealed.

Deliberate errors also carry a longer assessment window: HMRC can look back up to 20 years. Taking advice early is usually cheaper than waiting for an inspection.

Interest and penalties

If you underpaid VAT, late payment interest normally runs from the original due date until the tax is paid. For periods from 6 April 2025 the rate is the Bank of England base rate plus 4%; before that date it was base rate plus 2.5%. The rate changes when the base rate changes, so check the current figure before estimating a bill.

A penalty may also apply, depending on whether the error was careless or deliberate and how quickly you disclosed it. If you overpaid, HMRC may pay repayment interest, calculated at base rate minus 1% with a floor of 0.5%.

The penalties guide sets out the percentages and the disclosure reductions.

A business that finds a £12,000 underpayment while its current return shows £1.5 million in box 6 can use method 1, because £12,000 is under 1% of £1.5 million. The same error with a £1 million box 6 figure must go to HMRC under method 2.

Common mistakes

  • Netting a deliberate error against an innocent one.
  • Changing a submitted return instead of correcting it in a later period.
  • Measuring the 1% test against the wrong return's box 6 figure.
  • Assuming the 4-year window runs from the date the error was discovered.
  • Treating method 1 as a penalty disclosure when the error was careless.

Questions

Can I correct a VAT error on my next return?
Yes, if the net error is £10,000 or less, or between £10,000 and £50,000 and no more than 1% of box 6 on the return in which you discover it. Larger errors must be notified separately.
What is the £50,000 VAT error limit?
The £50,000 figure is the absolute ceiling for correcting an error on your next return. Between £10,000 and £50,000 you can self-correct only if the error is no more than 1% of box 6 on the current return.
How long do I have to correct a VAT error?
Normally four years from the end of the accounting period in which the error was made, or from the due date for under-claimed input tax. Deliberate errors can be assessed for up to 20 years.
Do I have to tell HMRC about a small error?
You must always correct the error, but within the method 1 limits you can do it on your next return rather than notifying HMRC separately. If the error was careless and you want the maximum penalty reduction, you still need to tell HMRC about it in writing.
Does HMRC charge interest on a corrected error?
Yes, if you underpaid VAT. Late payment interest normally runs from the original due date until the tax is paid, at base rate plus 4% for periods from 6 April 2025, separately from any penalty.
How do I notify HMRC of a VAT error?
Use HMRC's online service for VAT return errors, or write to the VAT Error Correction Team with the same information if you cannot use the online service.
