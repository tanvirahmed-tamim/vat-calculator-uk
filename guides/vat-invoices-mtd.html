VAT Invoices and Making Tax Digital: Records to Keep

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

An invoice that looks fine to a customer can still fail the VAT rules. These are the details HMRC expects, and the records that support them.

In short

A full VAT invoice needs supplier and customer details, a unique number, the tax point, a description, the net total, the rate and amount of VAT, and the gross total. Keep digital records for at least six years.

Invoice fields

A full VAT invoice normally needs to show:

  • the supplier's name, address and VAT registration number;
  • a unique invoice number;
  • the tax point, if it differs from the invoice date;
  • the customer's name and address;
  • a description of the goods or services, and the quantity or extent;
  • the net amount for each rate, the rate itself and the VAT amount;
  • the gross total;
  • any discount terms, if a discount is offered.

The invoice does not have to be paper. A PDF or a software-generated invoice is fine as long as it contains the required information and can be kept as a record.

Simplified invoices

Retailers can issue a simplified invoice for retail supplies under £250 including VAT. It must still show the supplier's name, address and VAT number, the date, a description of the goods or services, and the total including VAT. It does not need the customer's details or a separate VAT breakdown.

If a customer asks for a full VAT invoice, a retailer may need to provide one, particularly for business purchases where the customer wants to reclaim the VAT.

Credit notes

When a sale is cancelled, returned or reduced, a credit note adjusts the original invoice. It should reference the original invoice number and date, show the amount being credited, and show the VAT being reversed at the same rate as the original sale.

Do not simply delete the original invoice. The audit trail matters, and the VAT return for the period should reflect both the sale and the credit.

Making Tax Digital

Making Tax Digital for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file returns using compatible software. The requirement applies to all VAT-registered businesses, including those that registered voluntarily.

Digital records normally include:

  • the business name, address and VAT number;
  • the VAT account, showing output tax and input tax;
  • details of each supply and purchase, including the VAT rate and amount;
  • adjustments such as credit notes and bad debt relief.

Spreadsheets can be used if they are linked to compatible software through a bridging product. A PDF alone is not a digital record for MTD purposes.

Record retention

Keep VAT records for at least six years from the end of the accounting period they relate to. Records include invoices, receipts, credit notes, import and export documents, and the digital records behind each return.

HMRC can ask to see records during a compliance check. If records are lost, a reconstructed set may be accepted, but the burden is on the business to show that the figures are complete and accurate.

Common errors

  • Charging VAT without showing the rate and amount separately.
  • Using the wrong VAT number or omitting it entirely.
  • Issuing one invoice at a single rate when the supply mixes rates.
  • Failing to raise a credit note for a refund.
  • Keeping only PDFs and assuming that satisfies Making Tax Digital.
  • Destroying records after the accounting software is retired.

Questions

What must a VAT invoice include?
A full VAT invoice normally shows the supplier's name, address and VAT number, a unique invoice number and the tax point. It also shows the customer's name and address, a description of the supply, the net total, the rate and amount of VAT, and the gross total.
When can I use a simplified invoice?
A simplified invoice can be used for retail supplies where the total including VAT is no more than £250. It must still show the supplier's details, the tax point, a description and the VAT rate.
Do I have to use Making Tax Digital software?
Yes, if you are VAT-registered. VAT returns must be filed through compatible software that keeps digital records and submits the return directly to HMRC.
