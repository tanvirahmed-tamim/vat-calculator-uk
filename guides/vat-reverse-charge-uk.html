VAT Reverse Charge UK: How It Works, When It Applies and VAT Return Rules

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 25 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

The VAT reverse charge changes who accounts for VAT on a sale. Instead of the supplier charging VAT, the VAT-registered customer records the VAT on its own VAT return. The supplier invoices the net amount and states that the reverse charge applies. For a fully taxable customer, the output tax and input tax usually cancel out, so the commercial cost stays the same while the paperwork moves to the buyer.

In short

Two domestic regimes use the reverse charge: the CIS construction domestic reverse charge from 1 March 2021, and the VAT Notice 735 charge for specified goods and services such as mobile phones, computer chips, wholesale energy and wholesale telecommunications. Cross-border services follow separate place of supply rules.

Who this is for: UK contractors, subcontractors, wholesalers, retailers selling business-to-business, and finance teams that need to invoice or account for a reverse charge supply correctly.

General information: This guide is based on GOV.UK, HMRC VAT Notice 735, VAT Notice 700 and VAT Notice 700/12. It is not personal tax or accounting advice. Check the current HMRC guidance or confirm your treatment with a qualified UK accountant before you invoice or file.

How does reverse charge VAT work in the UK?

Under a domestic reverse charge, the supplier does not charge VAT on the invoice. The customer treats the supply as if it had made the sale itself: it calculates the VAT at the rate that would otherwise apply, adds that amount to its output tax, and normally claims the same amount back as input tax.

  1. The supplier works out the net value and the VAT rate that would normally apply.
  2. The supplier invoices the net value only and states that the reverse charge applies.
  3. The customer calculates the deemed VAT and enters it as output tax and input tax on the same return.
  4. The customer pays the net invoice value to the supplier and settles any VAT with HMRC through the return.

The reverse charge does not change the rate. A standard-rated supply stays at 20% and a reduced-rate supply stays at 5%. It changes the party that reports the VAT. The supplier records the sale without output tax, and the customer records both sides of the VAT entry. For a business that makes only taxable supplies and recovers all its input VAT, the net cash effect is usually zero.

The supplier's cash flow is neutral apart from timing: it never receives the VAT, so it never has to fund a VAT bill on that supply. The customer may face a timing difference if it pays the supplier before it files the return, because it accounts for output tax and input tax on the return rather than at the invoice date.

HMRC also uses the term reverse charge for cross-border services, where the place of supply moves the VAT to the customer's country. That is a separate set of rules. This guide covers the two UK domestic charges: construction under the Construction Industry Scheme and the specified goods and services listed in VAT Notice 735.

When does reverse charge VAT apply in the UK?

The construction domestic reverse charge applies from 1 March 2021 when all of these conditions are met:

  • The supplier and the customer are both registered for UK VAT.
  • The customer is registered for CIS, or is liable to be registered for CIS.
  • The supply is standard-rated at 20% or reduced-rated at 5%.
  • The work falls within the scope of CIS construction operations.
  • The supplier is not an employment business supplying staff.
  • The customer has not given written confirmation that it is an end user or an intermediary supplier.

Zero-rated work is outside the charge. A zero-rated new build therefore stays zero-rated and does not move to the reverse charge, even when the customer is CIS registered. The VAT for construction guide covers the full list of CIS operations and the 0%, 5% and 20% boundaries in detail.

An end user is a CIS-registered customer that will use the building or structure itself, rather than sell it on. An intermediary supplier is a CIS-registered business that buys the work and sells it on as part of a larger construction project, without being the end user. Either can give written confirmation, and the supplier then charges VAT normally.

The 5% disregard

If the reverse charge element is 5% or less of the whole supply value, the supplier and customer can agree from the start of the contract to apply normal VAT instead. The test uses the overall contract value, not each invoice. The disregard does not apply where the predominant element of the supply is zero-rated.

Labour, materials and excluded services

When labour and materials form one covered construction service, the whole supply sits inside the reverse charge. Standalone supplies of materials or goods are not construction services, so they stay outside the construction charge and follow normal VAT rules.

Some work is outside CIS and therefore outside the construction reverse charge when it is supplied on its own. Examples include signwriting, installing blinds or shutters, installing security systems, and drilling for or extracting oil, gas or minerals. VAT on property explains how to separate construction work from other property supplies.

Specified goods and services under VAT Notice 735

VAT Notice 735 applies a reverse charge to business-to-business supplies of specified goods and services between VAT-registered businesses in the UK. The list covers:

  • Mobile phones and computer chips.
  • Wholesale gas and electricity.
  • Emission allowances.
  • Wholesale telecommunications services.
  • Renewable energy certificates.
  • Construction services supplied within the domestic reverse charge.

For mobile phones and computer chips, the charge does not apply when the VAT-exclusive value of a single invoice is £5,000 or less. That is a per-invoice de minimis, not a first £5,000 allowance, so an invoice above the limit is caught in full. The other categories do not share that threshold.

Who pays the VAT and what should a reverse charge VAT invoice include?

The customer pays the VAT to HMRC through its VAT return. The supplier does not charge VAT and does not pay output tax on the supply. The customer still pays the supplier the net invoice value, so the supplier's price does not include a VAT add-on.

A VAT invoice for a reverse charge supply must carry the normal commercial details and make the treatment clear. Include:

  • The supplier's name, address and VAT registration number.
  • The customer's name and address, and its VAT registration number where required.
  • A unique invoice number and the invoice date.
  • A description of the goods or services and the net value.
  • The VAT rate that would otherwise apply, and the deemed VAT amount shown separately.
  • A statement that the reverse charge applies, such as "reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies".

Show the deemed VAT as information only. Do not add it to the total charged. A £10,000 supply at 20% shows £10,000.00 net, £2,000.00 reverse charge VAT, and a total of £10,000.00 payable. The customer needs the rate and the amount to complete Box 1 and Box 4 correctly.

Keep the invoice wording consistent with the VAT invoice and MTD guide. If the customer is an end user or intermediary, keep the written confirmation with your records and charge VAT in the normal way.

CIS deductions are separate from VAT. A subcontractor can suffer a CIS deduction on the labour element of the same payment while the VAT treatment follows the reverse charge. Check the customer's CIS status and the VAT conditions independently.

How is reverse charge VAT calculated? (Worked GBP example)

Take a VAT-registered subcontractor that supplies £10,000.00 of standard-rated construction services to a CIS-registered contractor. The reverse charge applies, so the supplier invoices £10,000.00 with no VAT added.

Reverse charge on a £10,000.00 net supply at 20%
FigureNormal VAT invoiceReverse charge invoice
Net supply£10,000.00£10,000.00
VAT at 20%£2,000.00 charged to the customer£2,000.00 deemed VAT, shown separately
Invoice total£12,000.00£10,000.00
Supplier pays HMRC£2,000.00 output tax£0.00 on this supply
Customer net VAT cost£0.00 after input tax recovery£0.00 after Box 1 and Box 4 entries

The customer enters £2,000.00 in Box 1 and £2,000.00 in Box 4, so the net VAT position is £0.00. The £10,000.00 net purchase goes in Box 7. If the customer is partially exempt, it may not recover the full £2,000.00, and the unrecoverable share becomes a real cost.

For a reduced-rate supply at 5%, the same £10,000.00 net value carries £500.00 of reverse charge VAT. The rate follows the underlying supply, not the identity of the customer. Use the reverse charge calculator to test a net value, rate and invoice total.

Where does reverse charge go on a VAT return?

VAT Notice 700/12 sets the return entries. The supplier and the customer record different boxes:

Reverse charge entries on the VAT return
PartyBoxEntry
SupplierBox 6Net value of the sale, with no output tax in Box 1
CustomerBox 1Reverse charge VAT as output tax
CustomerBox 4The same VAT as input tax, where full recovery applies
CustomerBox 7Net value of the purchase
CustomerBox 6No entry for the reverse charge purchase

The most common error is putting the net purchase in Box 6 as well as Box 7. For a domestic reverse charge, the customer does not include the net purchase in Box 6. Box 6 is for the customer's own sales, not its purchases.

Cross-border services can be different. Where a supply is outside the scope of UK VAT but the customer must account for it, Box 6 may also be needed. The imports and exports guide covers that boundary.

The Cash Accounting Scheme cannot be used for reverse charge supplies. Flat Rate Scheme users leave reverse charge supplies out of the flat rate calculation, as the Flat Rate Scheme comparison explains. Annual Accounting does not change the box entries.

Common mistakes

  • Charging 20% VAT when the construction reverse charge applies.
  • Applying the charge to a zero-rated new build or to a customer who is not CIS registered.
  • Treating the 5% disregard as an invoice-by-invoice test instead of a whole-contract test.
  • Adding the deemed VAT to the invoice total, so the customer pays VAT twice.
  • Putting the reverse charge purchase in Box 6 as well as Box 7.
  • Assuming the £5,000 phone and chip de minimis works as a first £5,000 allowance.

Frequently asked questions

What is reverse charge VAT and does it still apply in the UK?
Reverse charge VAT is a mechanism where the VAT-registered buyer accounts for VAT on a supply instead of the supplier. It still applies in the UK: the construction domestic reverse charge has run since 1 March 2021, and VAT Notice 735 covers specified goods such as mobile phones and computer chips.
Is the VAT reverse charge mandatory?
Yes. The reverse charge is mandatory when the conditions are met, and the supplier cannot choose to charge VAT instead. The main exceptions are written end user or intermediary confirmation in construction, and the optional 5% disregard agreement.
Do materials and goods follow the reverse charge?
Materials supplied as part of covered construction services follow the service and sit inside the reverse charge. Standalone supplies of materials or goods are outside the construction charge, although specified goods such as mobile phones and computer chips have their own Notice 735 rules.
Which VAT return boxes does the reverse charge use?
The supplier reports the net sale in Box 6 and does not enter output tax in Box 1. The customer enters the reverse charge VAT in Box 1 and Box 4, and the net purchase in Box 7, not Box 6.
