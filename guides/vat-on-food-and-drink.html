VAT on Food and Drink: Zero, Reduced and Standard Rates

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

Food is not automatically VAT-free. Most groceries are zero-rated, but the moment food is cooked, served, sweetened or poured into a glass, the rate can change to 20%.

In short

Most food bought cold for home consumption is zero-rated. Catering, hot takeaway food, confectionery, crisps, ice cream, soft drinks and alcohol are standard-rated at 20%. The boundary turns on the product and the circumstances of sale.

Who this is for: Restaurants, cafes, takeaways, pubs, bakeries, food retailers, caterers and anyone selling food or drink.

Zero-rated food

Most food intended for human consumption is zero-rated when it is sold cold for consumption off the premises. That includes bread, meat, fish, vegetables, fruit, dairy products, rice, pasta, flour and most ingredients used for home cooking.

Zero-rated is still a taxable supply. It goes on the VAT return, and the business can normally recover input tax on costs linked to those sales. The zero rate is not the same as exempt.

Standard-rated food

A long list of food and drink is excluded from the zero rate and charged at 20%. The main categories are:

  • Confectionery, including chocolate, sweets and most cereal bars.
  • Crisps and savoury snacks.
  • Ice cream, ice lollies and similar frozen products.
  • Soft drinks, mineral water and juices, unless they qualify for a specific relief.
  • Alcohol of all kinds.
  • Food sold for consumption on the premises, and hot takeaway food.

The product description matters more than the shop. A chocolate-covered biscuit is standard-rated, while a plain biscuit is normally zero-rated. A cake is zero-rated, even when it contains chocolate, unless it is a confectionery item by nature.

Catering and takeaway

Catering is standard-rated at 20%, whether it is a restaurant meal, a wedding buffet or food supplied at an event. Hot takeaway food is also standard-rated: if the food is heated, kept hot or sold hot at the point of sale, the zero rate does not apply.

Cold takeaway food is usually zero-rated. That is why a sandwich shop and a hot-food counter can sit next to each other and charge different rates. If you sell both, keep the product ranges and till records separate.

A delivery charge follows the VAT treatment of the food. A hot pizza and its delivery are standard-rated; a cold sandwich and its delivery are zero-rated.

Mixed baskets

A basket containing zero-rated and standard-rated items is not automatically taxed at one rate. Each item follows its own treatment. If you sell a hamper or a meal deal for a single price, apportion the price between the components on a fair and reasonable basis and show the VAT on the standard-rated part.

A composite supply, where the items are inseparable, can be treated as a single supply at the rate of the dominant element. A meal with a drink is usually a mixed supply, not a composite one, because the drink is separately identifiable.

Records

Food businesses need product-level records: what was sold, whether it was hot or cold, whether it was eaten on the premises, and how the price was split. Keep the menus, recipes and till reports that support the treatment.

Common mistakes

  • Treating all takeaway food as zero-rated when hot food is standard-rated.
  • Zero-rating confectionery, crisps or soft drinks.
  • Applying one rate to a mixed basket without apportioning.
  • Forgetting that delivery follows the food's VAT treatment.

Questions

Is all food zero-rated for VAT?
No. Most cold food for home consumption is zero-rated, but catering, hot takeaway, confectionery, crisps, ice cream, soft drinks and alcohol are standard-rated at 20%.
Is takeaway food zero-rated?
Only cold takeaway food, and not all of it. Hot takeaway food is standard-rated at 20%, and so are confectionery, crisps and soft drinks.
Is a chocolate-covered biscuit zero-rated?
No, it is normally standard-rated as confectionery. A plain biscuit is zero-rated, and a cake is generally zero-rated even if it contains chocolate.
Do I charge VAT on delivery?
Yes, at the same rate as the food. Hot food and its delivery are standard-rated; cold zero-rated food and its delivery are zero-rated.
How do I tax a meal deal?
Apportion the price between the zero-rated and standard-rated items on a fair and reasonable basis, and charge VAT on the standard-rated part.
Is alcohol ever zero-rated?
No. Alcohol is standard-rated at 20%, even when it is sold with food or as part of a meal.
