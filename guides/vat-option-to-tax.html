Option to Tax: Making Commercial Property VATable

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

Commercial rent is exempt by default. Opting to tax turns it into a standard-rated supply, which lets the landlord recover VAT on the property but adds 20% to the rent.

In short

An option to tax makes a commercial property taxable at 20%. It usually lasts 20 years and must be notified to HMRC within 30 days. Residential and certain charity uses are excluded, and the option can transfer with the property.

Who this is for: Commercial landlords, property developers, investors and businesses that let or buy commercial property.

What the option does

Commercial rent and most sales of commercial property are exempt. An option to tax changes that: the landlord charges 20% VAT on the rent or sale and can recover VAT on qualifying costs such as repairs, refurbishment and professional fees.

The option does not change the property itself or the tenant's status. A tenant that is VAT-registered can usually recover the VAT, so the option is often neutral between businesses. A tenant that cannot recover VAT, such as a small charity or an unregistered business, faces a real 20% cost increase.

When to opt

Opting is most useful when the landlord has significant VAT on costs to recover, for example after a refurbishment, or when the property is let to VAT-registered tenants. It is less useful when the tenant cannot recover VAT, because the rent becomes more expensive.

The decision is usually irrevocable for 20 years, so model the effect over the whole period, not just the first year. Once opted, the option applies to future supplies of the property, including a sale, unless a specific exception applies.

Notification

Notify HMRC within 30 days of deciding to opt, using the option to tax notification form. Keep a copy with the property records. The option takes effect from the date you specify, which can be the date of notification or an earlier date if the conditions are met.

You do not need permission to opt, but you must keep the notification and be able to show it if HMRC asks. If the property is sold, the option can transfer to the buyer, so the sale documents should state the position clearly.

Exclusions

Some supplies cannot be opted, or are excluded from the effect of an option. These include residential property, certain charity buildings and some land used for residential purposes. A property that is partly residential and partly commercial needs a careful apportionment.

There are also rules that disapply an option in specific circumstances, such as a sale of a property that has been used for a residential purpose. Check before assuming an option covers the whole building.

Sales and transfers

When a commercial property is sold as a transfer of a going concern, the option can transfer with it. The buyer takes on the option and the obligations that go with it. If the sale is not a going concern, the seller charges VAT on the sale if the property is opted.

Stamp duty is separate. SDLT applies in England and Northern Ireland, LBTT in Scotland and LTT in Wales. VAT on the property is calculated separately from the property transaction tax. The VAT on property guide sets out the wider 0%, 5% and 20% map.

Common mistakes

  • Opting without checking whether the tenant can recover VAT.
  • Assuming the option covers residential parts of a mixed building.
  • Missing the 30-day notification window.
  • Forgetting that the option can transfer to a buyer.

Questions

What does opting to tax mean?
Opting to tax makes a commercial property taxable at 20%, so the landlord charges VAT on rent and can recover VAT on qualifying costs.
How long does an option to tax last?
Usually 20 years, and it is normally irrevocable during that period. The option can transfer with the property on a sale.
Do I need HMRC permission to opt to tax?
No. You notify HMRC within 30 days using the option to tax form. Permission is not required, but the notification must be kept.
Can I opt to tax residential property?
No. Residential property is excluded, along with certain charity buildings and some residential land. Mixed-use buildings need apportionment.
Does the tenant have to pay the VAT?
Yes, the rent becomes 20% more expensive. A VAT-registered tenant can usually recover it, but an unregistered tenant or a charity may not.
Does opting to tax affect stamp duty?
No. SDLT, LBTT and LTT are separate property transaction taxes. VAT on the property is calculated separately.
