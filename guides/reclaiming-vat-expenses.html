Reclaiming VAT on Business Expenses: Rules and Examples

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of Reclaiming VAT

If you are VAT-registered, much of the VAT you pay on business costs is recoverable. The exceptions are specific, but they are easy to trip over.

In short

You can usually reclaim VAT on business purchases if you hold a valid VAT invoice and the cost supports taxable supplies. Business entertainment, most cars and costs used for exempt supplies are blocked.

Input tax in one minute

Input tax is the VAT a VAT-registered business pays on goods and services it buys for the business. On each VAT return, input tax is offset against output tax. If output tax is higher, you pay the difference to HMRC. If input tax is higher, you usually reclaim the difference.

Reclaiming is not automatic. The purchase must be for business use, you normally need a valid VAT invoice, and the cost must relate to taxable supplies. That last condition is what links input tax to the nature of what you sell.

What is usually recoverable

Common business costs where VAT is normally recoverable include:

  • office supplies, printing and postage;
  • computer equipment, software and subscriptions used for the business;
  • accountancy and professional fees for the business;
  • advertising and website costs;
  • business travel, including train fares and hotel stays;
  • utilities and rent for business premises;
  • tools, materials and stock for resale.

The test is purpose, not category. A laptop used entirely for work is business use; a laptop used mainly for streaming films is not, or is only partly so.

What is blocked

Some VAT is specifically blocked, even for a registered business. The main categories are:

  • Business entertainment of UK clients. The VAT on entertaining UK business contacts is generally blocked. Entertaining overseas customers can be treated differently.
  • Most cars. VAT on a car available for private use is normally blocked, even if it is used for work. Vans and commercial vehicles follow different rules.
  • Goods and services used only for exempt supplies. If your output is exempt, the related input VAT is not recoverable.
  • Business gifts above the threshold. Gifts to the same person above £50 in a 12-month period can create an output tax liability.
  • Non-business activity. Private spending is not input tax at all.

The list is not exhaustive. If an expense is large or unusual, check the specific GOV.UK guidance before assuming it is recoverable.

A phone bill of £120 gross contains £20 of VAT. If the contract is 60% for business use, the reclaimable input tax is £12, not £20.

Mixed business and private use

When a cost serves both business and private purposes, you can usually reclaim only the business proportion. A phone contract used 60% for work allows 60% of the VAT to be reclaimed, provided you can support the split with records.

For motoring, there are special methods. Fuel VAT on a car used privately alongside business use normally requires a fuel scale charge, which adds a fixed amount to output tax. The charge depends on the car's CO2 emissions.

For premises with a private element, such as a home office, apportion by floor area or another reasonable measure, and keep a note of the method used.

Records and invoices

To reclaim input tax you normally need a valid VAT invoice showing the supplier's name, address and VAT number, the invoice number, the date, a description, the net amount, the VAT rate and the VAT amount.

For small purchases, HMRC can accept less formal evidence in limited circumstances, but relying on that is risky. Ask for a VAT receipt at the till, and keep it with the digital record.

If you use Making Tax Digital software, the invoice data usually enters the VAT account automatically. Check that the rate and the reclaimable proportion are correct before filing.

How to claim

  1. Confirm the business is VAT-registered and the cost is for business use.
  2. Check the expense is not in a blocked category.
  3. Apportion any private or exempt element.
  4. Obtain and keep a valid VAT invoice or receipt.
  5. Enter the input tax in the correct VAT period, based on the tax point.
  6. Keep the records for at least six years.

The VAT on expenses calculator extracts the VAT from a gross amount and lets you apply a reclaimable percentage. It is an arithmetic aid, not a ruling on whether the expense is eligible.

Common mistakes

  • Reclaiming VAT on client entertaining without checking the block.
  • Claiming the full VAT on a car used privately.
  • Reclaiming VAT on a purchase with no valid invoice.
  • Forgetting to apportion a home office or phone bill.
  • Claiming VAT on an expense that belongs to a previous VAT period.
  • Assuming a receipt showing only a total is enough to reclaim VAT.

Questions

Can I reclaim VAT on all business expenses?
No. Most business costs qualify, but some are blocked, including business entertainment of UK clients, most cars available for private use, and goods or services used only for exempt supplies.
What records do I need to reclaim VAT?
Keep a valid VAT invoice or receipt for each cost, note the business purpose, and record how any private-use apportionment was calculated. Records normally need to be kept for six years.
Can I reclaim VAT on a partly private expense?
Only the business proportion. If a phone is used 60% for work, 60% of the VAT is normally recoverable, provided the split is reasonable and supported by records.
