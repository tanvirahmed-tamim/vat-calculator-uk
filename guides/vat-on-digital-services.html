VAT on Digital Services: UK Rules for Online Sales

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of International and cross-border VAT

Digital services are easy to deliver across borders and hard to place for VAT. The customer's location and status usually decide whether you charge UK VAT, no VAT, or tax in another country.

In short

Digital services to UK consumers are standard-rated at 20% when supplied by a UK business. B2B digital supplies usually follow the reverse charge, and cross-border B2C supplies can require registration in the customer's country.

Who this is for: SaaS providers, app developers, online course creators, streaming services and digital publishers.

What counts as digital

Digital services are supplied electronically with minimal or no human intervention: the customer buys, downloads or streams without a person delivering the service. Examples include downloads, streaming, software as a service, e-books, automated online courses, web hosting and advertising space on a website.

Not everything delivered over the internet counts. A live webinar, a course with tutor support, a physical book, a booking service, legal advice sent by email and a PDF emailed by hand are outside the digital services rules. The label matters because B2C digital supplies follow the consumer's location, not the supplier's.

B2B supplies

A digital service supplied to a business usually follows the B2B place-of-supply rule: the customer's country. The customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge, and the supplier does not charge UK VAT on a cross-border supply.

A VAT registration number is the best evidence that the customer is in business. If there is no VAT number, you can accept other commercial evidence, such as a business website or contract. If you cannot evidence business status, treat the sale as B2C and charge the VAT due where the consumer belongs.

B2C supplies

A digital service supplied to a consumer is taxed where the consumer usually lives. A UK business selling to UK consumers charges 20%. A UK business selling to consumers in the EU must either register for the Non-Union VAT MOSS scheme in an EU member state or register for VAT in each country where it sells.

If you sell through a third-party platform or marketplace that sets the terms, authorises payment or handles delivery, the platform is responsible for accounting for the VAT instead of you. If you operate the platform, you are liable for the VAT unless you meet all the conditions for the supplier to remain responsible.

For example, a UK software business sells a subscription to a German business: no UK VAT is charged, and the German customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge. The same business sells to a German consumer: the sale is taxed in Germany, so the business uses the Non-Union scheme or registers locally.

Non-UK sellers

A non-UK business selling digital services to UK consumers must register for UK VAT, even without a UK establishment. There is no registration threshold for services supplied by businesses that are not established in the UK, so the domestic £90,000 figure is not a safe assumption.

A UK business buying digital services from a non-UK supplier usually accounts for the VAT under the reverse charge. A UK consumer pays UK VAT charged by the non-UK seller.

Evidence

Keep two pieces of evidence of where the consumer usually lives, such as a billing address, IP address, bank details, SIM country code or fixed-line location. The two pieces should not conflict. If they do, contact the consumer and resolve the discrepancy before deciding the rate.

Invoices should identify the customer, their country and, for a B2B sale, the VAT number and a note that the reverse charge applies. Keep the location evidence with the invoice and the transaction record.

Some situations have a presumption: a wifi hotspot, telephone box or hotel lobby is taxed where the hotspot is; a sale on board transport is taxed at the place of departure; a landline is taxed where the line is; a mobile sale follows the SIM country; and a decoder follows the postal address. Where a presumption applies, you do not need extra location evidence.

Common mistakes

  • Charging UK VAT on B2B digital services to an overseas business.
  • Assuming a consumer's location is the supplier's location.
  • Ignoring the non-UK seller registration rules for UK consumers.
  • Keeping only one piece of location evidence.

Questions

Do I charge VAT on digital services to UK consumers?
Yes, at 20% when you are a UK business selling to UK consumers. Digital services are standard-rated.
Do I charge VAT on digital services to an overseas business?
Usually no. The place of supply is the customer's country, and the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge.
Do non-UK sellers need a UK VAT number?
Yes, when selling digital services to UK consumers. There is no registration threshold for services supplied by businesses that are not established in the UK.
How many pieces of location evidence do I need?
Two, and they must not conflict, such as a billing address plus an IP address or bank details. If they conflict, contact the customer and resolve the discrepancy.
Are online courses digital services?
Automated courses are. A live webinar, or a course with live tutor support, is not a digital service under these rules.
Who accounts for VAT when I sell through a marketplace?
The platform is responsible when it sets the terms, authorises payment or handles delivery. If it does not meet those conditions, you remain responsible for the VAT.
