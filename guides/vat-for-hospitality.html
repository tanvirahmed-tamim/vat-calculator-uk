VAT for Hospitality: Food, Drink and Tourism Rates

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Hospitality sits on a set of fine lines: hot food versus cold, restaurant versus takeaway, room versus holiday let, and ordinary booking versus a tour operator package.

In short

Restaurant food, hot takeaways and hotel accommodation are standard-rated at 20%. Most cold takeaway food is zero-rated. Tour operators can use the Tour Operators Margin Scheme, and compulsory service charges are taxable.

Who this is for: Restaurants, cafes, takeaways, pubs, hotels, guest houses, holiday lets, event organisers and tour operators.

Food and drink

Food sold for consumption on the premises, such as a restaurant meal, is standard-rated at 20%. The same applies to catering supplied at an event. Most food sold cold for consumption off the premises is zero-rated, but confectionery, crisps, soft drinks and similar items are standard-rated.

Alcohol is always standard-rated. If a meal includes a drink, the VAT treatment of each element follows its own rules; a mixed supply can be treated as a single supply when the elements are inseparable.

The food boundary

Hospitality supplies and the usual rate
SupplyRateWhy
Meal eaten in a restaurant or cafe20%Consumption on premises
Hot takeaway food20%Heated or kept hot
Cold sandwich or salad, takeaway0%Ordinary zero-rated food
Confectionery, crisps, soft drinks20%Excluded from zero rating
Bottled water0%Food, not a soft drink
Alcohol20%Never zero-rated
Hotel room or holiday let20%Standard-rated accommodation

The boundary matters most for small outlets. If you sell both hot and cold food, keep the product ranges and the till records separate so the return can be supported. A single mixed counter does not make everything standard-rated, but poor records make the zero-rated share hard to defend.

Hot food that is not obviously hot

Food is standard-rated if it is heated to above ambient temperature for the purpose of being sold hot, or if it is kept hot. Food that has been cooked and then cooled for sale cold is not automatically caught. The test is the state of the food when it is sold, not how it was prepared.

A worked bill

A restaurant bill is £200 for food and drink, plus a compulsory 12.5% service charge of £25. The total is £225 net, and VAT at 20% is £45, giving £270 gross.

If the service charge were genuinely discretionary and paid straight to staff, it would generally be outside the scope of VAT. The more common arrangement, where the charge is added automatically and the business handles the money, is taxable.

Accommodation

Hotel accommodation, guest house rooms, bed and breakfast and holiday lets are standard-rated at 20% when you are VAT-registered. The temporary 5% rate ended on 30 September 2021; a 12.5% interim rate ran until 31 March 2022, and the standard rate returned on 1 April 2022.

A booking deposit is part of the accommodation supply and follows the same rate. If you also supply meals or parking, treat those elements according to their own rules.

Cancellation charges are not automatically outside the scope. HMRC's VAT Notice 709/3 treats a deposit as an advance payment, so VAT is due when it is received. If the guest cancels or does not arrive and you keep the money, the VAT stays due; it can only be reduced to the extent that you refund the customer. The same logic applies to a card guarantee that is charged after a no-show.

The furnished holiday lettings income tax regime was abolished from April 2025, but that did not change the VAT rate on holiday accommodation, which remains 20% once registered.

Tour operators

The Tour Operators Margin Scheme (TOMS) lets a tour operator account for VAT on the margin on certain travel packages rather than on the full selling price. It applies to travel, accommodation and other services bought in and resold as part of a package.

Under TOMS the invoice normally does not show VAT separately. The margin is calculated for the accounting period, and there are special rules for business customers and for sales outside the UK.

TOMS worked example

A package sells for £1,200 and the supplier costs are £900. The margin is £300. VAT at 20% on the margin is £50, found by multiplying £300 by 1/6. The customer pays £1,200, and the VAT is accounted for on the return rather than added to the invoice.

Service charges and tips

A compulsory service charge is taxable at the same rate as the principal supply, which for hospitality is 20%. A genuinely discretionary tip, where the customer is free to decide and the staff receive it, is outside the scope of VAT. Keep the treatment consistent and document it.

The distinction is about control, not wording. A line labelled "optional" that is added by default and retained by the business is still part of the price.

Event admission is standard-rated at 20%. Charity fundraising events can be exempt when the conditions are met, so check before charging VAT on a charity event.

What has changed

  • 2021–22: the temporary 5% rate for hospitality and tourism ended on 30 September 2021, a 12.5% interim rate ran to 31 March 2022, and the standard 20% rate returned on 1 April 2022.
  • 2025: the furnished holiday lettings income tax and corporation tax regime was abolished from April 2025; the VAT treatment of holiday accommodation did not change.
  • 2026: the temporary zero rate for domestic electricity in Great Britain affects utility bills, not the VAT rate on food, rooms or events.

Common mistakes

  • Treating all takeaway food as zero-rated when hot food is standard-rated.
  • Applying the old 5% hospitality rate after 30 September 2021.
  • Showing VAT separately on a TOMS invoice.
  • Treating a compulsory service charge as a discretionary tip.
  • Assuming the 2025 furnished holiday lettings change cut VAT on holiday lets.

Related terms

Tour Operators Margin Scheme, Standard rate, Zero-rated.

Questions

Is takeaway food zero-rated?
Only cold takeaway food, and not all of it. Hot takeaway food is standard-rated at 20%, and confectionery, crisps and soft drinks are also standard-rated.
Is VAT charged on hotel rooms?
Yes, at 20% when the hotel is VAT-registered. The temporary 5% rate ended on 30 September 2021, a 12.5% interim rate ran to 31 March 2022, and the standard rate returned on 1 April 2022.
What is TOMS?
The Tour Operators Margin Scheme. It lets a tour operator account for VAT on the margin on certain travel packages instead of the full selling price.
Do I charge VAT on tips?
A compulsory service charge is taxable at the same rate as the principal supply, usually 20%. A genuinely discretionary tip paid to staff is outside the scope of VAT.
Do I charge VAT on event tickets?
Yes, admission is standard-rated at 20%. Some charity fundraising events can be exempt when the conditions are met.
