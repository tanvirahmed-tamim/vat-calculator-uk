How to Remove VAT From a Gross Price: Formula and Examples

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 5 min read

Part of VAT calculation

Removing VAT is the reverse of adding it. The common mistake is subtracting the rate from the gross, which always gives too low a net figure.

In short

Divide the gross amount by 1 plus the rate. At 20%, divide by 1.20: £120 becomes £100 net with £20 VAT. At 5%, divide by 1.05: £105 becomes £100 with £5 VAT.

Why subtracting 20% fails

Take a gross price of £120 at 20%. Subtracting 20% gives £96. That feels right, but it is wrong. The VAT was calculated on the net price, which is lower than the gross, so 20% of the gross is too much to remove.

At 20%, VAT is one-sixth of the gross amount, not one-fifth. The correct net is £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100, and the VAT is £20. Subtracting 20% from £120 removes £24 instead of £20.

The formula

To remove VAT from a gross amount:

Net = Gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)

At 20% the divisor is 1.20; at 5% it is 1.05. A historical 17.5% rate uses 1.175. The VAT element is the gross minus the net.

The VAT fraction

If you only need the VAT amount, multiply the gross by the VAT fraction: rate ÷ (100 + rate).

  • At 20%: 20 ÷ 120 = 1/6. VAT on £120 is £20.
  • At 5%: 5 ÷ 105 = 1/21. VAT on £105 is £5.
  • At 17.5% (historical, pre-2011): 17.5 ÷ 117.5 = 7/47. VAT on £117.50 is £17.50.

The VAT fraction calculator does this for any gross amount and rate.

Pence need care. A gross of £10.00 at 20% gives a net of £8.333…, which rounds to £8.33, leaving VAT of £1.67. The calculator rounds both amounts so they always add back to the gross.

When to use it

  • Checking whether a quoted inclusive price matches the net price on an invoice.
  • Working out the input tax on a receipt that only shows the total.
  • Entering gross takings into bookkeeping software that expects net figures.
  • Comparing inclusive and exclusive quotes from different suppliers.

Caveats

Not every gross price contains VAT you can extract this way. Margin scheme invoices normally do not show VAT separately, and the VAT is calculated on the dealer's margin. Exempt and zero-rated supplies contain no VAT to remove, even though the price is gross. When an invoice mixes rates, split the lines by rate before removing VAT.

For second-hand goods, use the margin scheme calculator. For a receipt with VAT, the expenses calculator turns the gross into input tax.

Questions

Why is subtracting 20% from a gross price wrong?
Because VAT is calculated on the net amount, not the gross. At 20% the VAT is one sixth of the gross, so £120 contains £20 of VAT and £100 net. Subtracting 20% would remove £24 and leave £96.
How do I remove 5% VAT?
Divide the gross amount by 1.05, or multiply it by the fraction 1/21. A gross of £105 becomes £100 net with £5 of VAT.
Does this method work for margin scheme invoices?
No. A margin scheme invoice normally does not show VAT separately, and VAT is calculated on the dealer's margin rather than the full selling price. Use the margin scheme calculator instead.
