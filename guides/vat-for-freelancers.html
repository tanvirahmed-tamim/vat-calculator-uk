VAT for Freelancers and Sole Traders: Rules and Thresholds

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Freelancers follow the same VAT rules as any other business: register above £90,000, charge the right rate, and reclaim VAT on business costs. The differences lie in registration timing, expenses and clients abroad.

In short

Register when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months, or when you expect it to exceed it in the next 30 days. Most freelance services are standard-rated at 20%; B2B services to overseas clients are usually outside the scope of UK VAT. Once registered, you must charge VAT from the effective date and file returns through compatible software.

Who this is for: Sole traders, freelancers and independent contractors who sell services, including consultants, designers, writers and developers.

When to register

Registration is compulsory when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, or when you expect it to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. Taxable turnover includes standard, reduced and zero-rated sales, but not exempt sales or income outside the scope of VAT.

The threshold has been £90,000 since 1 April 2024, when it rose from £85,000. Guidance quoting £85,000 predates that change.

Tell HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you crossed the threshold. If you cross it because you expect a future sale, the deadline runs from the date the expectation arose. The registration threshold guide works through both tests.

Voluntary registration below the threshold can be useful if you sell to other businesses and want to reclaim input tax, but it means charging 20% to consumers.

A worked timeline

Crossing the threshold on the backward look
DateWhat happens
MarchRolling 12-month turnover passes £90,000
By 30 AprilNotify HMRC, within 30 days of the month end
1 MayRegistration is effective from the first day of the second month
From 1 MayCharge VAT on invoices and file returns

That is the normal backward-look pattern. If you register because you expect to cross the threshold in the next 30 days, registration is effective from the date the expectation arose, not from a later month boundary.

Late registration is expensive. You must account for VAT on sales from the date registration should have taken effect, even if you never charged the customer. If your prices were quoted inclusive, that VAT comes out of your own margin.

Charging VAT

Most freelance services are standard-rated at 20%. Add VAT to the net fee and show it separately on the invoice. A day rate of £400 becomes £480 gross at 20%.

Some services are exempt, such as certain education and healthcare, and some are outside the scope. Check the liability before assuming 20%. If you are unsure, the rates guide explains the bands.

Expenses

Reclaim VAT on business costs where you hold a valid invoice and the cost is for business use: software, hardware, office costs, travel, insurance and marketing.

Blocked items include business entertainment of UK clients, most cars, and private spending. For mixed-use costs, claim only the business proportion. You can normally claim input tax up to four years after the return period it relates to.

Homeworking is a common grey area. If you work from home, you can reclaim VAT on business costs such as a dedicated broadband line or office equipment, but not on the domestic energy bill itself simply because you work there.

The VAT on expenses calculator extracts the VAT from a gross cost and applies a business-use percentage.

Overseas clients

For B2B services, the place of supply is generally where the customer belongs, so you do not charge UK VAT. The customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge in its own country.

For B2C services, the place of supply is generally where you belong, so UK VAT applies. Digital services to consumers have their own rules.

Keep evidence of the customer's business status and location, such as a contract, a website or a VAT number. The professional services guide covers the place-of-supply tests in more detail.

The reverse charge also runs the other way. If you buy services from a supplier abroad, you may have to account for UK VAT on the purchase and reclaim it on the same return. Record both sides so the return balances.

Common mistakes

  • Registering late, after the 30-day notification window has closed.
  • Assuming late registration only affects future invoices, when it also covers past sales.
  • Treating every invoice as standard-rated without checking for exempt or outside-scope services.
  • Claiming VAT on costs without a valid VAT invoice or receipt.
  • Ignoring the reverse charge on services bought from suppliers abroad.

Questions

Do I have to register for VAT as a sole trader?
Only if taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months or is expected to exceed it in the next 30 days. Sole trader status does not exempt you.
Can I charge VAT before I am registered?
No. You cannot charge VAT until your registration is effective. If you need to invoice VAT before then, delay the invoice or register voluntarily first.
Can I reclaim VAT on a laptop?
Yes, if it is bought for business use and you hold a valid VAT invoice. If you also use it privately, claim only the business proportion.
Do I charge VAT to a client in the US?
Usually no for B2B services, because the place of supply is the US. Keep evidence that the client is a business.
Do I need Making Tax Digital?
Yes, once VAT-registered. File returns through compatible software that keeps digital records.
