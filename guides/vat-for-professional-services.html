VAT for Professional Services and Overseas Clients

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

Part of VAT by business type

Professional services are usually standard-rated when supplied in the UK. The difficult part is cross-border work, where the place of supply decides whether you charge UK VAT at all.

In short

Professional services are standard-rated at 20% when supplied in the UK. For B2B services to overseas clients, the place of supply is usually the customer's country, so no UK VAT is charged. Disbursements are outside the scope.

Who this is for: Consultants, accountants, lawyers, architects, agencies and other professional service firms with UK or overseas clients.

Place of supply

VAT is charged where a supply is made. For services, the place of supply usually depends on whether the customer is a business or a consumer, and where the customer belongs. The place of supply guide sets out the full decision table.

For most B2B services, the place of supply is where the customer belongs. For most B2C services, it is where the supplier belongs. Land, events, transport and digital services have special rules.

B2B and B2C clients

A UK consultancy invoicing a French company for B2B services does not charge UK VAT. The French customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge. A UK consultancy invoicing a French consumer usually charges UK VAT at 20%.

Keep evidence of the client's business status and where they belong. A VAT number, a contract, a business website and correspondence are all useful. If the client cannot evidence business status, treat the supply as B2C and apply the consumer rules.

If you receive professional services from an overseas supplier, you usually account for the VAT under the reverse charge: output tax in box 1, the same amount as input tax in box 4, and the value in boxes 6 and 7.

Goods and exports

Goods exported from the UK are zero-rated when you keep evidence that they left the UK. There is no equivalent general zero rate for services: a service is either taxed where it is supplied or outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules.

If you sell goods to an overseas consumer and deliver them abroad, the zero rate can apply, but the evidence requirements are strict. Keep the export declaration, shipping documents and customs entry.

Invoices

Show the client's details, their country and, for a B2B sale, their VAT number and a note that the reverse charge applies. Do not show UK VAT on a supply that is outside the scope.

For a B2C service that is taxed in the UK, show VAT at 20% as normal. For zero-rated goods, show the zero rate and keep the export evidence with the invoice.

Disbursements

A genuine disbursement is a cost you pay on behalf of a client and recharge at exactly the same amount. It is outside the scope of VAT, provided you acted as the client's agent, the client was charged the supplier's price, and the cost is separately identified.

A recharge with a mark-up, or a cost you bought in your own name, is part of your supply and follows its VAT treatment. Do not treat every expense as a disbursement.

Evidence

For services, the evidence is about the client: who they are, where they belong and whether they are in business. For zero-rated exports of goods, keep shipping and customs evidence. HMRC can ask for it years later.

Without evidence, a B2B treatment can fail and a zero-rated export can be reassessed as a standard-rated sale.

Common mistakes

  • Charging UK VAT on B2B services to an overseas business.
  • Not charging UK VAT on B2C services to an overseas consumer.
  • Treating a marked-up recharge as an outside-scope disbursement.
  • Failing to keep evidence of the customer's business status and location.

Questions

Do I charge VAT to an overseas business client?
Usually no for B2B services, because the place of supply is the customer's country. The customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge.
Do I charge VAT to an overseas consumer?
Usually yes for B2C services, because the place of supply is where you belong. Digital services have separate rules.
What is a disbursement?
A cost you pay on behalf of a client and recharge at exactly the same amount. It is outside the scope of VAT when the strict conditions are met.
How do I prove where a client belongs?
Keep the contract, the client's business website, a VAT number and correspondence. HMRC can ask for the evidence later.
Does the reverse charge apply to my purchases from abroad?
Yes, for most B2B services bought from overseas suppliers. You account for the deemed VAT on your VAT return, subject to the normal recovery rules.
Do I charge VAT on goods sent to an overseas client?
Exports of goods are usually zero-rated when you keep evidence that they left the UK. Keep the export declaration and shipping documents.
What should an invoice to an overseas client show?
The client's details and country, their VAT number for a B2B sale, and a note that the reverse charge applies. Do not show UK VAT on an outside-scope supply.
