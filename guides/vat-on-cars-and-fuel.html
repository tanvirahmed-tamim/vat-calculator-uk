VAT on Cars and Fuel: Blocked Input Tax and Scale Charges

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

Cars are the clearest example of a blocked cost in VAT. Even a VAT-registered business usually cannot reclaim the VAT on buying a car, and reclaiming fuel VAT brings its own charge.

In short

VAT on most cars is blocked because the car is assumed to be available for private use. Commercial vehicles are usually recoverable. If you reclaim fuel VAT for a car with private use, you account for a CO2-based fuel scale charge.

Who this is for: Businesses that buy, lease or run cars and vans, including sole traders, companies and motor traders.

Cars and input tax

VAT on buying most cars is blocked, even when the business is VAT-registered and the car is used for work. The reason is that a car is assumed to be available for private use unless the evidence shows otherwise.

The block applies to the purchase of the car, not to everything connected with it. Servicing, repairs, tyres and accessories are usually standard-rated and recoverable in the normal way, subject to private-use apportionment.

Exceptions

There are exceptions where VAT on a car can be recovered:

  • Cars used exclusively for business, with no private use at all.
  • Taxis and private hire vehicles.
  • Driving school cars.
  • Pool cars that meet the strict conditions.
  • Certain cars bought for resale or by a motor trader.

The exception must be evidenced. A pool car, for example, must not be kept at an employee's home and must be used for business journeys only.

Leasing

When a car is leased and there is private use, 50% of the VAT on the lease is normally recoverable. The other 50% is blocked. If the car is used exclusively for business, the full VAT may be recoverable.

The 50% rule applies to the leasing charge, not to repairs or servicing. Keep the lease agreement and the VAT invoices with the vehicle records.

Fuel scale charge

If you reclaim VAT on fuel used in a car that is available for private use, you must account for a fuel scale charge. The charge is a fixed amount of output tax based on the car's CO2 emissions, not on the actual fuel used.

The alternative is to reclaim no VAT on fuel and keep private and business fuel separate. Most businesses use the scale charge because it avoids mileage records for fuel.

The scale charge table is updated each year. Use the current table for the period, and do not carry forward an old figure. The fuel scale charge guide works through the calculation.

Records

Keep the purchase invoice, the lease, the fuel receipts and the scale charge calculation. For a car used exclusively for business, keep evidence of that exclusive use, because the default assumption is private availability.

Motor traders have additional rules for the margin scheme and stock vehicles. The motor trade guide covers those.

Common mistakes

  • Reclaiming VAT on a car that is available for private use.
  • Applying the 50% rule to repairs instead of the lease.
  • Reclaiming fuel VAT without accounting for the scale charge.
  • Using an old scale charge table after the annual update.

Questions

Can I reclaim VAT on a car?
Usually no, because most cars are assumed to be available for private use. There are exceptions for cars used exclusively for business, taxis, driving school cars and qualifying pool cars.
How much VAT can I reclaim on a leased car?
Usually 50% when the car has private use. The other 50% is blocked. If the car is used exclusively for business, the full VAT may be recoverable.
Can I reclaim VAT on a van?
Yes, normally, when the van is for business use and you hold a valid VAT invoice. Vans are not subject to the same block as cars.
What is the fuel scale charge?
A fixed amount of output tax a business accounts for when it reclaims VAT on fuel used in a car available for private use. It is based on CO2 emissions.
Do I have to use the fuel scale charge?
No. You can choose not to reclaim VAT on fuel and keep private and business fuel separate. Most businesses use the scale charge because it is simpler.
Is VAT charged on car repairs?
Yes, at 20%. Repairs and servicing are standard-rated and can normally be recovered, subject to private-use apportionment.
