VAT registration, returns and compliance

Maintained by the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 9 min read

Registration, invoicing and filing are the practical obligations that sit behind the calculation. This collection covers the threshold test, the paperwork an invoice must carry, Making Tax Digital and the records HMRC expects you to keep.

In short

You must register for VAT when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, or is expected to exceed it in the next 30 days. Returns are usually quarterly and filed through Making Tax Digital software. VAT invoices must show the supplier's VAT number, the tax point, the rate and the VAT amount.

The registration test

The registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period. It is not a calendar-year or tax-year test: you look back at the previous 12 months each time, so a strong month can push you over the line.

There is a second, forward-looking test. If you expect your taxable turnover to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone, you must register even if the last 12 months are below the threshold. Once registered, you charge VAT and can usually reclaim it on business purchases.

The registration threshold calculator checks a rolling turnover figure, and the threshold guide explains the voluntary and deregistration options.

Registering and deregistering

You normally have to tell HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you exceeded the threshold, and registration takes effect from the first day of the second month after you crossed it. You can also register voluntarily before you reach the threshold, which can be useful if you buy a lot from other VAT-registered businesses.

Deregistration uses a lower threshold of £88,000, and the test looks forward. You can ask HMRC to cancel if you expect taxable turnover in the next 12 months to stay below it. The gap between the two thresholds is deliberate, to stop businesses repeatedly registering and deregistering.

What a VAT invoice must show

A valid VAT invoice normally includes the supplier's name, address and VAT number, a unique invoice number, the tax point, the customer's name and address, a description of the supply, the net total, the rate and amount of VAT, and the gross total.

Making Tax Digital

VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and file their VAT returns through compatible Making Tax Digital software. Spreadsheets can still be used, but they must be linked to the filing software. Filing by post or through the old HMRC portal is no longer available for most businesses.

Deadlines, penalties and records

Returns and payments are usually due one calendar month and seven days after the end of the period. Late payment and late submission each attract penalties and interest, and the longer a return is outstanding the more it costs. Keep records for six years: invoices, credit notes, import and export documents, and your digital records.

Questions

When do I have to register for VAT?
You must register when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12 months, or when you expect it to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days. Tell HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you crossed the threshold.
Can I register for VAT voluntarily?
Yes. You can register before you reach the threshold, which can help if you buy a lot from other VAT-registered businesses and want to reclaim input tax. Once registered, you must charge VAT on your taxable sales.
What happens if I file my VAT return late?
Late submission and late payment each attract penalties and interest, and the cost rises the longer the return is outstanding. File through Making Tax Digital software before the deadline to avoid them.
How long must I keep VAT records?
Normally six years. Keep VAT invoices, credit notes, import and export documents and your digital records so you can support every figure on your return.
