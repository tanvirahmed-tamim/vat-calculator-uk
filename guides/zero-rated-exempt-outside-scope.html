Zero-Rated, Exempt and Outside the Scope of VAT Explained

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 19 November 2026 · 6 min read

Part of UK VAT rates

All three can look like 'no VAT' on an invoice, but they are treated very differently on a VAT return. Confusing them can cost a business money.

In short

Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, count towards the registration threshold and usually allow input VAT recovery. Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but charged no VAT, and they generally block input tax recovery. Out of scope means there is no VAT supply at all.

The definitions

A zero-rated supply is a taxable supply charged at 0%. It is inside the VAT system: it goes on the VAT return, it counts as taxable turnover, and it can carry input VAT recovery.

An exempt supply is also within the scope of VAT, but no VAT is charged on it. It is not a taxable supply, so it does not count towards the registration threshold, and input VAT on costs used to make it is generally blocked. It is still reported on the VAT return, in box 6.

A third category, out of scope, is different again: there is no supply for VAT purposes at all, for example a private sale or certain business transfers.

What goes in box 6

Box 6 asks for the total value of sales and all other outputs, excluding VAT. HMRC's VAT Notice 700/12 paragraph 3.7 lists zero-rated, reduced-rate and exempt supplies as examples of what belongs there. There is no separate exempt turnover box on the VAT return.

So an exempt sale is not omitted from the return. What it does not do is create output tax in box 1, and it does not count as taxable turnover for the registration test.

This is the point that most often trips people up: exempt sales appear in box 6 alongside taxable sales, even though no VAT is charged on them.

Input tax recovery

If you make only taxable supplies, including zero-rated ones, you can normally reclaim VAT on your business purchases in full.

If you make exempt supplies, the VAT on costs used to make them is generally not recoverable. That includes a share of overheads such as rent, software and professional fees when those costs support exempt activity.

A business that sells zero-rated children's clothing can usually reclaim the VAT on hangers, packaging and shop fittings. A business that sells exempt insurance products generally cannot reclaim the VAT on its office costs.

The registration threshold

The £90,000 registration threshold is based on taxable turnover. Zero-rated sales count; exempt sales do not. A business with £200,000 of exempt income is not required to register on that basis alone, while a business with £95,000 of zero-rated sales is.

This is one of the most practical differences between the two categories, and a common source of error when a business starts making a new type of supply.

Partial exemption

Businesses that make both taxable and exempt supplies are partly exempt. They must apportion input VAT and usually apply a partial exemption method agreed with HMRC.

There is a de minimis test: if exempt input tax is small enough, it can be treated as recoverable in full. The calculation is technical, and errors can be expensive, so most partly exempt businesses use an accountant or specialist software.

A worked comparison

A business has 12 months of sales made up of £80,000 of zero-rated books and £30,000 of exempt insurance commission. Its taxable turnover is £80,000, so it is below the £90,000 registration threshold. Box 6 still reports £110,000, because both the zero-rated and the exempt sales belong there.

Working out which category applies

  1. Is there a supply for VAT purposes at all? If not, it is out of scope.
  2. Does an exempting provision cover it, such as insurance, finance, education or health? If so, it is exempt.
  3. Does a zero-rating provision cover it, such as most food or printed books? If so, it is zero-rated.
  4. If none of those apply, it is standard-rated or reduced-rated according to the specific rules for that supply.

Records and wording

A zero-rated invoice should still show the 0% rate and the VAT amount of £0.00. An exempt invoice should not charge VAT at all. Do not label an exempt supply as zero-rated, and do not describe a zero-rated supply as exempt: the wording affects how customers and software treat it.

Keep records that show why a supply is in a particular category. For a borderline product, a copy of the relevant GOV.UK guidance or a written ruling is worth keeping.

Questions

Can I reclaim VAT on costs related to zero-rated sales?
Usually yes. Zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies, so VAT on related purchases is generally recoverable in full, subject to the normal rules on business use and records.
Can I reclaim VAT on costs related to exempt sales?
Generally no. Exempt supplies do not carry VAT, so input tax linked to them is normally blocked. Partially exempt businesses use an apportionment method to work out what can be recovered.
Do zero-rated sales go on the VAT return?
Yes. Zero-rated sales are included in box 6 as taxable turnover, even though the VAT charged is £0.00. Exempt sales also go in box 6, but they are not taxable turnover for the registration threshold.
