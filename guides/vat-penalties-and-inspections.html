VAT Penalties and Inspections: What Happens Next

By the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

Part of Registration, returns and compliance

VAT penalties are not one charge. Late filing, late payment, errors and deliberate behaviour each have their own regime, and interest runs alongside them. Knowing which rule applies is the first step to limiting the damage.

In short

Late submission is points-based: £200 once you reach the threshold, then £200 for each later late return. For payments due on or after 1 April 2025, late payment adds 3% at day 15 and another 3% at day 30, then 10% a year from day 31, plus interest. Inaccuracy penalties range from 0% to 100% depending on behaviour and disclosure.

Who this is for: VAT-registered businesses that have missed a deadline, discovered an error, received a penalty letter, or want to understand what an HMRC inspection involves.

Late filing

Since 1 January 2023, late VAT returns are dealt with by a points system rather than an automatic fine. You get one point each time a return is late. When the number of points reaches the threshold for your filing frequency, HMRC charges a £200 penalty, and a further £200 for each subsequent late return while the points remain active.

The thresholds are 4 points for quarterly filers, 5 for monthly filers and 2 for annual filers. Points expire after 24 months if you keep filing on time, and the penalty resets once you have a clean period. A nil return is still a return, so a missed nil return earns a point.

The date that matters is the date HMRC receives the return, not the date you send it. The deadline is usually one calendar month and 7 days after the end of the accounting period.

Late payment

Late payment penalties are separate from late filing. The current regime applies to payments due on or after 1 April 2025. There is no penalty for the first 15 days. If the VAT is still unpaid at day 15, the first penalty is 3% of the amount outstanding. If it is still unpaid at day 30, a further 3% is added to the amount still outstanding at that date.

From day 31, a second penalty accrues daily at an annual rate of 10% on the outstanding balance until it is paid. A payment plan agreed with HMRC can reduce or remove these penalties, so contact HMRC early rather than waiting for the letter.

Earlier periods used a different structure: 2% at day 15, 2% at day 30 and 4% a year from day 31. Do not apply the current percentages to a payment that was due before 1 April 2025.

The penalty is based on the tax outstanding, not on your turnover. A business that owes £15,000 and pays 51 days late faces roughly £900 in first penalties plus about £86 in second penalties, before interest.

Interest

Late payment interest runs from the due date until the tax is paid. For periods from 6 April 2025 the rate is the Bank of England base rate plus 4%; before that date it was base rate plus 2.5%. It is charged daily and is separate from the penalty. Interest is not tax, so it is not shown on the VAT return and cannot be reclaimed as input tax.

If HMRC owes you a repayment, repayment interest may be paid at a lower rate. The two rates are set by HMRC and can change, so check the current rate on GOV.UK.

Inaccuracy penalties

Errors on a return can attract a penalty based on behaviour. A careless error can attract up to 30% of the tax, a deliberate error up to 70%, and a deliberate error that is concealed up to 100%. The actual percentage is reduced for telling HMRC, helping HMRC and correcting the error promptly.

A genuine mistake with a reasonable excuse may avoid a penalty altogether, but "reasonable excuse" is interpreted narrowly. Relying on an adviser is not usually a reasonable excuse, and neither is a lack of knowledge when the obligation was clear.

The error correction guide explains when you can fix an error on your next return and when you must notify HMRC separately.

Inspections

HMRC can check your VAT records and, in some cases, visit your premises. A routine inspection usually starts with a letter and a request for records: sales and purchase invoices, the VAT account, bank statements, contracts and import or export paperwork.

The officer is looking for evidence that the returns match the records, that the right rate was used, and that input tax was claimed only where it is due. Keep the records organised by period and be ready to explain any unusual entries. You can ask for the scope of the check in advance and for time to produce documents.

Appeals

Most penalty letters offer a review and then an appeal to the tax tribunal. You normally have 30 days from the date of the decision to ask for a review. Paying the tax does not prevent you from appealing the penalty, and a successful appeal can cancel or reduce it.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a nil return cannot be late.
  • Waiting for a penalty letter before asking for a payment plan.
  • Treating interest as reclaimable input tax.
  • Ignoring a penalty letter until the appeal window has closed.

Questions

Is there a penalty for filing a VAT return late?
Yes, but it is points-based. You get one point per late return, and a £200 penalty applies only when you reach the threshold: 4 points quarterly, 5 monthly or 2 annually.
How much is the late payment penalty for VAT?
For payments due on or after 1 April 2025, there is no penalty for the first 15 days. At day 15 the first penalty is 3% of the amount outstanding, with a further 3% at day 30. From day 31 a second penalty accrues at 10% a year. Earlier periods used 2%, 2% and 4%.
Do I pay interest as well as a penalty?
Yes. Late payment interest runs from the due date at the Bank of England base rate plus 4% for periods from 6 April 2025, and plus 2.5% before that. It is charged separately from any penalty.
Can I appeal a VAT penalty?
Yes. Most penalty letters offer a review, and you can appeal to the tax tribunal. You normally have 30 days from the date of the decision.
What counts as a reasonable excuse?
Something unexpected and outside your control, such as a serious illness, a bereavement or a system failure, that you could not reasonably have avoided. Relying on an adviser is not usually enough.
How far back can HMRC inspect VAT records?
HMRC can normally assess VAT for the last 4 years, and up to 20 years where the error is deliberate. Records must normally be kept for 6 years.
