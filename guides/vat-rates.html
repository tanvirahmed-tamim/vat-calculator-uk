UK VAT rates and liability

Maintained by the VAT in UK team · Last reviewed 10 November 2026 · 8 min read

The UK has three VAT rates and a separate category of exempt supplies. Choosing the right one decides how much you charge, what goes on the return and whether you can reclaim related input tax. This collection brings the bands and the history together.

In short

The UK standard VAT rate is 20%, unchanged since 4 January 2011. A reduced rate of 5% applies to a defined list including domestic energy and children's car seats. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, while exempt supplies are inside VAT but charged no VAT. Zero-rated is not the same as exempt.

The four outcomes

Every UK supply falls into one of four outcomes for VAT. The rate decides the multiplier you apply to a net price and whether the sale belongs on the VAT return.

What the standard rate covers

The 20% standard rate is the default for most goods and services, including professional fees, consultancy, electronics, adult clothing, furniture, most software, most repairs and hotel accommodation outside the temporary reduced-rate periods. If no relief names your supply, standard rate applies.

What the reduced rate covers

The 5% reduced rate applies to a specific list, including:

  • domestic energy such as gas and electricity;
  • children's car seats;
  • some residential renovations and conversions;
  • certain energy-saving materials and heating systems;
  • some mobility aids and health-related installations.

The conditions are detailed. For renovations, the age of the building, its previous use and the type of work all matter, so check the specific rules before you charge 5%.

What the zero rate covers

Zero-rated supplies include:

  • most food for human consumption, excluding catering, hot food, confectionery, alcohol and soft drinks;
  • printed books, newspapers and certain periodicals;
  • children's clothing and footwear;
  • most drugs and medicines supplied on prescription;
  • certain aids for disabled people.

Zero-rated is not the same as exempt. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, so they belong on the VAT return and you can usually reclaim the VAT on related purchases.

Exempt and out of scope

Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but no VAT is charged on them. Common examples include:

  • most financial and insurance services;
  • certain education and training;
  • some healthcare services;
  • burial and cremation services;
  • certain property transactions.

Out of scope is different again: there is no supply for VAT purposes at all, for example a private sale between individuals or some transfers of a business as a going concern.

The difference matters most when you are working out how much VAT you can reclaim on your own costs. The zero-rated vs exempt guide works through the consequences for input tax and partial exemption.

How to check a borderline supply

  1. Identify the exact supply and what the customer actually receives.
  2. Check the GOV.UK VAT rates guidance and the notice for that category.
  3. Look for a reduced-rate or zero-rate relief that names your product.
  4. If the supply is mixed, split it into separate elements before choosing a rate.
  5. For a large or uncertain supply, ask HMRC for a ruling or take advice.

Some products sit on the edge of a category. The classic example is the Jaffa Cake, which HMRC argued was a biscuit while the manufacturer argued it was a cake, and the tribunal agreed it was a cake.

Cereal bars, smoothies, flavoured water, hot takeaway food and digital publications have all produced disputes. The label on the packaging is not decisive; the nature of the product is.

The VAT calculator lets you apply any rate, but the rate itself is a legal question, not an arithmetic one.

Temporary rates and history

Rates can change by law. The standard rate was cut to 15% between December 2008 and December 2009, and a temporary 5% rate applied to hospitality, holiday accommodation and attractions from July 2020 to September 2021. The full rate history lists every change since VAT began on 1 April 1973 at 10%.

Questions

What is the current UK VAT rate?
The standard VAT rate is 20%. A reduced rate of 5% applies to a defined list, including domestic energy and children's car seats, and some supplies are zero rated at 0%.
Is zero-rated the same as exempt?
No. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, so they appear on the VAT return and usually allow input tax recovery. Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but charged no VAT, and they generally block input tax recovery.
How do I know which rate applies to my product?
Identify the exact supply, check the GOV.UK VAT rates guidance for that category, and look for a relief that names your product. If the supply is mixed, split it before choosing a rate.
When did VAT last change?
The standard rate has been 20% since 4 January 2011. Temporary reduced rates applied from December 2008 to December 2009 at 15%, and to hospitality and tourism at 5% from July 2020 to September 2021.
