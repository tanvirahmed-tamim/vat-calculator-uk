UK VAT frequently asked questions

VAT basics

What is VAT?

VAT is a consumption tax charged on most goods and services supplied by VAT-registered businesses in the UK. A business charges VAT on its sales (output tax), reclaims VAT on eligible purchases (input tax) and pays the difference to HMRC. The standard rate is 20%.

Who has to charge VAT?

Any business registered for VAT must charge it on its taxable supplies, unless a specific scheme or rule says otherwise. Registration is compulsory once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in a rolling 12 months, or is expected to pass it in the next 30 days.

Registration threshold guide

What is the difference between output tax and input tax?

Output tax is the VAT you charge customers on your sales. Input tax is the VAT you pay on business purchases. On a VAT return you normally subtract input tax from output tax and pay the difference to HMRC, or reclaim it if input tax is higher.

How VAT is calculated

What is a taxable supply?

A taxable supply is a supply of goods or services made in the course of business that is not exempt. It includes standard-rated, reduced-rate and zero-rated supplies. Exempt supplies and supplies outside the scope of VAT are not taxable supplies.

UK VAT rates

What is the tax point?

The tax point is the date that decides which VAT rate and VAT period apply to a supply. It is usually the earlier of the invoice date or the date payment is received, but special rules apply to deposits and continuous supplies.

VAT invoices and MTD

What is a VAT invoice?

A VAT invoice is a document that records the supplier, the customer, the goods or services, the net amount, the VAT rate and the VAT charged. A valid VAT invoice is normally required before a business can reclaim input tax.

VAT invoice rules

Is VAT the same in all four nations?

Yes. VAT is a UK-wide tax, so the rates and registration threshold are the same in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Property taxes and income tax rates differ, but they are not VAT.

VAT across the UK

What is the standard VAT rate?

The standard rate is 20%. It applies to most goods and services unless a specific relief moves the supply into the reduced or zero band. It has been 20% since 4 January 2011.

VAT rates explained

Rates and supplies

What are the UK VAT rates?

The standard rate is 20%, the reduced rate is 5% and the zero rate is 0%. Some supplies are exempt or outside the scope. The rates apply across the UK, although the temporary zero rate for domestic electricity in Great Britain is an exception.

The rate table

What is the reduced rate?

The reduced rate is 5%. It applies to a limited list of supplies, including domestic gas and most domestic fuel, children's car seats, some home renovations and certain energy-saving materials.

Reduced-rate supplies

What is zero-rated?

Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%. They include most food, printed books, newspapers and children's clothing. Because they are taxable, they belong on the VAT return and usually allow input tax recovery.

Zero-rated vs exempt

What is exempt?

Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but no VAT is charged on them. Common examples include many financial and insurance services, certain education and some healthcare. You generally cannot reclaim VAT on costs used to make exempt supplies.

Exempt supplies explained

Is zero-rated the same as exempt?

No. Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, so input tax is usually recoverable. Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but charged no VAT, so related input tax is generally blocked. The distinction matters most for partly exempt businesses.

The difference

Can the VAT rate change?

Yes. VAT rates are set by the government and can change at a Budget or in an emergency. The standard rate has changed several times since 1973, most recently to 20% on 4 January 2011.

VAT rate history

Is food zero-rated?

Most cold food for home consumption is zero-rated, but not all food. Catering, hot takeaway food, confectionery, crisps, ice cream, soft drinks and alcohol are standard-rated at 20%.

VAT on food and drink

Is takeaway food zero-rated?

Only cold takeaway food, and not all of it. Hot takeaway food is standard-rated at 20%, and confectionery, crisps and soft drinks are also standard-rated.

Takeaway VAT rules

Is VAT charged on hotel rooms?

Yes, at 20% when the hotel is VAT-registered. The temporary 5% rate ended on 30 September 2021, a 12.5% interim rate ran to 31 March 2022, and the standard rate returned on 1 April 2022.

VAT for hospitality

Is VAT charged on domestic electricity?

In Great Britain, qualifying domestic electricity is temporarily zero-rated from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027. In Northern Ireland it remains at the reduced rate of 5%. Gas and other domestic fuels stay at 5% across the UK.

VAT on energy

Is business energy standard-rated?

Yes, business electricity and gas are standard-rated at 20%. A VAT-registered business can normally recover the VAT as input tax, subject to the normal rules.

Business energy VAT

Are solar panels zero-rated?

The installation of qualifying energy-saving materials, including solar panels, is zero-rated until 31 March 2027 when the conditions are met.

Energy-saving materials

Is VAT charged on a new build?

No. The first sale of a qualifying new dwelling is zero-rated when the conditions are met. Later repairs and improvements are standard-rated.

VAT on property

When does the 5% rate apply to property?

It applies to specific works: converting a non-dwelling into a dwelling, changing the number of dwellings, renovating a long-term empty home, and certain energy-saving installations.

Property rate boundaries

Calculation

How do I add VAT to a price?

Multiply the net price by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 20%, multiply by 1.20; at 5%, multiply by 1.05. For example, £250 × 1.20 = £300, so the VAT is £50.

How to add VAT

How do I remove VAT from a gross price?

Divide the gross price by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 20%, divide by 1.20: £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100, with £20 of VAT. At 5%, divide by 1.05: £105 ÷ 1.05 = £100.

How to remove VAT

Why is subtracting 20% from a gross price wrong?

Subtracting 20% from £120 gives £96, but the correct net is £100. VAT at 20% is one-sixth of the gross amount, not one-fifth. The VAT fraction is 20 ÷ 120 = 1/6.

The VAT fraction

What is the VAT fraction?

The VAT fraction extracts VAT from a gross amount. At 20% it is 1/6; at 5% it is 1/21. Multiply the gross amount by the fraction to get the VAT.

VAT fraction calculator

How do I round VAT?

Calculate VAT on the net amount and round to the nearest penny. Retailers must not round the VAT figure down, but may round each calculation up or down. Businesses issuing VAT invoices to other businesses may use the Notice 700 paragraph 17.5 concession and round the total VAT down to a whole penny.

Rounding rules

Should I round VAT per line or on the invoice total?

Pick one method per invoice and apply it to every line. On five lines of £1.12 net at 20%, per-line rounding gives £1.10 of VAT while rounding the £5.60 total gives £1.12. Neither is wrong; switching method part-way is the problem.

Per-line vs total rounding

How do I calculate VAT on a mixed invoice?

Group the lines by VAT rate, total the net amount for each rate, calculate VAT on each group, then add the groups together. Do not apply one rate to the whole invoice.

Mixed-rate invoices

Do I charge VAT before or after a discount?

After the discount. Apply the discount to the net price first, then add VAT to the discounted amount, because VAT is charged on the price the customer actually pays.

Discounts and VAT

Is VAT due on a deposit?

Usually yes for goods, because a deposit is a payment on account and creates a tax point when paid. A refundable damage deposit is not consideration for a supply and carries no VAT.

Deposits and tax points

Is VAT due when a gift card is sold?

Usually not. VAT is accounted for when the card is redeemed for goods or services, at the rate that applies to those goods or services.

Vouchers and gift cards

How do I handle a refund?

Issue a credit note, refund the customer, and reduce your output tax in the period the credit note is issued. Do not amend the original return retrospectively.

Credit notes

Can I change a submitted VAT return?

Not by editing the original return. Small errors are corrected on the next return, and larger errors are notified to HMRC separately. The correction window is normally four years.

Error correction

Registration and returns

When must I register for VAT?

You must register when taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or when you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days. The test is a rolling 12-month window, not a tax year.

Registration threshold

What is the deregistration threshold?

The deregistration threshold is £88,000, and the test looks forward. You can ask HMRC to cancel your registration if you expect taxable turnover in the next 12 months to be below £88,000. You cannot cancel retrospectively because turnover has already fallen.

Deregistration

Should I register voluntarily?

Voluntary registration can help if you sell mainly to VAT-registered businesses and want to reclaim input tax. It can hurt if you sell to consumers, because adding 20% makes your prices less attractive.

Voluntary registration

How do I file a VAT return?

You file through compatible Making Tax Digital software. The deadline is usually one calendar month and 7 days after the end of the accounting period, and payment must reach HMRC by the same date.

VAT returns and MTD

What is Making Tax Digital?

Making Tax Digital for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file returns using compatible software. It applies to all VAT-registered businesses, including voluntary registrations.

Making Tax Digital

What is a VAT group?

A VAT group treats two or more eligible companies under common control as a single taxable person, with one VAT number and one return. Intra-group supplies are generally disregarded.

VAT group registration

Do intra-group supplies carry VAT?

Generally no. Supplies between members of the same VAT group are disregarded, although there are exceptions for certain supplies involving persons outside the group.

Intra-group supplies

What is the Flat Rate Scheme?

The Flat Rate Scheme lets eligible small businesses pay VAT at a fixed percentage of VAT-inclusive turnover instead of accounting for VAT on every sale and purchase. Limited cost businesses use 16.5%.

Special schemes

What is the margin scheme?

The margin scheme lets second-hand goods dealers account for VAT on the margin between the buying and selling price rather than the full selling price. The invoice normally does not show VAT separately.

Margin scheme calculator

What is the domestic reverse charge?

For certain construction and CIS supplies, the domestic reverse charge moves the duty to account for VAT from the supplier to the customer. The supplier invoices without VAT and the customer accounts for the deemed VAT.

Domestic reverse charge

Reclaiming VAT

Can I reclaim VAT on business expenses?

Usually yes if you are VAT-registered, the expense is genuinely for business use, you hold a valid VAT invoice and the cost relates to taxable supplies. Some categories are blocked.

Reclaiming VAT on expenses

Which expenses are blocked for VAT?

Common blocked items include business entertainment of UK clients, most cars available for private use, and costs used only for exempt supplies. The rules are detailed, so check before claiming a large or unusual expense.

Blocked input tax

Can I reclaim VAT on a car?

Usually no, because most cars are assumed to be available for private use. There are exceptions for cars used exclusively for business, taxis, driving school cars and qualifying pool cars.

Cars and input tax

How much VAT can I reclaim on a leased car?

Usually 50% when the car has private use. The other 50% is blocked. If the car is used exclusively for business, the full VAT may be recoverable.

Car leasing

What is the fuel scale charge?

A fixed amount of output tax a business accounts for when it reclaims VAT on fuel used in a car available for private use. It is based on CO2 emissions, not actual fuel used.

Fuel scale charge

Can I avoid the fuel scale charge?

Yes, by not reclaiming VAT on fuel and keeping private and business fuel separate. That avoids the charge but requires records of business fuel.

The alternative

Can I reclaim VAT on entertaining clients?

No, VAT on entertaining UK clients is blocked. It is a real cost to the business, even when the expense is allowed for income tax.

Business entertainment

Can I reclaim VAT on a staff Christmas party?

Yes, generally. Staff entertainment is recoverable when it is for business purposes, because employees are excluded from the entertainment block.

Staff entertainment

Can I reclaim import VAT?

Yes, if you are VAT-registered, the goods are for business use, and you hold the import evidence. Postponed VAT accounting puts the import VAT and the recovery on the same return.

Imports and exports

Can I reclaim VAT on a laptop?

Yes, if it is bought for business use and you hold a valid VAT invoice. If you also use it privately, claim only the business proportion.

Freelancer expenses

Property and business types

Do landlords charge VAT on rent?

No on residential rent, which is exempt. Commercial rent is also exempt unless the landlord opts to tax the property, after which 20% VAT applies.

VAT for landlords

Can I reclaim VAT on repairs to a rented house?

Usually no. Residential letting is exempt, so VAT on repairs and other costs linked to it is generally blocked.

Residential letting

What does opting to tax mean?

It makes a commercial property taxable at 20%, so the landlord charges VAT on rent and can recover VAT on qualifying costs. The option usually lasts 20 years.

Option to tax

Do builders charge VAT on new builds?

No. The first sale of a qualifying new dwelling can be zero-rated when the conditions are met. General repairs and maintenance are standard-rated at 20%.

VAT for construction

Does the domestic reverse charge apply to all construction work?

No. It applies to specified construction and building services supplied between VAT-registered businesses where the customer is registered for CIS.

Reverse charge scope

Is a kitchen fit-out 5% or 20%?

Usually 20%. The 5% rate applies to specific conversions and renovations, not to general improvements or repairs.

Construction rates

Do freelancers need to register for VAT?

Only when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months, or is expected to exceed it in the next 30 days. Sole trader status does not exempt you.

VAT for freelancers

Do I charge VAT to an overseas business client?

Usually no for B2B services, because the place of supply is the customer's country. The customer accounts for VAT under the reverse charge.

Professional services

Can charities reclaim VAT?

Only on business activities. VAT linked to grants and donations is generally not recoverable, because that income is outside the scope of VAT.

VAT for charities

Does Amazon collect VAT for me?

Sometimes. UK marketplaces can account for VAT on sales by overseas sellers. Check your seller agreement and your records to see who is responsible.

VAT for e-commerce

International

Do I charge VAT on exports?

No. Exports of goods are zero-rated when you keep evidence that the goods left the UK. The sale still goes on your VAT return.

Exports

Is the £135 threshold a VAT exemption?

No. It is a value threshold that changes the import rules for low-value consignments. It is not a general exemption from VAT.

Low value imports

What is postponed VAT accounting?

A way for VAT-registered importers to declare import VAT on their VAT return instead of paying it at the border. It has been available since 1 January 2021.

Postponed VAT accounting

What is place of supply?

The rule that decides which country has the right to tax a sale. It determines whether UK VAT applies or the sale is taxed elsewhere.

Place of supply

Where is the place of supply for B2B services?

Usually the customer's country. The supplier does not charge UK VAT on a cross-border B2B supply, and the customer accounts for tax under the reverse charge.

B2B place of supply

Where is the place of supply for B2C services?

Usually the supplier's country. A UK business charges UK VAT to a consumer abroad, unless a special rule applies.

B2C place of supply

Do digital services follow the same rule?

No. Digital services are generally taxed where the consumer belongs, so the customer's location decides the VAT.

Digital services

Do I charge VAT on sales to the EU?

Not on goods exported from Great Britain to the EU, provided you keep export evidence. B2B services to EU businesses are usually outside the scope of UK VAT.

VAT on EU sales

What is OSS?

The One Stop Shop, an EU scheme that lets a business account for VAT on EU B2C sales through one EU member state instead of registering in each country.

OSS and IOSS

Does Northern Ireland have different VAT rates?

No. The rates are the same as the rest of the UK. The differences are in the rules for moving goods under the Windsor Framework.

The Windsor Framework

Compliance and penalties

Is there a penalty for filing a VAT return late?

Yes, but it is points-based. You get one point per late return, and a £200 penalty applies only when you reach the threshold: 4 points quarterly, 5 monthly or 2 annually.

Late filing penalties

How much is the late payment penalty for VAT?

For payments due on or after 1 April 2025, there is no penalty in the first 15 days. At day 15 the first penalty is 3% of the amount outstanding, with a further 3% at day 30. From day 31 a second penalty accrues at 10% a year. Earlier periods followed the previous 2%, 2% and 4% regime.

Late payment penalties

Do I pay interest as well as a penalty?

Yes. Late payment interest runs from the due date at the Bank of England base rate plus 4% for periods from 6 April 2025, and plus 2.5% before that. It is charged separately from any penalty.

Interest

Can I appeal a VAT penalty?

Yes. Most penalty letters offer a review, and you can appeal to the tax tribunal. You normally have 30 days from the date of the decision.

Appeals

What counts as a reasonable excuse?

Something unexpected and outside your control, such as a serious illness or a system failure, that you could not reasonably have avoided. Relying on an adviser is not usually enough.

Reasonable excuse

How long must I keep VAT records?

Normally six years from the end of the accounting period they relate to. Records for deliberate errors or disputed items may need to be kept longer.

Record keeping

Do I need to keep paper invoices?

No, provided you keep a clear digital copy and the digital record is complete. The link between the record and the source document should be traceable.

Digital records

Can HMRC visit my premises?

Yes. HMRC can check records and, in some cases, visit premises as part of a VAT inspection. You can ask for the scope and for time to produce documents.

VAT inspections

Can I correct a VAT error on my next return?

Yes, if the net error is £10,000 or less, or between £10,000 and £50,000 and no more than 1% of box 6 on the return in which you discover it. Larger errors must be notified separately.

Error correction limits

What is the £50,000 VAT error limit?

It is the absolute ceiling for correcting an error on your next return. Between £10,000 and £50,000 you can self-correct only if the error is no more than 1% of box 6 on the current return.

The notification route

Do exempt sales go in box 6?

Yes. Box 6 includes standard-rated, reduced-rate, zero-rated and exempt supplies, all excluding VAT. There is no separate exempt turnover box on the VAT return.

What goes in box 6

Nations and cities

Is VAT different in Scotland?

No. VAT is UK-wide, so the rates and registration threshold are the same as in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. LBTT and Scottish income tax are separate taxes.

VAT across the UK

Does LBTT replace VAT?

No. LBTT is a property transaction tax. VAT is separate and can still apply to fees or to an opted commercial property.

Property taxes and VAT

Is VAT different in Wales?

No. VAT is UK-wide, so the rates and registration threshold are the same as in England, Scotland and Northern Ireland. LTT and the Welsh rates of income tax are separate.

VAT across the UK

Does LTT replace VAT?

No. LTT is a property transaction tax. VAT is separate and can still apply to fees or to an opted commercial property.

Property taxes and VAT

What is an XI VAT number?

A VAT number beginning with XI identifies a Northern Ireland business for certain goods movements under the Windsor Framework. It sits alongside the UK VAT number.

VAT across the UK

Do I need a local address to register for VAT?

No. You register with HMRC based on your business activity, not on the city or nation you trade from.

VAT across the UK

Is VAT different in London?

No. London uses the UK-wide VAT rates and thresholds. The local differences are SDLT, business rates and the sector mix, which are separate from VAT.

Local detail and VAT

Do I pay both SDLT and VAT on a property?

Possibly. SDLT applies to the purchase, while VAT can apply to fees or to the property if it is opted to tax. They are separate taxes and are calculated on different bases.

Property taxes and VAT
