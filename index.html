The method

How to work out VAT in the UK

To calculate VAT in the UK, start with the amount and the rate. If you are not sure how to count VAT in the UK, the method is the same for a single price, an invoice or a spreadsheet.

  1. Pick the rate. Use 20% for the standard rate, 5% for the reduced rate, 0% for the zero rate, or enter a custom rate.
  2. To add VAT, multiply the net amount by the multiplier. At 20% the multiplier is 1.20, at 5% it is 1.05 and at 0% it is 1.00. The result is the gross total. The VAT element is the gross total minus the net amount.
  3. To remove VAT, divide the gross total by the same multiplier. The result is the net amount, or the price without VAT. The VAT element is the gross total minus the net amount. Do not subtract 20% from the gross total, because that gives a net figure that is too low.
  4. Round the VAT to the nearest penny, then add it to the net amount or subtract it from the gross total. The calculator does this step for you.
  5. Check the rate for your goods or services with HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) on GOV.UK. The calculator does the arithmetic and rounding, not the liability decision.

VAT fraction: 1/6 at 20% and 1/21 at 5%

The VAT fraction is a shortcut for removing VAT from a gross total. At 20%, 1/6 of the gross total is VAT, so £120 gross contains £20 of VAT. At 5%, 1/21 of the gross total is VAT, so £105 gross contains £5 of VAT.

Zero-rated is not VAT-exempt

Zero-rated supplies are taxable supplies at 0%. They still belong on the VAT return and usually allow input tax recovery on related costs. VAT-exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but are not taxable supplies, and input tax linked to them is generally blocked.

The calculator does the arithmetic and penny rounding. It does not decide which rate applies. Check the rate for your goods or services with HMRC on GOV.UK.

UK VAT rates

UK VAT rates at 20%, 5% and 0%

The UK has three VAT rates: standard 20%, reduced 5% and zero 0%. VAT-exempt supplies are a separate category, not a rate. The rate depends on the goods or services you sell.

UK VAT rates and typical examples
Rate Applies to Example
20% Standard Most goods and services £100 → £120
5% Reduced Domestic gas and other fuels, children’s car seats, some home improvements £100 → £105
0% Zero Most food, books, children’s clothing £100 → £100

Qualifying domestic electricity in Great Britain is temporarily zero-rated from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027. Northern Ireland electricity stays at 5%.

FAQs

Common VAT questions

How much is VAT in the UK?
The standard rate is 20%, the reduced rate is 5% and the zero rate is 0%. Most goods and services use the standard rate. Some supplies are VAT-exempt, which is not the same as zero-rated. Check the rate for your goods or services with HMRC on GOV.UK.
How do I add VAT to a price?
Multiply the net amount by 1.20 at 20%, 1.05 at 5% or 1.00 at 0%. The result is the gross total. The VAT element is the gross total minus the net amount. Round the VAT to the nearest penny before you add it. Read the full guide to adding VAT
How do you remove VAT from a price?
To take VAT off a gross price, divide the gross total by 1.20 at 20% or 1.05 at 5%. The result is the net amount, or the price without VAT. The VAT element is the gross total minus the net amount. Do not subtract 20% from the gross total, because that gives the wrong net figure. Read the full guide to removing VAT
What is the VAT fraction at 20% and 5%?
At 20% the VAT fraction is 1/6 of the gross total. At 5% it is 1/21. Multiply the gross total by the fraction to get the VAT element. For example, £120 gross at 20% contains £20 of VAT. Open the VAT fraction calculator

Across the UK

One VAT system, four nations

VAT is a UK-wide tax set by the UK government. The same rates and thresholds apply in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, but each nation has its own devolved taxes and some special rules.

England

Standard 20%, reduced 5%, zero 0%. Stamp Duty Land Tax applies to property purchases instead of a devolved equivalent.

Scotland

The same VAT rates. Land and Buildings Transaction Tax replaces SDLT, and Scottish Income Tax is separate from VAT.

Wales

The same VAT rates. Land Transaction Tax replaces SDLT, and the Welsh rates of Income Tax are devolved.

Northern Ireland

The same VAT rates, with special goods rules under the Windsor Framework and XI VAT numbers for some movements of goods.
