The formulas

How UK VAT is calculated at 20%, 5% and 0%

The UK VAT formula multiplies a net price by 1 plus the rate expressed as a decimal. At the 20% standard rate, net × 1.20 = gross, so £250 becomes £300 and the VAT element is £50. At the 5% reduced rate, net × 1.05 = gross, so £50 becomes £52.50. The 0% zero rate multiplies by 1.00, which leaves the price unchanged because zero-rated supplies are still taxable supplies.

Removing VAT uses division, not subtraction. Gross ÷ 1.20 = net at the standard rate, so £120 becomes £100 with £20 of VAT. Gross ÷ 1.05 = net at the reduced rate, so £105 becomes £100 with £5 of VAT. Subtracting 20% from a gross amount gives the wrong answer because VAT is charged on the net, not on the gross. The VAT fraction is the shortcut: 1/6 at 20% and 1/21 at 5%.

UK VAT rates with the formula to add and remove VAT
Rate Add VAT (net → gross) Remove VAT (gross → net) VAT fraction Typical supplies
20% standard net × 1.20 gross ÷ 1.20 1/6 Most goods and services
5% reduced net × 1.05 gross ÷ 1.05 1/21 Domestic energy, children’s car seats, some renovations
0% zero net × 1.00 gross ÷ 1.00 0 Most food, books, children’s clothing
Exempt no VAT added no VAT included — Finance, insurance, education, residential rent

VAT fraction check: 1/6 against 1/21

The VAT fraction extracts the tax from a gross amount without a division step. At 20%, every £120 of gross contains £20 of VAT, because 1/6 of £120 is £20. At 5%, every £105 of gross contains £5 of VAT, because 1/21 of £105 is £5.

Zero-rated is not exempt

HMRC treats zero-rated supplies as taxable at 0%, so a zero-rated sale belongs in box 6 of the VAT return and the business can usually reclaim input tax on related costs. Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but no VAT is charged on them, so input tax linked to exempt activity is generally blocked.

The statutory framework is the Value Added Tax Act 1994, with the detailed rules in HMRC VAT Notice 700.

FAQs

Common VAT questions

What is VAT?
Value Added Tax is a consumption tax charged on most goods and services supplied by VAT-registered businesses in the UK. The standard rate is 20%, the reduced rate is 5%, and some supplies are zero-rated at 0%. A business charges VAT on its sales (output tax), reclaims VAT on eligible purchases (input tax), and pays the difference to HMRC.
How do I add VAT to a price?
Multiply the net price by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 20% the factor is 1.20, so £250 becomes £300. At 5% the factor is 1.05, so £250 becomes £262.50. Round the VAT itself to the nearest penny before adding it to the net amount.
How do I remove VAT from a gross price?
Divide the gross price by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 20%, £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100, so the VAT element is £20. Subtracting 20% from £120 gives £96, which is too low, because VAT at 20% is one-sixth of the gross amount, not one-fifth.
What is the difference between zero-rated and exempt?
Zero-rated supplies are taxable at 0%, so they still appear on your VAT return and you can usually reclaim related input VAT. Exempt supplies are within the scope of VAT but charged no VAT, and they generally block input tax recovery. The distinction matters most for businesses that make a mixture of supplies.
When must I register for VAT?
You must register if your total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000, or if you expect it to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days. The test is a rolling 12-month window, not a tax year. The deregistration threshold is £88,000.
Can I reclaim VAT on business expenses?
Usually yes if you are VAT-registered, the purchase is genuinely for business use, you hold a valid VAT invoice, and the cost relates to taxable supplies. Some categories are blocked, including business entertainment of UK clients and most car purchases. Use the VAT on expenses calculator to check a figure.

Across the UK

One VAT system, four nations

VAT is a UK-wide tax set by the UK government. The same rates and thresholds apply in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, but each nation has its own devolved taxes and some special rules.

England

Standard 20%, reduced 5%, zero 0%. Stamp Duty Land Tax applies to property purchases instead of a devolved equivalent.

Scotland

The same VAT rates. Land and Buildings Transaction Tax replaces SDLT, and Scottish Income Tax is separate from VAT.

Wales

The same VAT rates. Land Transaction Tax replaces SDLT, and the Welsh rates of Income Tax are devolved.

Northern Ireland

The same VAT rates, with special goods rules under the Windsor Framework and XI VAT numbers for some movements of goods.
